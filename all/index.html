Everything on one page

In 1969 a soul band called The Winstons needed a B-side. They cut an instrumental, and about halfway through the drummer, Gregory Coleman, played four bars on his own.112111 Those few seconds have since been lifted into thousands of other records - WhoSampled listed 6,873 as of July 2025 - and they run through jungle and drum and bass.110 Coleman never saw a royalty for it.109

That is sampling in one story: a piece of an old recording put to work in a new one, a machine that made it possible, a genre built on the result, and a long argument about who should be paid. A flip is the trade's word for doing it well - taking a sound and turning it into something its makers never planned.

Here is how it works, start to finish: what counts as a sample and what does not, where the idea came from, the machines, the breaks everyone uses, how a flip gets made, what the courts have said in the US, the UK and Europe, how clearance works and what it costs, who gets paid, how it sounds around the world, and what AI is doing to all of it. The law sections describe what courts held and what people in the business say they do. They are not legal advice.

What sampling is, and what it is not

The short version: A sample is a piece of an existing recording placed inside a new one. Play the same part again with your own hands and it becomes an interpolation; re-record the whole song and it is a cover; rework the original recording and it is a remix. The distinctions sound fussy until money is involved, because each one calls on a different set of owners.

What is sampling in music?#

A sample is part of an existing recording placed in a new work.1 The word that matters is "recording". If the sound coming out of the speaker was captured at somebody else's session, with somebody else's drummer in somebody else's room, it is a sample, whether it lasts one snare hit or four bars.

The name arrived with the machine. Peter Vogel and Kim Ryrie, the two Australians behind the Fairlight CMI of 1979, are credited with calling the process "sampling".2 The practice is older than the name. Pierre Schaeffer was building pieces out of recordings of trains in Paris in 1948,3 and Kool Herc was stretching drum breaks across two copies of the same record in the Bronx in 1973.4 The long version is at /history/.

It is also common. Tracklib, a company that sells sample licenses and so has an interest in the answer, counted samples or interpolations in 17% of the songs on the Billboard Hot 100 in 2022.5

What is the difference between a sample and an interpolation?#

The difference is who is playing. An interpolation takes part of an existing song and records it again from scratch, with none of the original audio.1 Studios call the result a replay.

Two of the records most often filed under "early sampling" are mostly replays. No Kraftwerk recording is on "Planet Rock" (1982): the melody and the beat were copied by hand.6 The groove on the Sugarhill Gang's "Rapper's Delight" (1979) is a studio band playing Chic's "Good Times", though Nile Rodgers of Chic has said the strings came straight off his record.78

The distinction has a price tag. A sample uses the recording and the song inside it. A replay uses only the song, and the US Copyright Office says an interpolation needs a license from the song's owner and no permission from the recording's owner, "regardless of how similar the new recording may be to an old recording".1 That is one permission fewer, which is not the same as none. Tracklib's version: "an interpolation is not a cover version so interpolations do need clearance."9

Is a remix the same as a sample?#

No, though they are cousins. A cover is a new recording of an existing song that keeps its basic melody and character; in the US it travels on a statutory license at a set rate, and that license grants nothing in anybody's recording.1 A remix is an existing recording with sounds added, changed or taken away, so the owner of that recording is part of the conversation.1 A mashup combines two or more sampled recordings into one work.1

Jamaica had the remix first. By 1970 nearly every single pressed there carried a "version" on the B-side: the same recording with the vocal pulled out, the mix that grew into dub.10 The best-known mashup is Danger Mouse's "The Grey Album" (2004), Jay-Z's a cappellas laid over pieces of the Beatles' "White Album".11

What do loop, chop and flip mean?#

  • A loop is a section of a record set to repeat. On early samplers, with a few seconds of memory, the audible repeat became part of the sound.12
  • A chop is a recording cut into pieces and played back in a new order. The standard example is Marley Marl lifting the kick, snare and hi-hat out of "Impeach the President" for MC Shan's "The Bridge" (1986).13
  • A flip is a sample changed enough to do a job its makers never planned. Four bars looped is a sample; the same four bars cut up, slowed, filtered and laid under different drums is a flip.

The moves themselves are at /making-a-flip/.

Do you need permission to sample a song?#

Every recorded song is two separate works, the composition and the sound recording, and a sample copies both, so it usually involves two owners: whoever holds the recording (the master) and whoever holds the song (the publishing).1415 The popular belief in a safe number of seconds has no law behind it - "There is no time limit or maximum length to avoid clearance issues," as Tracklib puts it9 - and the courts that have ruled are at /law/, the paperwork and the prices at /clearing/.

Why do producers sample instead of playing it themselves?#

Because a recording holds things a new performance cannot: one room, one drummer's hands on one afternoon, the wear on the copy it came from. Marley Marl has said he found this out by accident in the early 1980s, when a snare he caught by mistake sounded better than the one in his drum machine.161718 DJ Muggs of Cypress Hill describes what followed: "Once Marley Marl started sampling, it really flipped the whole rap game on its head and created this new artform."19

The artistic case has been made in writing since 1985, when the Canadian composer John Oswald argued that recordings should be quotable the way books are: "Without a quotation system, well-intended correspondences cannot be distinguished from plagiarism and fraud."20 Producers add a practical point, that the limits of the machines did some of the composing. Ski, on the 10 seconds of memory in an SP-1200: "The limited sampling time made you become more creative."21

The case against is as old. The first US court to rule on a sample, in Grand Upright Music v. Warner Bros. Records (1991), opened its opinion with "Thou shalt not steal."22 Pete Waterman, whose production team obtained an injunction in 1987 over seven seconds of their record inside "Pump Up the Volume", called the practice "wholesale theft".23 Record companies tend to put it more coolly. When EMI moved against "The Grey Album" in 2004, its spokeswoman said only that "there is a well-established market for licensing samples".11

A third complaint comes from the people on the old records. Gregory Coleman, who drummed the Amen break on the Winstons' "Amen, Brother" (1969), died in 2006 without receiving royalties for it.2425 Who gets paid, and who never did, is the subject of /money/.

Where sampling came from

The short version: Nobody invented sampling in one go. Composers were cutting up recordings in Paris in 1948, Jamaican engineers were remixing them by 1970, and Bronx DJs were looping drum breaks by hand in 1973, all before a machine existed to do it. The digital sampler arrived in 1979, got cheap by 1987, and produced about four years of dense collage that ended in lawyers' offices. What came after was slower, costlier and spread across the world.

Who invented sampling?#

It depends which part you mean. The idea of composing with recorded sound is usually traced to Pierre Schaeffer, who made his "Cinq études de bruits" at French radio's Studio d'Essai in Paris in 1948. The first of the five, "Étude aux chemins de fer", is assembled from recordings of trains, and the set went out on air on October 5 that year as a "Concert de bruits", a concert of noises.3

The idea of a keyboard that plays recordings belongs to Harry Chamberlin, a Californian who built his by hand: a strip of tape and a playback head under every key, about eight seconds a note.31 The idea of looping the best four bars of somebody else's record belongs to DJ Kool Herc. And the word itself is credited to Peter Vogel and Kim Ryrie of Sydney, who needed something to call what their Fairlight did.2

What came before the sampler?#

Tape. Buchanan and Goodman's "The Flying Saucer" (1956) was a spoken skit with clips of current hits cut in for punchlines. James Tenney's "Collage No. 1 (Blue Suede)" (1961) was built from Elvis Presley's recording of the song.32

Chamberlin's keyboards turned the trick into an instrument, though nobody agrees when. One history has the invention in 1946 and the Model 200 in 1951; another says the late 1940s; Canada's National Music Centre says the 1950s.313334

The famous version is a copy. In 1962 Chamberlin's salesman, Bill Fransen, took two of the keyboards to England and ordered 70 matched tape heads from a Birmingham firm run by the Bradley brothers. A year later the Bradleys were selling the Mellotron Mark I.34 Chamberlin found out in 1966 and sold them the technology for about $30,000 plus royalties.34 Its best-known outings both date from 1967: the Beatles' "Strawberry Fields Forever" and the Moody Blues' "Nights in White Satin".3433

In London the BBC gave the tape cutters a room of their own. The Radiophonic Workshop was formally created on April 1, 1958, at the Maida Vale studios, after studio producer Daphne Oram and her colleague Desmond Briscoe had lobbied for it, and its staff made sound for radio and television by splicing tape and running loops several meters long.35 Delia Derbyshire moved to the Workshop in 1962 and the next year turned Ron Grainer's written theme for "Doctor Who" into electronic sound.35

What did Jamaica have to do with it?#

Jamaica worked out that a finished record is raw material. The story, which rests on his own account, is that the Spanish Town sound system operator Rudolph "Ruddy" Redwood went to Duke Reid's Treasure Isle studio in Kingston to have an acetate cut, and engineer Byron Smith left the vocal off the Paragons' "On the Beach". Redwood played it at his dance anyway.10 The year is given as 1967 or 1968, depending on whose book you read.1029

By 1970 nearly every Jamaican single carried a version on its B-side.10 The stripped drum-and-bass track underneath was a riddim, and producers reused riddims and sold them to each other.29 Dub took the version further: vocal out, bass and drums forward, echo and reverb over everything.29 Its best-known engineer was King Tubby (Osbourne Ruddock), an electrical engineer in the Waterhouse district of Kingston who bought a retired four-track desk from Dynamic Studios, customized it and played it like an instrument.1029 Four different 1973 LPs have been called the first dub album.10

One Kingston kid who grew up around those sound systems and their talking deejays was Clive Campbell. He moved to the Bronx in November 1967, aged 12.364

What happened at 1520 Sedgwick Avenue?#

On August 11, 1973, Cindy Campbell threw a back-to-school party in the community room of her building at 1520 Sedgwick Avenue in the Bronx. Girls paid 25 cents and boys 50, and her 18-year-old brother Clive, by then DJ Kool Herc, played the records.4

Herc had noticed that dancers waited for the break, the stretch where the band drops out and the drummer carries on. So he skipped the rest. With two turntables he jumped from one record's break to the next, and with two copies of the same record he could keep a single break going as long as he liked.437 He called it the Merry-Go-Round; the earliest known one ran James Brown's "Give It Up or Turnit a Loose" into the Incredible Bongo Band's "Bongo Rock" into Babe Ruth's "The Mexican".36 It is a sampler loop performed by hand.

Whether that night counts as the birth of hip-hop is argued by the people who know most about it. Jeff Chang has called the party "a creation myth", and the historian Jay Quan says the same ideas were developing at other parties around the Bronx.4 By 1975 Grandmaster Flash was DJing in Herc's style and Afrika Bambaataa had a sound system of his own.36

Flash made a method of it. He marked the best part of each record with a crayon or grease pencil, worked out how to cue it, rebuilt his mixer, and by his own telling could stretch a 10-second drum break to 10 minutes.38 In 1981 he put it on a record: "The Adventures of Grandmaster Flash on the Wheels of Steel" runs seven minutes, cuts between Chic's "Good Times", Queen's "Another One Bites the Dust" and Blondie's "Rapture", and was recorded live on the turntables.39

Did "Rapper's Delight" sample "Good Times"?#

Mostly no. The Sugarhill Gang's single, released in September 1979, runs on a studio band playing Chic's bassline and groove.7 Nile Rodgers first heard it at Leviticus, a New York club, and took the DJ to be rapping over a Chic record: "I had no idea that he was actually playing a record."8 By his account the bass and guitar had been replayed and the string line came off his record, cued in by hand.8 The usual account of the aftermath is that he and Chic's Bernard Edwards threatened a copyright action, the matter was settled, and both were added as co-writers.7 Rodgers, asked in 1996 whether they sued, was blunter: "Sure, we sued."8

Did "Planet Rock" sample Kraftwerk?#

Also no, though Arthur Baker, who produced it, says a Kraftwerk member's book claimed otherwise. Afrika Bambaataa & the Soulsonic Force's "Planet Rock" came out on Tommy Boy in June 1982. Baker had keyboardist John Robie play the "Trans-Europe Express" melody by hand and paid a programmer to copy the "Numbers" beat on a Roland TR-808.640 The studio's Fairlight contributed only stock sounds: an explosion, handclaps and an orchestra stab.6

The payment is known only from Baker's side. He says Kraftwerk got $1 for every record sold and that Tommy Boy raised the 12-inch list price from $4.98 to $5.98 to cover it. "We borrowed the melody, which we ended up paying more for than if we had sampled it now," he said in 2013.40

What was the first song to use a sample?#

It depends on what counts. "The Flying Saucer" had other people's records spliced into it in 1956.32 The first hit record with a Fairlight on it is usually given as Kate Bush's "Babooshka" (1980), where the breaking glass is a sample.41

When did samplers reach pop music?#

The Fairlight CMI was unveiled in 1979 at around $25,000, an 8-bit machine that could hold about a second of sound.4142 Peter Gabriel set up a company to import it to Britain; John Paul Jones was the first British customer, then Kate Bush and Trevor Horn.434142 Its stock orchestra stab, lifted from a recording of Stravinsky's "Firebird", is the one on "Planet Rock".416

Cheaper machines followed, and /machines/ takes them one by one. E-mu's Emulator arrived in 1981, and New Order used one to sample Kraftwerk's "Uranium" for "Blue Monday" (1983).43 The E-mu SP-1200 (1987) gave hip-hop producers about 10 seconds of gritty 12-bit memory, and the Akai MPC60 (1988) gave them 16 pads and swing.2118

The discovery that mattered most to hip-hop was an accident. Marley Marl, an intern at Unique Recording Studios in the early 1980s, was trying to lift a riff off a record during a Captain Rock session when he caught a snare hit by mistake, and it sounded better than his drum machine's.1617 "Thank God the beat was playing, because it probably wouldn't have happened if the beat wasn't playing," he told NPR in 2013.1617 The year and the machine have never been pinned down; one account puts it a couple of years before 1986.18

What were the collage years?#

Roughly 1987 to 1991, when the machines were affordable and the bills had not yet arrived. Hank Shocklee of the Bomb Squad built Public Enemy's records on an SP-1200 with about 12 seconds of sample time shared between eight pads, by his count, and got around the limit by sampling records at the wrong speed: "You take a 33 1/3 record and play it on 45, and you cheat the system."21

Nobody kept a tally for "It Takes a Nation of Millions to Hold Us Back" (1988) or "Fear of a Black Planet" (1990). Kembrew McLeod and Peter DiCola's book "Creative License" estimates 100 to 200 samples on each of the early Public Enemy albums, and Chuck D told them the opening track of the second holds "about forty-five to fifty voices".44 Shocklee describes the first as built like a band, each one- or two-bar loop standing in for an instrument, with "Bring the Noise" pushed to about 109 or 110 beats per minute when most rap records sat near 107.45

In 1989 came the two albums the era is remembered by. De La Soul's "3 Feet High and Rising", produced by Prince Paul, included a skit called "Transmitting Live from Mars" that ran over 12 seconds of the Turtles' "You Showed Me".26 The Beastie Boys' "Paul's Boutique", made with the Dust Brothers and released on July 25, is usually said to sample 105 records - DJ Mag prints the figure flat, and Wikipedia's fan-sourced tally puts 24 of them on the last track.4647 Mike Simpson of the Dust Brothers has put it at anything from 100 to 300 samples, and two IP lawyers discussing the album in 2025 said they had seen estimates from 150 to 300.4849 Engineer Mario Caldato Jr. remembers more than $250,000 going on clearances.50

That November the Canadian composer John Oswald took the idea to its limit with "Plunderphonic", a 25-track CD of reworked recordings, Michael Jackson's "Bad" among them, with every source credited and no copies for sale. His 1993 follow-up, "Plexure", draws on more than 1,000 tracks by one count and 5,000 tape samples by another.3220 A letter from lawyers for the Canadian Recording Industry Association reached him on Christmas Eve. He settled and handed over his remaining copies to be destroyed; about 700 were already out.20

How did the collage years end?#

In court, or on the way there. Two former members of the Turtles sued De La Soul and Tommy Boy Records in 1989; SPIN reported that November that they were asking for $1.7 million in punitive damages.51 The case settled out of court. A UK law firm's later account dates the suit to 1991, the claim at $2.5 million and the settlement at up to $1.7 million, and neither the year nor the amount of the settlement has been confirmed.26 In 1991 a New York federal court granted an injunction over Biz Markie's "Alone Again" in Grand Upright Music v. Warner Bros. Records, after which labels began requiring every sample to be cleared.22

The usual account says records as dense as "Paul's Boutique" stopped being affordable at that point.4746 Prices did climb: RZA recalls paying $1,500 to $2,500 a sample in his early years, $2,000 for a Gladys Knight sample that he reckoned in 2016 would cost $10,000.52 Sample-built albums kept arriving all the same, DJ Shadow's in 1996 and the Avalanches' in 2000, the second held up for months by clearances.5354 The cases themselves are at /law/.

Was "Endtroducing....." the first album made entirely from samples?#

Guinness World Records says so, crediting DJ Shadow's 1996 album as "the first album to be recorded using only sampled sounds" in an entry that misspells the title.53 He made much of it on an Akai MPC60.18 Oswald's "Plunderphonic", built from other people's recordings, is seven years older, though it was never on sale.20 Guinness gives no method for the award.

What happened when Britain got hold of the sampler?#

It had a hit and an injunction inside the same season. M/A/R/R/S, a collaboration between the bands Colourbox and A.R. Kane with two DJs, released "Pump Up the Volume" in August 1987. With the single at number two in the UK, producers Stock Aitken Waterman obtained an injunction over seven seconds of their hit "Roadblock", and distribution stopped for five days while the two sides made a deal.5523 Editions released abroad dropped the sample.55

The Justified Ancients of Mu Mu, later the KLF, were less lucky. Their album "1987 (What the Fuck Is Going On?)", out that June, used long unlicensed stretches of ABBA's "Dancing Queen" among others. After ABBA complained, the Mechanical-Copyright Protection Society ordered the unsold copies destroyed. Bill Drummond and Jimmy Cauty drove to Sweden hoping to meet ABBA, failed, threw some of the LPs off the ferry and burned the rest in a field near Gothenburg. They then reissued the record with the samples cut out and the silences left in.56 One reference account counts 500 copies burned, at 3 a.m.57

What is the Amen break?#

Four bars of drums played by Gregory Coleman on the Winstons' "Amen, Brother", a 1969 B-side. It lasts about six seconds, 5.2 of them clean drums by UKF's measure.5859 It reached DJs through the "Ultimate Breaks and Beats" compilations of 1986 to 1991, turned up in hip-hop on Salt-N-Pepa's "I Desire" (1986) and N.W.A's "Straight Outta Compton" (1988), and then crossed the Atlantic, where British producers sped it up for raves.592760 Carl Cox's "I Want You (Forever)" and Lennie De Ice's "We Are I.E.", both 1991, are early examples, and jungle and drum and bass were built on it afterward. Mixmag put the tally at "over 2500 tracks" in 2018.27 The rest of the canon is at /breaks/.

Where did Brazilian funk get its beat?#

From the B-side of a Los Angeles electro 12-inch. Funk carioca grew up in Rio's dance parties in the 1980s on imported Miami bass and freestyle records, and DJ Marlboro has named "Planet Rock" as the main influence.61 The beat under most 1990s funk is the "Volt Mix": the "808 Beatapella Mix" on the flip of "8 Volt Mix" by DJ Battery Brain, released on Techno Hop Records in 1988 and carried into the Rio circuit by the importer Carlos Machado, known as DJ Nazz.62 A home-grown rhythm, the tamborzão, replaced it in the 2000s.62 Scenes like this one are followed at /world/.

What does Daft Punk's "One More Time" sample?#

Horns from "More Spell on You" by Eddie Johns, a record dated 1978 in one account and 1979 in another.6330 The liner notes of "Discovery" (2001) credit four samples. His is not among them.30

In May 2021 the Los Angeles Times found Johns, then 70 and recently homeless in Los Angeles, and reported that he had never been paid. A Daft Punk representative said royalties go to the French company that holds the rights, GM Musipro, whose founder said it had bought the catalog in 1995 and would look into Johns's file.63

How many samples are on "Since I Left You"?#

Robbie Chater of the Avalanches gave Sound On Sound a conservative estimate of more than 3,500 in 2002, reasoning from two Akai S2000 programs of 100 keygroups each on most of the 18 songs.64 The magazine printed the same figure again in 2016.65 Other counts stop near 900.54 The Australian group built the album largely on a Yamaha Promix 01 mixer and Akai S2000 samplers.64 It came out in Australia in November 2000 after a delay for clearances, and the editions released overseas the next year lost or swapped several samples.54 The Madonna "Holiday" bassline on it began as a joke on a tape of finds. "We just got so used to it on there we thought we may as well try to clear it," Chater said.52

What was chipmunk soul?#

Rap built on soul vocals sampled and sped up until the singer squeaks, at its peak in the early to mid-2000s. The name is said to be Questlove's.28 Rsonist of the Heatmakerz, who used it on the Diplomats' "Dipset Anthem" (2003), credits RZA with starting it around 1996 and dates the craze from about 1999 to late 2005. Just Blaze and Kanye West made it the sound of Jay-Z's "The Blueprint".28

Who was Nujabes?#

Jun Seba, a Tokyo producer who opened a record shop in Shibuya in 1995 and founded the label Hydeout Productions three years later. As Nujabes he released "Metaphorical Music" in 2003 and "Modal Soul" in 2005, and scored much of the anime "Samurai Champloo" (2004). He died after a traffic collision in Tokyo on February 26, 2010, aged 36, and is now often called a godfather of lo-fi hip-hop.6667

Was "Donuts" really made in a hospital bed?#

Partly, at most. J Dilla's "Donuts" came out on Stones Throw on February 7, 2006, his 32nd birthday: 31 tracks in a little over 43 minutes. He died three days later.68 The story that grew around it has him making the record in the hospital with a Boss SP-303 and a box of 45s. Dan Charnas's 2022 biography "Dilla Time" reports that most of it was made earlier, largely in Pro Tools, and that Stones Throw's art director Jeff Jank stretched a 27-track beat tape of under 30 minutes into the album, with Dilla's approval.6968

What was Grey Tuesday?#

A one-day online protest on February 24, 2004. Danger Mouse had released "The Grey Album" that month in a small run, and EMI and Capitol told him and the shops carrying it to stop.1170 The activist group Downhill Battle answered by organizing about 170 websites to host the album for a day, with several hundred more turning their pages gray. By its own estimate the album was downloaded more than 100,000 times. Capitol's lawyers sent letters.11

Where did vaporwave come from?#

From YouTube. Starting in 2009 Daniel Lopatin, who records as Oneohtrix Point Never, posted slowed and looped fragments of pop songs under the name sunsetcorp, then collected them in 2010 as "Chuck Person's Eccojams Vol. 1".7172 James Ferraro's "Far Side Virtual" and Macintosh Plus's "Floral Shoppe" followed in 2011.7173

The method was 1980s and 1990s ballads, ads and hold music, slowed and chopped, then given away or sold cheaply on Bandcamp and SoundCloud, built on samples one essay called "unclearable".7372 Lopatin's own description was "it's kinda folksy by nature".73 The story from there runs through stem splitters and music generators, at /ai/.

The machines

The short version: A sampler is a digital recorder you can play like an instrument: it measures a sound thousands of times a second, stores the numbers and reads them back at whatever pitch you ask for.74 The first commercial one, the Fairlight CMI of 1979, was an 8-bit computer with a light pen and a five-figure price in pounds.7576 Eight years later a drum box with 10 seconds of 12-bit memory was setting the sound of hip-hop, and its flaws were kept on purpose when its designer reissued it in 2021.77

How does a sampler work?#

A record or a microphone hands the sampler a smoothly moving signal. The sampler measures it at regular ticks and writes each measurement down as a number. Read the numbers back at the same speed and the sound returns; read them faster or slower and it comes back higher or lower, the way a turntable does when you change its speed. Two settings decide how good the copy is.

Sample rate is how many measurements are taken each second, and it sets the highest pitch the machine can capture.74 The Nyquist rule says the top frequency is half the sample rate, so 44.1 kHz, the rate the CD made standard, reaches 22.05 kHz.74 Sound above that limit does not vanish. It folds back as a false lower tone, an effect called aliasing: a 30,000 Hz harmonic sampled at 44.1 kHz reappears at 14,100 Hz.78 A low-pass filter in front of the converter is there to stop it.74

Bit depth is how many loudness steps each measurement can land on: 2 to the power of the bit depth, with about 6 dB of dynamic range for every bit.74 Each measurement gets rounded to the nearest step, and the rounding error is heard as quantization noise.74

Bit depth Loudness steps Dynamic range Where you meet it
8-bit 256 about 48 dB Fairlight CMI Series I, Casio SK-1
12-bit 4,096 about 72 dB E-mu SP-1200, Akai S900, Akai MPC60
16-bit 65,536 about 96 dB Akai S1000, EP-133 K.O. II
24-bit 16,777,216 about 144 dB Akai MPC4000

The 16-bit and 24-bit figures are the published ones; the 8-bit and 12-bit rows are the same formula run on smaller numbers.7478

The third limit was memory. Every second of sound costs the sample rate multiplied by the bit depth, and in the 1980s that bill was steep. An Akai S900 could spread its 750 KB over 63 seconds by dropping to 7.5 kHz, or spend it all on a little under 12 seconds at top quality.7980 Every early sampler owner made some version of that trade, usually several times a session.

What was the first sampler?#

The tape-era ancestors - musique concrète, the Chamberlin and the Mellotron - are at /history/.

The Fairlight CMI came out of Sydney, where Peter Vogel and Kim Ryrie built it on the bones of Tony Furse's Qasar M8, a 1975 design.75 It was demonstrated in 1979, ran on two 8-bit 6800 processors, and let you poke at its screen with a light pen. Museums Victoria, which holds one, calls it the first commercially available digital sampling instrument.76 The Series I sampled at 8-bit and 24 kHz across eight voices. The Series II of 1982 raised the rate to 32 kHz and added Page R, a pattern sequencer. The Series III of 1985 went to 16-bit, 50 kHz stereo and 16 voices.75

What one cost depends on who is telling it. One history gives £18,000 for the first model, £27,000 for the Series II and £60,000 for the Series III.75 Wikipedia has £12,000 as the UK price, US$27,500 for each of the units sold in the US in 1980, and £30,000 for the Series II.42 Reverb says £13,000.81 The first customers are just as slippery: the museum record has Peter Gabriel and Stevie Wonder buying the first units in 1979, while Wikipedia has Wonder and Herbie Hancock buying in 1980 and John Paul Jones as the first UK customer.7642

E-mu's Emulator followed in 1981 and sold about 500 units before it was dropped in early 1984.8283 Its price is given as $8,000 in one account and "£8050" in another, which reads like the same number wearing a different currency sign.8182 The first unit had been promised to Daryl Dragon until Stevie Wonder placed an order.82 The Emulator II arrived in 1984, at a reported $7,995.8483

When did samplers get cheap?#

In 1984 the Ensoniq Mirage went on sale at $1,695, when an Emulator ran about $8,000.81 Ensoniq had been founded by three former Commodore engineers, Bruce Crockett, Al Charpentier and Bob Yannes.81 Cheap came with conditions: Hank Shocklee, who built the first version of Public Enemy's "Rebel Without a Pause" on a Mirage, remembered having about three seconds of sample time.81

The floor dropped further with the Casio SK-1, a toy-sized keyboard that sold for under $100. It had a built-in microphone, four voices, 8-bit sound and 1.4 seconds of sampling, enough for one word or one dog bark. It later became a standard choice for circuit bending.85

Why does the SP-1200 sound like that?#

Because it is a 12-bit machine sampling at 26.04 kHz, and because of what happens when it changes pitch. Dave Rossum designed it.77 Half of 26.04 kHz is 13.02 kHz, so the top of the record is gone before the drums ever reach a pad. Re-tuning a sound on the SP-1200 adds what Rossum's company calls "aliasing and imaging artifacts", and the company kept them on purpose in its reissue.77 The Village Voice called the result "gritty 12-bit sound quality" and credited the machine for crunchy drums, choppy samples and murky filtered basslines.21 The producer Ski put it in two words: "so dirty."21

The E-mu SP-12 came first, in 1985, and the SP-1200 replaced it in 1987.77 The US launch prices in circulation, $2,745 and $2,995, come from Wikipedia alone; Sound On Sound's October 1987 review listed the SP-1200 in Britain at £2,199 including VAT.8687 The SP-1200 held 10 seconds of sound in total, and no single sound could run longer than 2.5 seconds, because of what RAM cost in 1987.7721 The Village Voice, citing the manufacturer, makes it 10.07 seconds; Hank Shocklee, from memory, said 12.21 Its eight outputs are not alike either: channels 1 and 2 pass through dynamic SSM2044 filters, 3 to 6 have fixed filters, and 7 and 8 have none.77

Ten seconds is not much, so producers cheated. Shocklee again: "You take a 33 1/3 record and play it on 45, and you cheat the system."21 The record goes in fast and high, and the pad is tuned back down afterward, one of the moves at /making-a-flip/. The arithmetic is friendly. 45 divided by 33 1/3 is 1.35, so 10 seconds of memory swallows about 13.5 seconds of record. Tuning the sound back down also lowers its effective sample rate, which is one more layer of dirt on a machine already known for it.

Rossum Electro-Music sold a reissue from 2021 to 2025 at $3,999 with 20 seconds of memory, and closed the line with a 40th Anniversary Edition of 100 units at $4,999.7786

What did the Akai S900 and S950 add?#

Time. The Akai S900 of 1986 was a 12-bit sampler with eight voices, room for 32 samples and a sample rate you could set anywhere from 7.5 to 40 kHz.79 The S950 that followed in 1988 pushed the top rate to 48 kHz and could be expanded from 750 KB to 2,250 KB; a British review in March 1989 priced it at £1,399 including VAT and measured 9.89 seconds at full quality on the base unit.7988

The S950 also brought time-stretch, which changes how long a sound lasts without changing its pitch. It could stretch from 1% to 999% and might take up to 91 seconds to think about it.88 One retailer history calls it the first sampler of its kind with the feature.80 The S1000 then moved the series to 16-bit stereo at 44.1 kHz, the sample rate of the CD, with 2 MB of memory expandable to 8 MB.80

Who designed the MPC?#

Two people. Roger Linn did the functional design and the interface of the Akai MPC60, and David Cockerell built the sound engine.89 It came out in 1988 at a reported $5,000: 12-bit, 40 kHz, 16 pads, a 99-track sequencer, and 13.1 seconds of sampling that an upgrade doubled to 26.2.8990 Its voice count is given as both 16 and 18.90

The line that followed is long. The MPC60II came in 1991 and the MPC3000 in 1994, with 32 voices, a filter and effects, and Linn still involved.89 The MPC2000 of 1997 was 16-bit and designed without him; the MPC2000XL followed in 1999 and the 24-bit MPC4000 in 2002.899192

One MPC is now a museum object. J Dilla's MPC3000 Limited Edition, one of about 2,000 made in 2000, was given to the Smithsonian's National Museum of African American History and Culture by his mother, Maureen Yancey, in a donation announced in July 2014.93

What is MPC swing?#

A delay on every other 16th note, and nothing more mysterious than that. Linn's own description: "I merely delay the second 16th note within each 8th note."94 The setting is a percentage. At 50% the two 16th notes are even and there is no swing; at 66% the pair becomes a triplet.94 The useful territory is in between. Linn says 54% loosens a straight beat without making it sound swung, and that 62% at 90 BPM feels looser than a full 66%.94

He ranks swing first among six reasons for the MPC's feel and is unsentimental about the rest: "Not much magic," he told Attack Magazine in 2013.94 He also says he introduced recording quantization, the snapping of played notes to a timing grid, on his LM-1 drum machine in 1979.94 The Alchemist, a working user, says the dial runs from 50 to 75 and that he likes 62 or 63.89

What is the ASR-10?#

Ensoniq's sampler of 1992, and the answer to a lot of "what did they make that on" questions. It descended from the 13-bit EPS of 1988 and the 16-bit EPS 16+ of 1990.81 Its users include RZA, Kanye West, Timbaland, The Neptunes, Prince Paul and The Alchemist.81 RZA's summary: "The whole first 100 Wu-Tang songs was made on an ASR-10."81

Why do beatmakers love the SP-303 and SP-404?#

Madlib made three tracks on Madvillainy in a hotel room in Brazil with a Boss SP-303, a portable turntable and a cassette deck.95 That is the pitch for the whole SP line: a sampler small enough to pack.

It began with the Boss SP-202 Dr. Sample in 1998, with eight pads and 4 minutes 20 seconds of sampling.96 The SP-303 of 2001 added a tape echo and Vinyl Sim, an effect that imitates a worn record; Roland's own history credits it on Madvillainy and on J Dilla's Donuts, and names Four Tet and Animal Collective as users.96 The SP-505 followed in 2002 and the SP-606 in 2004.97

The Roland SP-404 of 2005 had 12 pads, 29 effects and a CompactFlash slot, and became the instrument of a beat scene that includes Flying Lotus, Ras G and Dibia$e.9695 The SP-555 came in 2008 and the SD-card SP-404SX in 2009.97 The SP-404A is dated 2014 in one timeline and 2017 in Roland's own article.9796 The SP-404MKII of 2021 has an OLED screen, 16 GB of storage and Skip Back Sampling.96

What sampler should a beginner know about?#

There are more answers than there used to be, and none of them costs what a Fairlight did.

The Teenage Engineering EP-133 K.O. II launched in November 2023 at $299 (€349, £299) with 12 pads, 999 sample slots, 64 MB of memory and 16-bit sound at about 46 kHz.9899 In January 2026 a 128 MB version at $329 became the standard model and the 64 MB one was discontinued.100

The Akai MPC One, from 2020, is a standalone MPC with a 7-inch touchscreen and no battery, reviewed at $699 / £639.101 It belongs to a generation that began in 2017 with the MPC Live and MPC X, which run the MPC software on the device itself; the MPC Live III of 2025 can pull a finished mix apart into stems on board, a trick covered at /ai/.9189 The SP-404MKII is the current member of the SP line described above.96

Native Instruments' Maschine started in 2009 as a pad controller that needed a computer. The Maschine+ runs on its own and was reviewed in January 2021 at $1,399 / £1,099.102

On a computer, Ableton Live has been stretching audio to the song's tempo since its release on October 30, 2001.103 Early versions wanted warp markers placed by hand; Auto-Warp arrived in Live 5.103 Co-founder Gerhard Behles wrote the first stretch algorithm himself and is candid about it: "It was worse than any of the algorithms we use today."104 For Live 5, Ableton licensed zplane's élastique, which became the "Complex" mode.104 Serato Sample, a plug-in announced as a public beta on April 27, 2017, finds the key, sets cue points and chops, stretches and re-pitches inside whatever software hosts it, using the Pitch 'n Time engine Serato first released in 1998.105106

Pad 04

The breaks everyone uses

The short version: The same few dozen records turn up under other people's songs again and again, and the most used of them is about six seconds of drumming from a 1969 B-side.25109 WhoSampled, a volunteer-built database, listed "Amen, Brother" 6,873 times as of July 2025.110 The drummer who played it died in 2006 without a royalty, and most of the players behind the other famous breaks tell a similar story.109

What is a break?#

A break is the stretch of a record where the band drops out and the drums play alone.111 DJs looped that stretch on two turntables, producers later sampled it, and a few seconds of one drummer's afternoon ended up under thousands of other records.

What is the Amen break?#

A four-bar drum solo in the middle of "Amen, Brother", a 1969 instrumental by The Winstons that went out as the B-side of "Color Him Father".11225 The drummer was Gregory Coleman, known as G.C.112 The A-side was the hit, a top-10 R&B single that won a Grammy. The flip drew little notice.6025

The solo starts about 1:26 in and runs at roughly 136 to 138 beats per minute.25111 It lasts about six seconds;109112 some accounts round it up to seven.25111

How did the Amen break spread?#

On a compilation. "Amen, Brother" sits on volume 1 of Ultimate Breaks and Beats, the LP series that gathered the records DJs were hunting for.113 That volume is dated to 1986.60 Salt-N-Pepa's "I Desire" used the break the same year, and by the end of 1988 it was under Mantronix's "King of the Beats", N.W.A's "Straight Outta Compton" and Rob Base and DJ E-Z Rock's "Keep It Going Now".6025

Then British producers got hold of it. In early-1990s jungle and drum and bass the break was sped up and chopped: cut into single hits and put back in a new order.25112 It has since turned up on records by Oasis, Slipknot, The Prodigy, DJ Shadow, Jay-Z and Tyler, the Creator, and in the Futurama theme.25114

What was Ultimate Breaks and Beats?#

The reason the canon is a canon. Lenny Roberts and Louis "BreakBeat Lou" Flores compiled 25 volumes for Street Beat Records, starting in 1986 and ending in 1990 or 1991, depending on whose discography you read.13115116 Breaks that had cost DJs about $20 a copy were now on one cheap LP, with the titles printed on it.115

Whether the series was legal is still argued. It followed Roberts's bootleg Octopus Breaks series, and one widely used discography calls the Street Beat volumes bootlegs too.13113 Flores says the series was legitimate, a matter of getting mechanical and master licenses.13

Did the Winstons get paid for the Amen break?#

Not in royalties. Neither Coleman nor Richard L. Spencer, the bandleader, singer and saxophonist who owned the copyright, ever received any for the break.109122 Coleman died in 2006, homeless and destitute.1096025

By Spencer's account he learned about the sampling in 1996, when he was working for the Washington transit system and a record label called asking for the master tape. By then the statute of limitations had run.602425 His view of it moved over the years. To The Economist in 2011: "Now these guys copy and paste it and make millions."25 To the BBC in 2015: "It's not the worst thing that can happen to you."6025

What changed in between was a fundraiser. On February 18, 2015, a British DJ, Martyn Webster, opened a GoFundMe called "The Winstons Amen Breakbeat Gesture" with a goal of £1,000. The page calls it a voluntary goodwill gesture, separate from royalties or clearance.122 After about a month Mixmag reported more than £20,000.123 The campaign page shows £24,000 from about 1,811 donors, and Mixmag used the same figure when Spencer received the money in November 2015.122109 The BBC put the sum handed over lower, at more than £18,000, about $26,000.25 Spencer thanked the donors on camera: "Thank you so much for this great contribution to my life."24 He died in 2020.25

On May 14, 2026, the Library of Congress named "Amen, Brother" to the National Recording Registry, one of 25 recordings in that year's class. Its announcement credits Coleman by name for the six-second break.58

Who played the Funky Drummer break?#

Clyde Stubblefield, on a James Brown single recorded in 1969 and released in 1970.124125 The break is an eight-bar drum solo, about 20 seconds long, built on 16th-note hi-hats with ghosted snare notes in between.125124112

It runs under Public Enemy's "Fight the Power", LL Cool J's "Mama Said Knock You Out", Run-D.M.C.'s "Run's House", Dr. Dre's "Let Me Ride" and the Beastie Boys' "Shadrach", and outside hip-hop on George Michael's "Freedom '90" and Ed Sheeran's "Shirtsleeves".124 WhoSampled's count rose from 1,302 in September 2016 to 1,987 in July 2025.117110

Stubblefield was not listed as a songwriter and saw little in royalties.124 "I got none of it," he said, and to the New York Times: "They never gave me credit, never paid me."124 Money from cleared uses went to Brown and now goes to his estate.125 Prince paid about $90,000 of Stubblefield's medical bills, a reported figure dated to 2000.126124 Stubblefield died of kidney failure on February 18, 2017, at 73.124

Where does the famous shout in "Think" come from?#

Lyn Collins's "Think (About It)", released in June 1972 on People Records, written and produced by James Brown, with The J.B.'s playing and John "Jabo" Starks on drums.127 Producers take three separate things from it: the drum loop that starts about 1:21 in, the tambourine, and the two-word vocal shout from the break, a vocal stab in producer terms.128129 Splice credits the first shout to Bobby Byrd and the second to Brown; other accounts name nobody.128129

The first known use of the stab is The Beatmasters' production of Cookie Crew's "Females (Get On Up)" in 1987.129 A year later Rob Base and DJ E-Z Rock's "It Takes Two" took it platinum, with the sung hook re-recorded by Rhonda Parris.127128 A section of the drum loop runs through early-1990s jungle and drum and bass, and it is a breakcore staple too.129 WhoSampled's count rose from 1,588 in September 2016 to 3,984 in July 2025.117110

What is the Apache break?#

A long drum-and-bongo passage from a studio project whose kit drummer is still disputed. "Apache" was Jerry Lordan's tune, recorded by Bert Weedon in 1960, and a UK No. 1 for five weeks for The Shadows that year.130 The version that matters to DJs is the Incredible Bongo Band's, from the 1973 album Bongo Rock, a project of MGM executive Michael Viner and arranger Perry Botkin Jr.131132

King Errisson played the percussion.131 The kit was either Jim Gordon in Los Angeles or Kat Hendrikse in Vancouver, and it is unclear whose take is on the master.131112 The 2013 documentary Sample This tells the story at feature length.133

DJ Kool Herc looped the break in his "Merry-Go-Round" routine; Complex puts that at about 1973, Wikipedia in the late 1970s.130132 It reached records in 1981 with the Sugarhill Gang's replayed "Apache (Jump On It)" and Grandmaster Flash's "The Adventures of Grandmaster Flash on the Wheels of Steel", then Goldie's "Inner City Life" (1994) and Nas's "Made You Look" (2002).131 WhoSampled listed 808 uses as of July 2025.110

Who played the drums on "Impeach the President"?#

Roy C, who wrote and produced the record, said he did not remember the drummer's name.134 The Honey Drippers were high school students from Jamaica, Queens, and the 1973 single came out on Alaga Records.134 What gets lifted is the opening bar of drums.134

It owes its second life to a bulk buy. In 1980 Aaron Fuchs of Tuff City bought about 50 copies at 25 cents each and handed them to DJs.134 Marley Marl put it under MC Shan's "The Bridge" (1986) and Eric B. and Rakim's "Eric B. Is President"; it is also on Audio Two's "Top Billin'" (1987), The Notorious B.I.G.'s "Unbelievable", Digable Planets' "Rebirth of Slick" and Nas's "I Can".134 WhoSampled listed 824 uses as of July 2025.110

As of 2018 Roy C had earned no royalties from sample use, and his attempt to sue over Shaggy and Janet Jackson's "Luv Me, Luv Me" (1998) went nowhere.134 Tuff City sued Sony and Def Jam in 1992 over LL Cool J and EPMD tracks, and Fuchs said that year that he licensed the break for a "low four-figure sum".135

How a flip gets made

The short version: A flip starts with a find - a record nobody else has, or a new idea about one everybody has - and ends with that sound doing a job its makers never planned. In between, the producer loops, chops, filters, re-pitches, stretches and layers it, and most of those moves began as workarounds for machines with a few seconds of memory.2112 Since about 2018, software has been able to pull the voice, drums or bass back out of a finished mix, which changes what there is to find.166167

What does it mean to flip a sample?#

To flip a sample is to change it enough that it does new work. A straight loop of four bars is a sample. The same four bars cut into pieces, re-ordered, slowed, filtered down to the bassline and laid under different drums is a flip. The word covers every decision between hearing a record and finishing a beat.

Ethan Hein's hip-hop glossary gets it into one line: "To creatively repurpose or alter a sample, especially in an unexpected way."168

What is crate digging?#

Crate digging is hunting through records for something to sample.171 The prize, for a long time, was the break: the stretch of a record where the band drops out and the drummer carries on alone.13 The most-used ones are in /breaks/. (How records get made and pressed in the first place is its own story: https://groove.cold.fyi/.)

Digging got a shortcut early. Paul Winley put out "Super Disco Brakes" in 1979, and in the early 1980s Lenny Roberts was pressing bootleg 12-inches under the name "Octopus Breaks".13 In 1985 Roberts founded Street Beat Records to do it legitimately, with licensing, and from 1986 to 1991 he and Louis "BreakBeat Lou" Flores compiled 25 volumes of "Ultimate Breaks & Beats".13142 "Amen, Brother", "Funky Drummer", "Apache" and "Impeach the President" are all on it,142 and all in the breaks table at /crate/.

Lou did more than compile. "I extended many drum breaks so that it would be easier for D.J.'s and producers to use them," he has said.13 He also slowed "Amen, Brother" from 45 to 33 for the break, and that slowed version is the one on N.W.A.'s "Straight Outta Compton".13 The compilation had flipped the record before the producers got to it. Roberts died in 1996.13

What is library music?#

Library music is, in Jonny Trunk's words, "non-commercial music made for economic use in film, TV and broadcasting".172 It was never sold in shops. Labels such as KPM, De Wolfe, Bruton, Chappell and Themes in the UK, and Telemusic and others in France, Italy and Germany, pressed it in runs of about 200 to 500 copies.172 When the libraries cleared out their vinyl in the mid-1990s it landed with collectors, and by 2014 some LPs were reported to fetch £1,000 or more.172

A lot of the British catalog was recorded abroad, and taste had nothing to do with it. From 1965 to 1978 the UK Musicians' Union kept an embargo on library sessions, so publishers booked studios in Germany, Belgium, the Netherlands and Switzerland instead.173 It ended with an agreement with Bruton, KPM and Themes on July 1, 1978, which set a fee of £35 for a three-hour session.173

Producers like library records for the playing and for the paperwork: the library owns the whole copyright, so there is one party to deal with when it comes to /clearing/.163 Madlib and Freddie Gibbs's "Thuggin'" is built on "Way Star" by Rubba, a library cut, and KPM recordings by Alan Hawkshaw have turned up under JAY-Z and Drake.163 The modern descendant is Frank Dukes's Kingsway Music Library, new music made specifically to be sampled.163

How do you find out what a song sampled?#

The usual first stop is WhoSampled, a database built from user submissions. In November 2025 it listed 1,246,970 songs and 622,026 samples, by its own count.174 On November 19, 2025 Spotify announced it had bought the site for an undisclosed sum, to feed a feature called SongDNA; the site stays up on its own, with ads and app fees removed.174

Digging has also moved online with a license attached. Tracklib, founded by Pär Almqvist and pitched as "digital crate-digging", launched in 2018 selling original recordings at $1.99 a track, with license tiers of $50, $500 and $2,500 depending on how much was sampled, plus a share of revenue.171 What it charges now is in /money/.

What is chopping?#

Chopping is cutting a recording into pieces and playing the pieces back in a new order.13 The usual origin story is drums: for MC Shan's "The Bridge" in 1986, Marley Marl lifted the kick, the snare and the hi-hat out of "Impeach the President" as separate sounds and programmed his own pattern with them, and he is credited with making the method popular.13

Small memory made choppers of everyone. A machine that holds a few seconds will not take a whole phrase, so the phrase goes in as fragments (/machines/ has the numbers). Ski, who worked on the SP-1200, put it this way: "The limited sampling time made you become more creative."21 In early jungle the same idea was applied to whole breakbeats, sliced into hits and re-ordered from a sequencer over MIDI.12

Why do old sample loops sound like loops?#

Because there was no room to hide them. A loop is a section set to repeat, and on early gear the sustained part of a sound had to be a very short one, so the repeat itself was audible and became part of the character.12 Layering came from the same shortage: one sound's sharp attack stacked on another's sustain gets a full note out of two tiny samples.12 Stack a clean kick under a dusty one today and you are doing the same thing for different reasons.

How do producers pull the bassline out of a record?#

They filter everything else away. The famous version of the trick was an accident. Working on Public Enemy's "Black Steel in the Hour of Chaos", Hank Shocklee found that pushing a jack only halfway into the SP-1200 "filters the high frequency" and leaves the bass.2112 A half-seated cable did the job a low-pass filter does on purpose, and a muffled, bass-only version of somebody else's record became a standard part of the sound.

Why were sped-up vocals everywhere?#

Before time-stretch was any good, the only way to make a sample fit a faster tempo was to play it faster, and faster means higher. That is where the "chipmunk" vocals of early jungle and hardcore come from.12 Kanye West did it on purpose. On turning Chaka Khan's "Through the Fire" into "Through the Wire", he said: "I sample them at regular speed, then speed them up inside the ASR-10."81

Time-stretch broke the link between speed and pitch, and its early flaws became a sound of their own. Jungle producers ran breaks such as Amen, Think, Apache and Funky Drummer through the Akai S950's stretch, and used it on vocals for what one history calls an "odd, metallic-sounding timbre"; Amiga computers running OctaMED and Atari ST sequencers did much of the rest.169 A 1989 review explains the noise: the S950's stretch is a simplified form of digital harmonising, and its delay setting gives echoes at high values and something like a ring modulator at low ones.88 The Akai does not get all the credit. In a 2012 Channel 4 documentary, Goldie's stretched breaks are put down to an "H3 Harmonizer", with no Akai in the story.175

Resampling is the last move in this family: record the sampler's own output back in as a new sample, effects and all. Public Enemy's "Rebel Without a Pause" was first built on an Ensoniq Mirage and then resampled on an Akai S900, and Shocklee felt it lost some edge on the way.81 One old-school tip is to re-pitch a drum loop and resample it before doing any editing.12

Did J Dilla really turn quantize off?#

The popular answer is yes: Dilla left his beats "raw and unquantized", played by hand and never corrected.176 Dan Charnas, whose book "Dilla Time" came out in February 2022, says that explanation is wrong.177

Charnas sorts rhythm into three kinds. Straight time divides the beat evenly. Swing time divides it into uneven pairs. Dilla time puts straight and swung feels in the same bar at once, with some hits placed ahead of the grid and some behind it.170 In that account the lurch is designed. The deviations were, in Ethan Hein's summary of the book, "intended and meticulously executed", and Charnas points to the MPC's independent swing and shift-timing settings, which Dilla was using from about 1997 or 1998.170177 The Smithsonian, announcing the donation of his MPC in 2014, said he is "often heralded as the first" to use off-beat, deliberately imperfect beats.93

What is stem separation?#

A stem is one part of a mix - vocals, drums, bass, everything else - and stem separation is software that splits a finished stereo recording back into those parts.178 For most of sampling's history that was impossible: a sample came with whatever was playing at that moment, which is why breaks, intros and bare basslines were worth so much.

iZotope's RX 7, in 2018, shipped a tool called Music Rebalance that used separate neural networks for vocals, bass and percussion, with a fourth bucket for the rest. Each network estimates a mask, a map of where its instrument sits at every moment and frequency, and it learned to do that from mixes paired with their isolated parts.166 In November 2019 Deezer's research team released Spleeter, free and open source under an MIT license, with a claimed speed of about 100 times faster than real time on a graphics card.167179 It ran from the command line only, and Deezer did not claim to have solved the problem.167 CDM's verdict at the time was that it worked "not perfectly, but well enough to be legitimately promising".179

Then it became a menu item. By one software vendor's account, FL Studio added stem separation in 2023 and Logic Pro in early 2024, with Studio One Pro 7 after them; Serato Sample splits a track into vocal, melodic, bass and drum stems.180 Ableton Live 12.3 went into public beta on September 2, 2025 with four stems, processed on the user's own computer, in the Suite edition only, on algorithms from Music AI, the company behind Moises.181178182 MusicRadar framed it as Ableton catching up with Serato Sample, FL Studio, Logic Pro and RipX.181

A vocal buried under a full band is now a candidate, and so is a drum pattern that never gets a bar to itself. The most public demonstration came from the Beatles. For the 2021 documentary "The Beatles: Get Back", Peter Jackson's team built a system called MAL, and it was later used on a 1970s home cassette of John Lennon singing at a piano, a demo shelved in the mid-1990s because voice and piano shared one track.183 Paul McCartney said: "There it was, John's voice, crystal clear."183 The software cleaned an existing recording and generated nothing; "Now and Then" came out in 2023 and went on to win the Grammy for Best Rock Performance, reported as the first winner made with AI assistance.183184

Is sampling legal? The law, case by case

The short version: Every recording carries two copyrights, the song and the performance of it, and a sample touches both, which is why it needs two permissions where a replay needs one. In the US, two federal appeals courts disagree about whether a sliver of a recording can be too small to count, and fair use has saved a sample-type use about once. In the EU, the Kraftwerk case has been in court since 1999 and produced a new definition of pastiche in April 2026. No country has a six-second rule.

Is sampling legal?#

With licenses from the owners, sampling is ordinary business, and /clearing/ covers how those get made. Without them, the starting point in the countries where courts have ruled is that it usually infringes both copyrights in the record, and the argument is over which narrow exceptions apply.185 Those exceptions differ by country and, inside the US, by which appeals court hears the case.186

Why does a sample need two permissions when a replay needs one?#

The US Copyright Office says it flatly: "Sound recordings and musical compositions are considered two separate works for copyright purposes."14 The musical composition is the song itself, music plus any lyrics, and its authors are the songwriters. The sound recording is one fixed performance of that song, authored by the performers and the producers who captured or shaped the sound.14 In practice the recording (the "master") usually sits with a label or the artist, and the composition (the "publishing") with the songwriter or a publisher.185

Lift two bars off a record and you have copied both things at once: the notes somebody wrote and the particular performance somebody recorded. So a sample takes two licenses, one for the master and one for the publishing.15

A replay or interpolation re-records the part with new players. The old recording is never touched, so only the composition is used and only the publishing side has to agree. It is still a clearance: an interpolation is not a cover version.9 The reason the master drops out is written into US law. Section 114(b) says the recording owner's rights "do not extend to the making or duplication of another sound recording" that is independently fixed, even one that imitates the original.187

Cover versions have a set-rate route in the US, the section 115 compulsory license for "making and distributing phonorecords of nondramatic musical works".190 Nothing like it exists for samples. Sample clearance is a free negotiation, and either owner is free to name any price or say no.9

Is there a six-second rule for samples?#

No. Nor a two-bar rule, a three-note rule or a ten-second rule. "There is no time limit or maximum length to avoid clearance issues," says Tracklib, a licensing service with cleared samples to sell. In-house lawyers writing for the Association of Corporate Counsel agree that no fixed rule exists.9186

The folklore has a traceable origin. In Newton v. Diamond the Ninth Circuit found a three-note, roughly six-second flute passage too trivial to infringe, and the figure escaped into studio lore as a general allowance.191186 The case covered only the composition, since the Beastie Boys had licensed the recording, and it binds only the Ninth Circuit.191 A year later the Sixth Circuit ruled on recordings and pointed the opposite way.192

The "change it 30 percent" rule fares no better; Tracklib calls it "nothing but a myth."9

A third belief is that free releases do not count. Clearance specialist Deborah Mannis-Gardner said in 2016 that free mixtapes still need clearing because they carry promotional value, and Tracklib's version is that the need to clear does not track revenue.1939

What was the first sampling lawsuit?#

The first famous one never reached a judge. (/history/ has what led up to it.) De La Soul's 1989 album 3 Feet High and Rising included a skit, "Transmitting Live from Mars", built on an uncleared 12-second piece of the Turtles' 1969 single "You Showed Me".194 Two former Turtles, who perform as Flo & Eddie, sued the group and Tommy Boy Records in 1989. SPIN reported that November that they were asking for $1.7 million in punitive damages and that the label had offered a flat $1,000 after release.51 The case settled out of court. A UK law firm's later account dates the suit to 1991, the claim at $2.5 million and the settlement at up to $1.7 million.194 Neither the year nor the amount of the settlement has been confirmed, and in 1989 $1.7 million was the amount claimed.19451

The first US ruling came in 1991. Biz Markie's "Alone Again", on the album I Need a Haircut, used several bars and the title words of Gilbert O'Sullivan's 1972 hit "Alone Again (Naturally)". His team had asked for consent and had not received it.22 In Grand Upright Music v. Warner Bros. Records, Judge Kevin Duffy of the Southern District of New York granted a preliminary injunction in an opinion that opens with "Thou shalt not steal" and referred the matter to the US Attorney for possible criminal prosecution. No charges followed.22 The opinion cited no precedent.186 Labels began requiring every sample to be cleared with both owners, and Biz Markie called his next album All Samples Cleared!22

Two years on, in Jarvis v. A&M Records (1993), a dance track had digitally sampled a vocal bridge and a keyboard riff from Boyd Jarvis's 1982 "The Music's Got Me". The New Jersey federal court refused to throw out the composition claim and rejected the argument that the two songs had to sound alike overall; exact copying of a fragment was enough to send the question of its significance to trial.195 Jarvis's claim over the recording failed for a plainer reason: a label, Prelude Records, owned it.195

What is de minimis?#

De minimis is the idea that some takings are too small for the law to bother with. In the Ninth Circuit's version, the test is whether an average audience would recognize the appropriation.188

The leading music example is Newton v. Diamond (9th Cir., 2004). The Beastie Boys sampled three notes of flute, C - D flat - C, from James Newton's "Choir" for "Pass the Mic" on Check Your Head. They had a license for the recording and none for the composition. The court held the use of the composition de minimis and found no infringement.191196

What did Bridgeport v. Dimension Films decide?#

That a sliver of a recording gets no such pass. N.W.A.'s "100 Miles and Runnin'" took a two-second guitar chord from Funkadelic's 1975 "Get Off Your Ass and Jam", pitched it down and looped it, and the track turned up in the 1998 film I Got the Hook Up.187192 In 2005 the Sixth Circuit held that there is no de minimis defense for digitally sampling a sound recording, and compressed its reasoning into seven words: "Get a license or do not sample."187

The ruling binds federal courts in Ohio, Michigan, Kentucky and Tennessee, which takes in Nashville.192 The court expected a bright line to make licensing easier; lawyers writing a decade later said clearance costs rose instead.186 Bridgeport left fair use open as a defense, and the case settled after it was sent back down.197

Did the Madonna "Vogue" case overrule Bridgeport?#

It could not, since a different circuit decided it, but it disagreed out loud. VMG Salsoul v. Ciccone (9th Cir., 2016) concerned a horn hit lasting 0.23 seconds, allegedly taken from the Salsoul Orchestra's "Ooh I Love It (Love Break)" and transposed, truncated and layered into Madonna's 1990 single "Vogue".188 By two votes to one the court ruled for Madonna's side, holding that the de minimis exception "applies to infringement actions concerning copyrighted sound recordings."188 The majority acknowledged it was creating a circuit split, two appeals courts giving opposite answers to the same question, and Judge Silverman dissented in favor of the Bridgeport rule.188186

The Supreme Court has not stepped in, so the answer to "is a quarter-second of a record too small to matter?" still depends on where the suit is filed.196

Is sampling fair use?#

Rarely, on the record so far. Fair use is a US defense that weighs four factors case by case, with no fixed number of seconds anywhere in it.186 Its modern shape comes from Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music (US Supreme Court, 1994), which held that 2 Live Crew's commercial parody of Roy Orbison's "Oh, Pretty Woman" could be fair use.185 Campbell made transformative use, a new purpose or message, the center of the analysis.197 It was a case about parodying a composition, though, and did not address lifting a recording.191

The one sample-type win is Estate of Smith v. Graham (2d Cir., 2020). Drake's "Pound Cake" opens with about 35 seconds of a spoken-word piece, "Jimmy Smith Rap". The recording was licensed; the estate sued over the words.198 The appeals court upheld a fair use finding: the new track gave the passage a different message, the amount was reasonable, and there was no evidence of "an active market" for the original.198199 The ruling was a non-precedential summary affirmance on unusual facts.198

It also predates Andy Warhol Foundation v. Goldsmith (US Supreme Court, 2023), decided seven to two, which held that licensing a Warhol silkscreen of Prince to a magazine shared substantially the same commercial purpose as the photograph it was based on, so the first fair use factor leaned against it.200 The ruling was limited to that one licensing use.200 But the opinion says that adding a new meaning or message is not enough on its own, and lists among the would-be fair users this leaves out "a musician who finds it helpful to sample another artist's song to make his own."201 Penn Libraries' copyright adviser reads that as a sign sampling may be treated as a derivative work and argues it is closer to quotation; he expects a chill.202

Were "Blurred Lines" and the Ed Sheeran case about sampling?#

No. Neither involved a sample. They are composition cases about how much of a song's feel can be owned.

In Williams v. Gaye (9th Cir., 2018) the appeals court let stand a 2015 jury verdict that "Blurred Lines" infringed Marvin Gaye's "Got to Give It Up"; a dissenting judge called the result a "dangerous precedent".196203 In Skidmore v. Led Zeppelin (9th Cir., 2020) the full court sided with Led Zeppelin over "Stairway to Heaven" and treated a descending chromatic scale as unprotectable.196203 Ed Sheeran then won twice over "Thinking Out Loud": a unanimous New York jury in 2023, and a Second Circuit ruling in 2024 that the four-chord progression at issue was "too well-explored to meet the originality threshold that copyright law demands."204205 The Supreme Court declined the case in June 2025.203

The later rulings show US courts pulling back from protecting grooves and common chord patterns.

What happened in the Kraftwerk sampling case?#

Two seconds of metallic percussion have been in court since 1999. Kraftwerk released "Metall auf Metall" in 1977. In 1997 producer Moses Pelham looped a roughly two-second rhythm sequence from it under Sabrina Setlur's "Nur mir".206207 Two years later Kraftwerk's founders sued in Hamburg.208

The case has reached the Court of Justice of the European Union twice. In Pelham I (2019) the court held that a record producer can object to even a very short sample of its recording, unless the sample is used "in a modified form unrecognisable to the ear".189209

Then German law changed. Section 51a of the German Copyright Act, in force since June 7, 2021, allows use for caricature, parody and pastiche.206 In 2022 the Hamburg Higher Regional Court cut the dispute into three time periods and held the sample a permitted pastiche only from the day section 51a took effect.209207 Germany's Federal Court of Justice asked the CJEU what "pastiche" means.206

The Grand Chamber answered on April 14, 2026, in Pelham II. Pastiche is a concept of EU law with its own meaning, and it is no catch-all for every creative use of protected material.210207 It covers "creations which evoke one or more existing works, while being noticeably different from them" and which use characteristic protected elements in a recognizable artistic or creative dialogue with the source.189207 Humor is not required. The user's intent is irrelevant; what counts is whether the dialogue is objectively recognizable. Concealed imitation falls outside.189207

So sampling is capable of being pastiche, with no presumption that it is.206 The case went back to Germany's Federal Court of Justice to decide whether this particular loop qualifies.189 On September 3, 2026 it upheld the Hamburg ruling: the loop has been a permitted pastiche since June 7, 2021.211 Kraftwerk's side still holds its win for 2002 to 2021, which Pelham has challenged in the Federal Constitutional Court.208

The German ruling answers for one loop, and the two CJEU rulings still leave a gap between them. Pelham I favors the sample nobody can recognize. Pelham II favors the reference everybody can. A recognizable sample that is in no dialogue with its source sits between the two.

Is sampling legal in the UK?#

The UK has no fair use. Under the Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988, sampling without consent is infringement on its face if a "substantial" part is taken, judged case by case and by quality as much as quantity.185 In Produce Records v. BMG (1999), whether a seven-and-a-half-second sample was substantial was held to be a question for trial with musicologists; BMG settled. Ludlow Music v. Robbie Williams (2002) found that a short lyric borrowing still needed clearance.185

Britain's best-known sampling fights never produced a judgment at all. In 1987, with M/A/R/R/S's "Pump Up the Volume" at number two, Stock Aitken Waterman obtained an injunction over a seven-second vocal sample from their "Roadblock"; distribution stopped for five days until a deal was reached.23 The Verve's "Bitter Sweet Symphony" (1997) ended the same way, in an out-of-court settlement that gave ABKCO all of the publishing,212213 and when Mick Jagger and Keith Richards agreed in 2019 to hand their share back, the trade press cautioned that the exact effect on ownership was unclear;212214 the money is followed in /money/.

Since 2014 the UK has had its own exception for caricature, parody and pastiche, section 30A, which is a form of fair dealing: it still asks whether more was taken than needed and whether the use competes with the original.215 The leading case, Shazam Productions v. Only Fools The Dining Experience (2022), described pastiche as imitating a style or assembling a medley, with a result noticeably different from its sources. The defense failed there, and no UK court has applied section 30A to a music sample.215 Pelham II does not bind UK courts after Brexit, but lawyers expect it to be persuasive because section 30A came from the same EU provision.215207

Is sampling legal in Japan, Jamaica and Brazil?#

Japan gives record producers their own rights over reproduction and distribution, and gives performers moral rights of attribution and integrity.216 In the Jaco Pastorius case (Osaka District Court, 2018), reusing a recording as background music without a license infringed the record producer's reproduction right, and the court asked whether the sound of the original record was identifiable.216

Jamaica's "version" culture, many vocals over one riddim, grew up under very loose copyright enforcement.217 The biggest fight over who owns a riddim has played out in California. Cleveland "Clevie" Browne and the estate of Wycliffe "Steely" Johnson claimed their 1989 instrumental "Fish Market" is the source of reggaeton's dembow rhythm, naming more than 150 artists and between 1,800 and 2,000 songs.218219 On July 1, 2026 the judge denied summary judgment to both sides and sent the question of whether the rhythm is protectable toward a jury.218220 On September 1 he reconsidered and granted the defendants summary judgment on that theory: the plaintiffs had not identified a single copyrighted work containing the arrangement they claimed, which they had described across three recordings.220221 The order does not decide whether the rhythm is original, and it leaves the remaining claims to be scheduled; press accounts say those include direct samples of the original recording.220222 No appeal has been reported, though a Jamaican attorney who once represented the pair told the Jamaica Observer the plaintiffs can still bring one.223

Brazil's 1998 copyright law has a closed list of exceptions, and Brazilian scholars argue for a looser approach to sampling.224

Are old records in the public domain?#

In the US, far fewer than people assume. Recordings made before February 15, 1972 had no federal copyright at all until 2018, only a patchwork of state law.225 The Music Modernization Act, signed October 11, 2018, gave them federal remedies on much the same terms as later recordings.225 Protection runs 95 years from first publication plus a transition period, and recordings from 1957 to early 1972 are covered until 2067.225190 A 1960s soul or funk break, which covers most of /breaks/, is inside that window.

The state-law lawsuits that pushed Congress to act were brought against Sirius XM and Pandora by members of the Turtles, the same two musicians who sued De La Soul in 1989.225

How long after release can someone sue over a sample?#

US civil copyright claims must be filed within three years of when the claim accrues.226 Warner Chappell Music v. Nealy (US Supreme Court, 2024) dealt with what happens after that. Sherman Nealy's 1980s label released "Jam the Box"; while he was in prison a former partner licensed it, and it was interpolated in Flo Rida's "In the Ayer". Nealy sued in 2018 over uses going back to 2008.226

The Court held that the Copyright Act has no separate three-year cap on damages: a plaintiff with a timely claim is "entitled to damages, no matter when the infringement occurred."226 It assumed, without deciding, that the discovery rule applies, the idea that the clock starts when the owner learns of the infringement. Three dissenting justices said it "almost certainly does not" outside fraud or concealment.226 In circuits that use the discovery rule, an old uncleared sample found years later can carry damages for the whole period.226

How to clear a sample

The short version: A sample needs two yeses, one from whoever owns the recording and one from whoever owns the song, and there is no rate card or central office for either. Each owner names a price - usually a fee up front plus a share of the new track - and any of them is free to say no. Clearing after release is the expensive way round. Re-recording the part instead of lifting it drops the recording owner from the deal, by the US Copyright Office's account, and since about 2021 the companies that own old songs have been pitching that route themselves.

What permissions does a sample need?#

A sample normally needs a license for the master (the specific recording) and a second license for the publishing (the song underneath it), and the owners are often different from each other and from the artist on the sleeve.227 The US Copyright Office's handout for musicians puts it in government prose: licenses from both the sound recording's owner and the musical work's owner, "which in many cases can be different", "may be necessary".1

How does sample clearance work, step by step?#

There is nowhere to look any of this up. Mara Kuge of Superior Music Publishing says: "There isn't a central database or a central way to clear these songs."227 Every deal is a negotiation with every party that owns a piece.227

Deborah Mannis-Gardner, who runs the firm DMG Clearances, describes the job in six moves:228

  1. The artist sends the new track with a list of what was sampled and how much of it.
  2. The agent works out who owns the publishing and who owns the master.
  3. A request letter goes to each owner. The ones who approve send back a quote.
  4. The artist accepts or counters.
  5. Confirmation letters go out, along with release and billing details and the license requests.
  6. It is finished when every party has signed and been paid.

In between, the client goes over each quote with a manager, an attorney or the label before anything is papered.229

What do the owners ask for?#

A quote usually has two parts: a one-time fee and an ongoing royalty.228 On the publishing side the royalty is a percentage of ownership of the new song, so the sampled writers become co-owners of it. On the master side it is a percentage of what the new recording earns.228

Scope gets negotiated as well. Approvals from major labels and publishers typically cover records and one promo video; a compilation, a film or a TV placement means going back for a fresh approval. DMG asks for "all audio configurations in perpetuity and worldwide" to avoid that second trip.227

For documentaries, Mannis-Gardner uses most favored nations terms - every owner gets the same rate, so nobody can hold out for more than the next party. Getting there takes "a lot of groveling and begging," she says, and big feature films accept it less often.230 She has also changed how her firm structures deals, because older sample agreements were built around mechanical royalties from downloads and physical formats, and both are shrinking.230

How long does sample clearance take?#

Dr. Dre's team spent about two years negotiating an interpolation for "The Defiant Ones," and the deal fell through anyway when the owner reversed before it was papered.230 That is the far end. Mannis-Gardner has seen a clearance come back in one day and seen others run past a year.228 Kuge's planning number is at least a couple of months.227

Why do sample requests get refused?#

Because an owner is allowed to. Mannis-Gardner names Prince, while he was alive, and Anita Baker as artists who did not permit sampling at all.230 Often the publisher and label are willing and it is the writer's or artist's personal consent that stops the deal.229 During COVID her firm's denial rate rose to roughly 9% to 10%, the highest in its history by her account.229

A yes from the wrong party is worth nothing. Car Seat Headrest's label recalled and destroyed physical copies of an album after the songwriter behind a sampled Cars song declined to approve it; the publisher that had said yes turned out not to hold approval rights for the US. The band wrote a replacement song.228

What happens if you don't clear a sample?#

Kuge again, on leverage: "Once the album's already out, you have absolutely no leverage."227 The owner can require that the song be pulled.227 Mannis-Gardner adds that there is no exemption for non-commercial releases, that an owner can come asking at any time, and that she has cleared remix albums about 10 years after they came out. "It'll always catch up with you."227 She has also warned that US statutory damages for infringement can reach six figures.228 The statute's range is $750 to $30,000 for each work infringed, as the court considers just, rising to as much as $150,000 where the infringement was willful and falling to as little as $200 where the infringer had no reason to know.235

The standard cautionary case is Grand Upright v. Warner Bros. Records (1991), the injunction over Biz Markie's "Alone Again" told in /law/; lawyers at Cowan, Liebowitz & Latman say that once two licenses were needed per sample, more than one or two samples a song became cost-prohibitive.236

What is an interpolation?#

An interpolation takes part of an existing song and re-records it from scratch, without touching the original audio. The Copyright Office says it may need a license from the musical work's owner and needs no permission from the sound recording's owner, "regardless of how similar the new recording may be to an old recording."1 One negotiation instead of two.

A replay is the studio-grade version of the same move: a new recording built to match the sampled passage so closely that the original master is never used. It is a small trade with its own specialists. Replay Heaven says it has been "recreating existing recordings with extraordinary accuracy" for more than a quarter of a century and lists Clipse, John Summit, Rudimental and James Hype among its credits.237 Scorccio, run by the producer Mark Summers, says it has worked since 1999 and gives its output as 5,000-plus replays in one place on its site and 6,000-plus in another.238 Neither company publishes prices.237238

A replay does not remove the song. Scorccio's own FAQ treats a replay as a cover version for master purposes and says the publishing still has to be cleared.238 Mannis-Gardner makes the same point from the other side: she has had takedown notices over tracks whose makers thought they had changed the original enough.229

Can you clear a sample yourself?#

The steps are letters, quotes and signatures. The attorney Karl Fowlkes's 2026 walkthrough is written for artists who mean to send the request themselves, though he expects most will end up hiring a lawyer.232 Kuge's advice is that artists should not try it alone.227 A distributor will not do it: DistroKid says "we cannot help approve the use of samples from other songs".242 What follows is what clearance agents, Fowlkes and the US Copyright Office tell people doing this for the first time. It is general information, and none of it is legal advice.

  • The source, noted the day it is sampled. Song, artist, label, where in the track, how long. Mannis-Gardner's reason: "A lot of producers create beats and then they forget where they got the sample from."227
  • Two permissions. The Copyright Office treats the song and the recording as separate works, often separately owned. Kuge says a friend's verbal yes would not hold up in court, and that there may be owners the friend does not know about.2271
  • No seconds rule. The Copyright Office labels the belief that seven seconds or less is always fair use as fiction, in capitals, and says "there is no formula." It calls "no infringement intended" disclaimers an internet myth and says only a court can determine fair use.1
  • The ask comes before release, upload or sale of the beat. Afterward, the agents say, the leverage is gone and the owner can have the song pulled.227
  • An early start and a fallback. Owners refuse. Kuge allows at least a couple of months, and Mannis-Gardner has seen it pass a year.227228
  • Plain facts for the owners. Fowlkes's list is artist, song, what was used, how much, where it sits in the new track and what kind of release.232
  • Everything in writing. Fowlkes and the agents want fee, splits, credit and permitted uses on paper, with a check on whether video, sync and compilations are included.232227
  • A replay removes the master and leaves the song. That is how the Copyright Office and a replay studio both describe it: the song's owner still has to license it.1238
  • A cover is a different animal. In the US, the Copyright Office explains, a straight cover of a released song can go through the statutory mechanical license, provided the arrangement leaves the basic melody and character alone. That license grants nothing in anyone's recording.1
  • Cleared material, if the quote is out of reach. Mannis-Gardner points producers who cannot afford clearance to Tracklib and Splice,230 both covered in /money/.

Who gets paid

The short version: Sample money follows paperwork. It goes to whoever owns the recording and whoever owns the song, which is often a label, a publisher or a fund that bought the catalog, and often not the musician you can hear. The drummer on the Amen break never received a royalty for it. A newer economy sells sounds that are cleared in advance - Splice, Tracklib, composer libraries - and each comes with fine print about what "royalty-free" covers.

Where does the money from a sample go?#

When Beyoncé's "Energy" came out in 2022 with an interpolation of Kelis's 2003 hit "Milkshake," the credits went to Pharrell Williams and Chad Hugo, who wrote it. Kelis is not a credited writer of her own best-known song. She objected publicly that nobody had told her, and within days the interpolation was gone and Williams and Hugo were off the credits.243 Permission and payment run to the names on the copyright, and the voice on the record may not be one of them.

A clearance deal pays a fee, a share of the new song to the sampled song's writers and publishers, and a percentage of the new recording's revenue to whoever owns the sampled master.228 The shares vary enormously. Paul Anka, offered a small piece of Doja Cat's "Freak" in 2021, shrugged: "I'm not going to fight over 3 percent. Take it."239

Increasingly the owner is an investor. Primary Wave has operated since 2006 and grew fast after a reported $300 million investment in 2016 from firms including BlackRock.233 Hipgnosis bought the Rick James catalog and offered stems to producers, which led to Nicki Minaj's "Super Freaky Girl" in 2022. Primary Wave bought a share of Rick Astley's publishing and then pitched the idea that became Yung Gravy's "Betty (Get Money)" the same year.233 A song that lands inside a new hit earns again. Ava Max's "Kings and Queens" (2020) credits Desmond Child for an interpolated chorus melody, which gave that melody its first platinum certification 35 years after he first used it.239

Not everyone reads that as good news for musicians. The FADER's column on the catalog funds argues the negotiations "tend to benefit executives and middlemen more than the artists."233 Credit lines are getting longer either way. In 2025, 44% of Billboard Hot 100 top 10 songs had five or more credited writers, up from 37% in 2024, by one analyst's count, though the report does not say how many of those names are interpolation credits.244

Did the Amen break drummer get paid?#

No. Gregory "G.C." Coleman played the break on the Winstons' "Amen, Brother" in 1969, and the bandleader, Richard L. Spencer, held the copyright. Neither received royalties from its use on other people's records.10925 Spencer learned about the sampling in 1996, by which point the three-year limitation period had run out. Coleman died in 2006.2425

In 2015 two British DJs, Martyn Webster and Steve Theobald, organized a GoFundMe that paid Spencer.10924 Mixmag put the total at £24,000.109 The BBC figure, as relayed by Wikipedia, is "more than £18,000," about $26,000, possibly an earlier count.25 It was a collection from fans, and no royalty came with it.

Does George Clinton own the Parliament-Funkadelic samples?#

Mostly not, by the court record so far. Bridgeport Music, of Bridgeport v. Dimension Films, owns the rights to about 170 songs written by Clinton and his bandmates, as NPR reported in 2012. Bridgeport says Clinton signed them over in 1982 and 1983; Clinton disputes the signature.245 In 2001 a federal court in Tallahassee ruled that Clinton did not own the publishing in songs written between 1976 and 1983. In 2005 a federal judge in Los Angeles returned the masters of four Funkadelic albums to him, and Bridgeport kept the publishing.245 Bridgeport's attorney, Richard Busch, told NPR: "to the best of my knowledge, Bridgeport has prevailed each time."245

Why did The Verve lose the royalties to "Bitter Sweet Symphony"?#

Half the paperwork was done. "Bitter Sweet Symphony" (1997) uses a few seconds of the Andrew Oldham Orchestra's 1965 recording of the Rolling Stones' "The Last Time." According to the band's manager, John Kennedy, Decca cleared the recording; the publishing - the Jagger-Richards song, administered by ABKCO - was sought late, after copies had been pressed.213

ABKCO ended up with 100% of the publishing, including Richard Ashcroft's own writer share, and the credit became Jagger/Richards/Ashcroft. Billboard describes a dispute settled out of court; Rolling Stone describes a lawsuit from ABKCO's Allen Klein that resulted in Ashcroft handing over the royalties.213246 Ashcroft's only publishing payment was a reported $1,000, as part of the settlement. By 2019 Billboard estimated the song had earned almost $5 million in publishing.213

In April 2019 Mick Jagger and Keith Richards signed their share over to Ashcroft and dropped their credit. He announced it at the Ivor Novello Awards on May 23, 2019, calling it "a truly kind and magnanimous thing for them to do."246 A month later ABKCO was still the song's sole publisher.213

How much does Sting make from "I'll Be Missing You"?#

Why was De La Soul not on Spotify?#

Because the first six albums had never been legally available in digital form at all, and getting them there meant going back to the owners of everything on them.234 Tommy Boy first tried to clear the catalog in-house and then hired DMG Clearances. Owners were reluctant to step into the group's dispute with the label, some wanted back payment, and the effort stalled.234

Reservoir Media then bought the catalog and worked with the group. Deborah Mannis-Gardner started over in January 2022 and said, "It actually took one year." Streaming was announced in January 2023 and began on March 3, 2023.234 On a handful of songs the release used replays and interpolations in place of the original samples - for cost, for fear of a refusal, or to keep some of the revenue with the group.234

Mannis-Gardner contrasts that with "Paul's Boutique": Capitol/Universal and the Beastie Boys took the risk of putting their album on streaming services, and De La Soul's rights holders did not.234

  • Mac Miller. The 2014 mixtape "Faces" arrived on streaming on October 15, 2021, seven years late, held back by sample clearance.252
  • Kendrick Lamar. Mannis-Gardner says some tracks with uncleared samples were pulled from "To Pimp a Butterfly" before it came out.230

Are Splice samples really royalty-free?#

Yes, in the sense Splice defines, and that sense is narrower than "yours." Its licensing FAQ says every downloaded sound comes with a royalty-free, non-exclusive, perpetual license that survives canceling the subscription, that no credit to Splice or the sample's creator is required, and that it places no restriction on sync use.253 By the same terms the finished track belongs to the person who made it and the sound does not: "you do not own exclusive rights to the actual Sounds."253

The license stops short of several things. It does not let a buyer pass the sounds on as samples or loops, sublicense them in isolation even after modifying them, put them in a competing pack, use the creator's name or likeness without written permission, or train AI on them.253 And because thousands of people hold the same loop, Splice says it "can't guarantee" a track will not be flagged by Content ID; it issues a certified license, covering up to 25 samples, for disputing a claim.253

The company behind that FAQ was founded in 2013 and is large. In 2025, sources told the Financial Times it had about 600,000 paying subscribers and more than $100 million in annual revenue. By its own count Splice had nearly 350 million sample downloads in 2024, and it was reported to be valued at nearly $500 million in 2021.254 In April 2025 it bought Spitfire Audio for an undisclosed sum the FT put at about $50 million.254 On April 15, 2026, it announced AI tools built on a catalog of more than 3 million licensed samples, with a payment to the original creator each time a sound is used as a source and each time a variation is downloaded.255

How is Tracklib different from Splice?#

Splice sells sounds made to be used. Tracklib sells pieces of real records with the paperwork attached. The Swedish service launched in April 2018 with original recordings at $1.99 a track, a release license of $50 to $2,500, and a revenue share of 2% to 50% depending on how long the sample runs, by press accounts of the launch.256 It raised a reported $1.7 million in 2019 in a round led by Sony Innovation Fund and WndrCo, and moved to subscriptions in May 2020 at $5.99, $13.99 and $29.99 a month.257258

Today its licensing page bundles "Sample clearance with no upfront fee" into its Create plan in exchange for "a small percentage of any potential income," without stating the percentage, and describes its own loops as "100% royalty-free."259 In February 2025 it added a model for labels: a lower record royalty in exchange for a higher upfront fee, so the sample deal fits an existing artist contract.260

Who makes the loops?#

Frank Dukes (Adam Feeney) started Kingsway Music Library in 2011 because clearing samples frustrated him. His answer was to write original compositions meant to be sampled. Boi-1da used one on Drake's "0 to 100" in 2014, which led to Feeney's publishing deal and the first real money he made from music.261 Kingsway now lists more than 100 volumes, with contributors including Louis Bell and Allen Ritter, and claims songs built on its material have passed 10 billion streams.262 Dukes was the most-sampled artist on Tracklib in 2021.241

Kingsway's basic license, as the software lawyer Kyle Mitchell read it in 2021, worked like this: about $40 a pack covered self-released recordings up to one million streams, and a major-label release meant going back to Kingsway to negotiate with the loop's producer, with clearance not guaranteed and a co-producer credit required.263

Beat stores work on similar fine print. The Copyright Office warns that "lease," "premium" and "exclusive" are often all licenses of differing scope, that a "free" beat can carry terms, and that licenses can come with stream caps and expiry dates.1

Will a distributor release a song with an uncleared sample?#

DistroKid distributes covers, but its help page says "we cannot help approve the use of samples from other songs" and that stores do not allow another artist's stem or sample without permission.242 The same page accepts royalty-free samples from Splice, Loopcloud and the libraries that ship with a DAW, and says a track built on them is ineligible for DistroKid's YouTube Content ID add-on.242

TuneCore files samples, interpolations, remixes, mixtapes and mashups under one rule. A release that uses a recording you did not make needs a master use license from that recording's owner, and its help page is blunt about the rest: you "cannot distribute any content through TuneCore if you do not have 100% of the rights to that content."264

Are DJ mixes cleared?#

A DJ mix is an hour of other people's records, so the licensing sits with the platform. SoundCloud has scanned uploads with automated content identification since 2010. In December 2016 its co-founder Eric Wahlforss said new licensing deals, with GEMA "among others," meant DJ mixes would no longer be removed for copyright - a remark that reached the English-language press secondhand, with no comment from the company at the time.265 One distributor's guide describes Mixcloud as licensed with major labels and publishers, identifying the tracks in each show by audio fingerprint and paying rights holders on plays.266 Mixcloud's own help page says only that it is licensed and partners with "many of the largest rights holders, record labels and publishers around the world," without naming them.267 The booth end of that story is at https://showfile.cold.fyi/.

Sampling around the world

The short version: Every scene samples its own way. Boom bap chopped drums off vinyl, the West Coast hired players to replay funk, French producers ran disco through a filter, jungle built a genre on a few seconds of a 1969 drum solo, and a 1989 Jamaican riddim sits under most of reggaeton and was still before a US federal court in September 2026. Three questions follow the practice everywhere: who is credited, who is paid, and whether a rhythm can be owned at all.

What is sample drill?#

Around 2021 New York drill producers started dropping whole, familiar hooks over the genre's sliding 808s. Complex named it sample drill in May 2022 and called it a "nostalgic-yet-modern sound".273 The marker record is B-Lovee's "My Everything," produced by Cash Cobain around Mary J. Blige's "Everything"; Complex counted 468,000 TikTok videos using it in 2022.273

Cash Cobain was plain about the paperwork. "My Everything" was cleared, he told Complex, and plenty else was not: "The ones that are just on YouTube, they not cleared."273 He also drew a line at Fivio Foreign's album "B.I.B.L.E.," which samples Destiny's Child's "Say My Name." Too polished to count, in his view.273

What is Jersey club?#

Jersey club came out of Newark around the turn of the 2000s as a faster cousin of Baltimore club. DJ Tameil, one of its founders, dates it to 1999-2000 and says it was first called Brick City club; the name changed once producers from outside Newark took it up.282283 The sound is chopped vocal samples over a triplet kick pattern and 808 bass, with breakbeats from Lyn Collins' "Think (About It)".283 Its best-known sample is furniture: the squeaking bed from Trillville's "Some Cut".284 Drake borrowed the style for his 2022 album "Honestly, Nevermind," and Lil Uzi Vert's "Just Wanna Rock" followed the same year.284283

How do house and French touch use samples?#

House began as an edit. Frankie Knuckles spliced tape to stretch records that were too short for his floor, and the tracks that followed were built on loops of disco, soul and jazz-funk over Roland drum machines.285 One of those machines is part sampler itself. The TR-909 is analog except for its crash, ride and hi-hats, which are samples of cymbals that Roland engineer Atsushi Hoshiai brought in from his own collection.286287

French touch, also called filter house, is the 1990s French version: a sample from a late-1970s or early-1980s disco record, looped, with a filter or phaser sweeping across it so the loop seems to open and close. The names are Motorbass, whose 1996 album "Pansoul" is largely samples and filtered loops, Daft Punk's "Homework" (1997), Stardust, Cassius, and Thomas Bangalter's Roulé label.288

Stardust's one single, "Music Sounds Better With You" (1998), is the method at its barest. The hook is a guitar phrase from Chaka Khan's "Fate" (1981), looped under a new vocal by Benjamin Diamond.289

Why does jungle run on the Amen break?#

Jungle producers took the Amen break, a few seconds of drumming from The Winstons' 1969 B-side "Amen, Brother," cut it apart and laid it over basslines and vocal samples from dub, reggae and dancehall.29025291 The break's own story is at /breaks/. The drum and bass producer Roni Size told NPR: "It was just one of those breaks where you can just manipulate it."290

The genre's name may be a sample too. In Simon Reynolds' book "Energy Flash," MC Navigator traces the word to a chant that Rebel MC lifted from a Jamaican sound-system "yard-tape".291 Drum and bass kept the same short list of breaks - Amen, Apache, Funky Drummer, "Think (About It)" - worked on Akai samplers sequenced from an Atari ST.169292

What are big beat and trip-hop?#

Big beat stacked heavy breakbeats, acid-house synth lines, loops from 1960s and 1970s funk and rock, and scraps of film and TV dialogue. The name has two origin stories: a 1989 coinage by Iain Williams of the band Big Bang, and Brighton's Big Beat Boutique club night, tied to Norman Cook and Skint Records.293 Cook, as Fatboy Slim, made "The Rockafeller Skank" (1998) out of a Lord Finesse vocal, Just Brothers' "Sliced Tomatoes," a John Barry cue and more.294 He came away with none of the royalties. By his account four sample owners "all wanted 40%, or 50%," and once the shares were settled "there was none left for me."295

Trip-hop got its name from Mixmag's Andy Pemberton in June 1994, writing about DJ Shadow's "In/Flux" and other Mo' Wax records.296297 Portishead's "Dummy," released later that year, sounds like a stack of old soundtrack LPs. Much of it is the band. They recorded their own music, had it pressed to vinyl, wore the grooves down and sampled the pressings.298 The album also credits samples of other people, including Lalo Schifrin's "The Danube Incident" on "Sour Times" and Isaac Hayes' "Ike's Rap II" on "Glory Box".299 Geoff Barrow never took to the name: "When I first heard somebody call us trip-hop, I hated it."300

What are plunderphonics and mashups?#

Plunderphonics is John Oswald's word, from a 1985 essay, for music made openly out of other recordings. He gave his 1989 CD away free; the Canadian Recording Industry Association sent a cease-and-desist letter within a month, and under the settlement he handed over his remaining copies to be destroyed.2032

The pop landmark is The Avalanches' "Since I Left You," released in Australia in November 2000.54 The usual count is more than 3,500 samples;65 Wikipedia lists estimates nearer 900.54 The band made the record largely for themselves and kept no list of sources, so nobody can settle it.301 The UK and North American release slipped to 2001 and went out without the samples nobody would clear, among them dialogue from the film "Midnight Run".301

Negativland's 1991 EP "U2," on SST, sampled U2's "I Still Haven't Found What I'm Looking For" along with a bootleg tape of radio host Casey Kasem's studio outtakes. Island Records sued, claiming the music and the artwork infringed copyright and would confuse buyers waiting for "Achtung Baby," and the EP was withdrawn.302303304

A mashup lays one song's vocal over another's instrumental. Early markers are The Evolution Control Committee's "Whipped Cream Mixes" (1994), which set Public Enemy a cappellas over Herb Alpert, and Freelance Hellraiser's "A Stroke of Genie-us," which put Christina Aguilera over the Strokes at the start of the 2000s.305306

Why do vaporwave and lo-fi sound like old tapes?#

Vaporwave, an internet genre of the early 2010s, is made almost entirely of slowed-down samples of 1980s and 1990s smooth jazz, lounge, R&B and elevator music under heavy reverb. Its founding documents are Daniel Lopatin's "Chuck Person's Eccojams Vol. 1" (2010), James Ferraro's "Far Side Virtual" (2011) and Macintosh Plus's "Floral Shoppe" (2011).71307

Lo-fi hip-hop traces to the 2000s beat scene and Roland's SP-303 and SP-404, each of which has a lo-fi effect on the front panel. Nujabes and J Dilla are the names most often attached to it, and YouTube music streams carried it to a mass audience in the late 2010s.308

Who owns the dembow riddim?#

Reggaeton's beat is usually traced to Jamaica. Producers Steely & Clevie cut the "Fish Market" riddim in 1989 and reused its drum pattern on Shabba Ranks' "Dem Bow," which gave the rhythm its name.309310 A riddim is a backing track made to be reused by many singers.

In 2021 Cleveland "Clevie" Browne and the heirs of Wycliffe "Steely" Johnson filed Browne v. Clark in a California federal court against more than 150 artists, Bad Bunny, Karol G and Daddy Yankee among them, and the three major labels, over a song list the press puts at about 1,800 to nearly 2,000.311309221 On September 1, 2026 the judge gave the defendants summary judgment on the plaintiffs' selection-and-arrangement theory, which the defendants had called a "Frankenstein copyright" assembled from parts of three songs, without deciding whether the rhythm is original; the claims over songs alleged to sample Steely & Clevie's recordings directly go on.312220313 No appeal had been reported by September 4, 2026, and no court has found any named artist liable; the rulings are laid out at /law/.223

What does Brazilian funk sample?#

Funk carioca grew in Rio's favelas in the 1980s from Miami bass and freestyle records that DJs bought in Miami.61 Its first rhythmic base was a loop from Los Angeles, the Volt Mix, off a 1988 DJ Battery Brain single; it is in the crate.6261 The melodies are sampled too: horn stabs, accordion stabs, the opening of the "Rocky" theme.61 Since 2023 the car-audio strain, funk automotivo, has traveled abroad under the name "Brazilian phonk," a label not widely used in Brazil.61

How do Afrobeats and amapiano use samples?#

Burna Boy's "Last Last" (2022) is built on Toni Braxton's "He Wasn't Man Enough," and Burna Boy said in 2022 that Braxton takes about 60% of the royalties. Producer Chopstix said Braxton's team was approached after the song was finished.314

Nigerian artists have since turned to their own back catalog. In 2024 Ayra Starr's "Jazzy's Song" drew on Wande Coal's "You Bad," Tems' "Love Me Jeje" on a 1999 original, and ID Cabasa remade Styl Plus's 2003 "Olufunmi."315 Journalist Joey Akan sees the upside: "These [sample] records give an opportunity to the old folks to get new money." Publicist Gracey Mae sees what never comes out, because the original artist cannot be reached or asks too much.315

South Africa's amapiano has no single founder and a disputed birthplace within Gauteng. Its signature is the "log drum" bass sound, usually credited to the producer MDU aka TRP,316 and its sample packs often include sounds taken from gqom.317

Why is K-pop full of old melodies?#

Le Sserafim's 2026 single "Boompala" is built on Los del Rio's "Macarena," with both original writers credited. The Korea Herald counted it as part of a run: NCT Wish's "Ode to Love" draws on The Cranberries' "Ode to My Family," and Ive's "Attitude" uses the melody of Suzanne Vega's "Tom's Diner".318 Classical music serves the same purpose: Blackpink's "Shut Down" (2022) rides Paganini's "La Campanella," and Red Velvet's "Feel My Rhythm" uses Bach's "Air on the G String".318 Critic Lim Hee-yun says a long-lived melody helps K-pop reach non-fans; culture critic Kim Heon-sik warns of "relying on proven hits rather than presenting truly original creations".318

Which Western hits sample Bollywood?#

Truth Hurts' "Addictive" (2002), produced by DJ Quik, runs about 35 seconds of "Thoda Resham Lagta Hai," sung by Lata Mangeshkar for the 1981 film "Jyoti," and drew a lawsuit from composer Bappi Lahiri and the label Saregama.319 The scholars who documented the case, Wayne Marshall and Jayson Beaster-Jones, argue such reuse "unsettles easy charges of appropriation" while still trading on exoticism. They set it beside Timbaland's beat for Jay-Z's "Big Pimpin'," built on the Egyptian song "Khusara".319

What gets sampled besides records?#

Anything that makes a sound. DJ Shadow's "Endtroducing....." (1996), made on an Akai MPC60 with a Technics turntable and an Alesis ADAT tape recorder,321 samples John Carpenter's film "Prince of Darkness" and dialogue from "Twin Peaks".322 James Ferraro's "Far Side Virtual" is punctuated by Windows system sounds and written to mimic ringtones.323307 The habit is older than the sampler. Why is there a sped-up version of every song?#

Miguel's "Sure Thing" peaked at No. 36 on the Hot 100 in 2010. In April 2023, after TikTok users pushed it from 81 to 105 BPM, it reached No. 15.326 Labels noticed. Summer Walker and SZA got official sped-up releases in 2022 and 2023, and Cults made their own sped-up "Gilded Lily," about 10% faster.326 LVRN's Amber Grimes: "It's just too late in the game to be questioning Gen Z or questioning TikTok."326

The slow version came first. AI, stems and what comes next

The short version: The AI tool most producers use is the one that takes records apart. Song generators are a minority habit, but one generated track, "BBL Drizzy," was sampled on a Drake record in 2024 and cleared by paying only the human who wrote the words. The major labels sued the two biggest generators in 2024; three of those disputes settled into licensing deals in late 2025, and in 2026 the holdouts filed second, far larger suits. As of November 2026 no US court had ruled on whether training a music model on recordings is fair use.

How is AI changing sampling?#

Stem separation software splits a finished mix back into vocals, drums, bass and the rest; how it works is at /making-a-flip/. In Tracklib's 2024 survey of 1,107 producers, 25% said they used AI at all, and of those 73.9% used it for stem separation; 82.2% of the non-users gave artistic reasons for staying away.342 A 2026 Water & Music report for Moises, with about 80% of respondents recruited from Moises' own users, found 67% of 1,525 musicians had used AI, with stem separation and vocal isolation the top task at 71%.343 Google searches for "acapella extractor" rose 800% in the five years to 2022, and by Tracklib's 2022 report its tracks offered with stems were nine times more likely to be sampled.5

Do producers use AI to generate samples?#

Some do. In the Tracklib survey 21.2% of AI users generated song elements with it and 3% made entire songs.342 The Moises report put full-song generation from a prompt at 24% of AI users, and listed the two leading worries as authenticity (58%) and copyright or licensing (55%).343 Neither survey is a random sample of working musicians, and both come from companies that sell to them.

The house producer Todd Edwards has described his method in Suno Studio: "make my own disco or soft rock songs, then take that out, cut them up and use them as samples."344 Young Guru, Jay-Z's longtime engineer, guessed in March 2026 that "more than half" of sample-based hip-hop was being built on AI-made funk and soul. That is one man's estimate, from someone who says he still pays for samples himself.345

Can you sample an AI-generated song?#

One test case exists, and it started as a joke. On April 14, 2024 the comedian Willonius Hatcher, who performs as King Willonius, wrote a parody lyric and used the generator Udio to produce the vocal, melody and backing of a fake vintage soul song, "BBL Drizzy." On May 5 Metro Boomin posted a beat that sampled it.346 Hatcher was firm about authorship: "I think it's a misconception that people think AI wrote 'BBL Drizzy.'"346

At the end of May, Drake and Sexyy Red's "U My Everything" sampled it, in what Billboard described as the first official release to use an AI-generated sample.347 Hatcher's lawyer, Donald Woodard, explained how it was cleared: "You only need to clear the human side of it, which is the publishing." He treated the machine-made recording as public domain.347 Hatcher got an advance and a share of the publishing, amount undisclosed.348 Metro Boomin sought no ownership or royalties; his lawyer, Uwonda Carter, called the clearance question "the Wild, Wild West".347

That is one deal between willing parties. Vanderbilt law professor Joseph Fishman told Fortune that a song created entirely by AI would be public domain and free to sample, a scholar's reading and no court's.348 Music attorney Todd Rubenstein pointed somewhere less glamorous: "You really need to know what the terms of service are whenever you use an AI model."346 The US Copyright Office published a report on the copyrightability of AI output on January 29, 2025.349

What happened to the AI Drake song?#

In April 2023 a track called "Heart on My Sleeve," posted by someone using the name Ghostwriter, used AI imitations of Drake's and The Weeknd's voices. By April 17 it had passed 250,000 Spotify streams and 10 million TikTok views.350 By April 18 it was gone from Apple Music, Spotify, Deezer and Tidal, after 629,439 Spotify streams, worth about $1,888 at $0.003 a stream by the BBC's estimate. Universal called training generative AI on its artists' music "a violation of copyright law".351

A cloned voice is not a sample. No audio is lifted, so the dispute moves from copyright in a recording to a person's right in their own voice. In the US Congress, a revised NO FAKES Act was introduced on May 20, 2026. It would create liability for distributing unauthorized digital replicas of a voice or likeness and for platforms that knowingly host them, with a counter-notice procedure and exemptions for libraries and archives. Its sponsors listed the RIAA, the Recording Academy, the three majors, YouTube, TikTok and OpenAI as supporters. On that date it was a bill awaiting action in the Senate Judiciary Committee.352

The labels say so, and that is what the lawsuits are about. In 2024 the RIAA, acting for the three major label groups, sued Suno in federal court in Massachusetts and Udio in New York, alleging mass infringement of sound recordings in training.353

A settlement is a private deal. It sets no precedent and admits nothing.

What did the German court decide about Suno?#

A court has ruled against a music generator, in Munich. GEMA, the German authors' society, sued Suno in January 2025 over six compositions, among them "Daddy Cool," "Rasputin," "Big in Japan," "Forever Young" and the refrain of "Mambo No. 5." On July 31, 2026 the Munich Regional Court I ruled largely for GEMA.369370 It found the works were memorized inside the model, a reproduction the EU's text-and-data-mining exception did not cover, and reproduced in outputs with "no doubt as to the recognisability of the original musical works." It held Suno responsible, not its users, and ordered an injunction, disclosure and damages still to be calculated.369 The judgment is not final: Suno appealed, and the case was before the Munich Higher Regional Court as of September 30, 2026.371372

The case concerns compositions, because GEMA represents songwriters and publishers, and says nothing about recordings. It is also a first-instance German ruling. The same court found against OpenAI on November 11, 2025 over memorized song lyrics, and that decision was under appeal too.372

What has the US Copyright Office said about AI training?#

It has published a three-part report and decided nothing, because it cannot. Part 3, on generative AI training, appeared in a pre-publication version on May 9, 2025.349 In one law firm's summary, it says training implicates the reproduction right, that fair use turns on the facts of each case, and that commercial use of vast troves of works to make competing content, especially from illegal access, "goes beyond established fair use boundaries." It prefers voluntary licensing to a compulsory scheme and has no binding force.373

One question sits right on top of sampling law. US law has long let anyone make an independent soundalike of a recording.187 Whether a generated track that resembles a record, while copying none of its audio, touches the recording owner's rights at all is open. A 2026 Dubspot explainer for producers put it flatly: "no clear legal precedent exists yet."374

Can streaming services detect AI music?#

Deezer says it can, for the two best-known generators. Its detector identifies output from Suno and Udio models, and by the company's own figures fully AI-generated uploads climbed from about 10,000 a day in January 2025, 10% of everything delivered, to about 90,000 a day in June 2026, more than half of daily uploads.375 Deezer began labeling AI tracks in 2025, offered the technology to other platforms in January 2026, and in June 2026 released a tool that scans Apple Music and Spotify playlists. Its policy is to remove AI tracks that go unstreamed for six months or are tied to fraudulent streams.375

Other services drew different lines, as of July 2026: Bandcamp bans AI-generated tracks, Tidal cut monetization for AI music, Apple Music runs voluntary tagging and Spotify has a disclosure policy.375

Can an AI song win a Grammy?#

Not by itself. The Recording Academy's June 2023 protocols say: "A work that contains no human authorship is not eligible in any category." They add that the human contribution "must be meaningful."376 A February 2026 analysis found the guidelines rest largely on an honor system and leave cases such as AI-written chord progressions or cloned voices unclear.377

Is sampling theft or homage?#

Both words have been in use since the 1980s. In 1987 Pete Waterman, whose record was sampled on M|A|R|R|S' "Pump Up the Volume," called it "wholesale theft," his description and no court's.2355 Chopstix, who built Burna Boy's "Last Last" around a Toni Braxton record in 2022, sees a guest spot: "When a song is sampled and done right, it is indirectly a feature or collaboration."314 A Mixmag writer tracing Bollywood samples in Western dance music (/world/ has the records) asked, "as long as the source material is given due credit, what's not to love?"320

AI has restarted the argument one level down. Lawyers on the "BBL Drizzy" deal saw a recording with no owner to ask.347 The labels' lawsuits say the tool that made such a recording was itself built from theirs.353 In Europe, one law firm's reading of the EU's 2026 sampling judgment, covered at /law/, is that prompting "in the style of" an artist and publishing the first result is unlikely to count as pastiche.215

Do the original players get credit and pay?#

Often not. The Winstons' "Amen, Brother" is the most-sampled recording WhoSampled lists,110333 and in NPR's words the band "never saw a cent from it".290 The fundraiser that came later is at /breaks/. Even when a sample is paid for, the check may not reach a musician. Hank Shocklee of Public Enemy's production team said of fees for recordings: "the money just goes to the record company."378

There is a case that sampling pays the past. Burna Boy said in 2022 that Toni Braxton takes about 60% of "Last Last,"314 and journalist Joey Akan argues that such records put new money in older artists' pockets.315

Did clearance kill sample-heavy albums?#

The people who made them say yes. Chuck D: "So we had to change our whole style ... by 1991." Shocklee heard the result everywhere: "If you notice that by the early 1990s, the sound has gotten a lot softer."378 The records in question were dense. Estimates for the Beastie Boys' "Paul's Boutique" (1989) run from 100 to 300 samples, with Wikipedia's own tally at 105 songs, and engineer Mario Caldato Jr. recalled the band spending "over $250,000 for sample clearances" in 1989 dollars.44379

The best-known price tag comes from the 2011 book "Creative License" by Kembrew McLeod and Peter DiCola. DiCola estimated that if every sample on "Paul's Boutique" were licensed at modern rates, Capitol would lose about $20 million on roughly 2.5 million copies, a hypothetical figure that comes by way of Techdirt's summary.380 The book also quotes journalist Harry Allen's back-of-envelope figure that early Public Enemy albums would have needed to retail at "$159 each" to cover royalties, and EPMD's Erick Sermon recalling asks of $10,000 and $5,000 for single samples in the early 1990s.44

The counter-evidence is that sampling never left the charts. Tracklib, which sells licensed samples, counted them in 17% of 2022's Hot 100 hits and found that about a fifth of those leaned on the source so heavily they approached a remake.5 One big, fully credited sample had replaced a hundred small ones.
