The short version: Nobody invented sampling in one go. Composers were cutting up recordings in Paris in 1948, Jamaican engineers were remixing them by 1970, and Bronx DJs were looping drum breaks by hand in 1973, all before a machine existed to do it. The digital sampler arrived in 1979, got cheap by 1987, and produced about four years of dense collage that ended in lawyers' offices. What came after was slower, costlier and spread across the world.

Who invented sampling?#

It depends which part you mean. The idea of composing with recorded sound is usually traced to Pierre Schaeffer, who made his "Cinq études de bruits" at French radio's Studio d'Essai in Paris in 1948. The first of the five, "Étude aux chemins de fer", is assembled from recordings of trains, and the set went out on air on October 5 that year as a "Concert de bruits", a concert of noises.3

The idea of a keyboard that plays recordings belongs to Harry Chamberlin, a Californian who built his by hand: a strip of tape and a playback head under every key, about eight seconds a note.31 The idea of looping the best four bars of somebody else's record belongs to DJ Kool Herc. And the word itself is credited to Peter Vogel and Kim Ryrie of Sydney, who needed something to call what their Fairlight did.2

What came before the sampler?#

Tape. Buchanan and Goodman's "The Flying Saucer" (1956) was a spoken skit with clips of current hits cut in for punchlines. James Tenney's "Collage No. 1 (Blue Suede)" (1961) was built from Elvis Presley's recording of the song.32

Chamberlin's keyboards turned the trick into an instrument, though nobody agrees when. One history has the invention in 1946 and the Model 200 in 1951; another says the late 1940s; Canada's National Music Centre says the 1950s.313334

The famous version is a copy. In 1962 Chamberlin's salesman, Bill Fransen, took two of the keyboards to England and ordered 70 matched tape heads from a Birmingham firm run by the Bradley brothers. A year later the Bradleys were selling the Mellotron Mark I.34 Chamberlin found out in 1966 and sold them the technology for about $30,000 plus royalties.34 Its best-known outings both date from 1967: the Beatles' "Strawberry Fields Forever" and the Moody Blues' "Nights in White Satin".3433

In London the BBC gave the tape cutters a room of their own. The Radiophonic Workshop was formally created on April 1, 1958, at the Maida Vale studios, after studio producer Daphne Oram and her colleague Desmond Briscoe had lobbied for it, and its staff made sound for radio and television by splicing tape and running loops several meters long.35 Delia Derbyshire moved to the Workshop in 1962 and the next year turned Ron Grainer's written theme for "Doctor Who" into electronic sound.35

What did Jamaica have to do with it?#

Jamaica worked out that a finished record is raw material. The story, which rests on his own account, is that the Spanish Town sound system operator Rudolph "Ruddy" Redwood went to Duke Reid's Treasure Isle studio in Kingston to have an acetate cut, and engineer Byron Smith left the vocal off the Paragons' "On the Beach". Redwood played it at his dance anyway.10 The year is given as 1967 or 1968, depending on whose book you read.1029

By 1970 nearly every Jamaican single carried a version on its B-side.10 The stripped drum-and-bass track underneath was a riddim, and producers reused riddims and sold them to each other.29 Dub took the version further: vocal out, bass and drums forward, echo and reverb over everything.29 Its best-known engineer was King Tubby (Osbourne Ruddock), an electrical engineer in the Waterhouse district of Kingston who bought a retired four-track desk from Dynamic Studios, customized it and played it like an instrument.1029 Four different 1973 LPs have been called the first dub album.10

One Kingston kid who grew up around those sound systems and their talking deejays was Clive Campbell. He moved to the Bronx in November 1967, aged 12.364

What happened at 1520 Sedgwick Avenue?#

On August 11, 1973, Cindy Campbell threw a back-to-school party in the community room of her building at 1520 Sedgwick Avenue in the Bronx. Girls paid 25 cents and boys 50, and her 18-year-old brother Clive, by then DJ Kool Herc, played the records.4

Herc had noticed that dancers waited for the break, the stretch where the band drops out and the drummer carries on. So he skipped the rest. With two turntables he jumped from one record's break to the next, and with two copies of the same record he could keep a single break going as long as he liked.437 He called it the Merry-Go-Round; the earliest known one ran James Brown's "Give It Up or Turnit a Loose" into the Incredible Bongo Band's "Bongo Rock" into Babe Ruth's "The Mexican".36 It is a sampler loop performed by hand.

Whether that night counts as the birth of hip-hop is argued by the people who know most about it. Jeff Chang has called the party "a creation myth", and the historian Jay Quan says the same ideas were developing at other parties around the Bronx.4 By 1975 Grandmaster Flash was DJing in Herc's style and Afrika Bambaataa had a sound system of his own.36

Flash made a method of it. He marked the best part of each record with a crayon or grease pencil, worked out how to cue it, rebuilt his mixer, and by his own telling could stretch a 10-second drum break to 10 minutes.38 In 1981 he put it on a record: "The Adventures of Grandmaster Flash on the Wheels of Steel" runs seven minutes, cuts between Chic's "Good Times", Queen's "Another One Bites the Dust" and Blondie's "Rapture", and was recorded live on the turntables.39

Did "Rapper's Delight" sample "Good Times"?#

Mostly no. The Sugarhill Gang's single, released in September 1979, runs on a studio band playing Chic's bassline and groove.7 Nile Rodgers first heard it at Leviticus, a New York club, and took the DJ to be rapping over a Chic record: "I had no idea that he was actually playing a record."8 By his account the bass and guitar had been replayed and the string line came off his record, cued in by hand.8 The usual account of the aftermath is that he and Chic's Bernard Edwards threatened a copyright action, the matter was settled, and both were added as co-writers.7 Rodgers, asked in 1996 whether they sued, was blunter: "Sure, we sued."8

Did "Planet Rock" sample Kraftwerk?#

Also no, though Arthur Baker, who produced it, says a Kraftwerk member's book claimed otherwise. Afrika Bambaataa & the Soulsonic Force's "Planet Rock" came out on Tommy Boy in June 1982. Baker had keyboardist John Robie play the "Trans-Europe Express" melody by hand and paid a programmer to copy the "Numbers" beat on a Roland TR-808.640 The studio's Fairlight contributed only stock sounds: an explosion, handclaps and an orchestra stab.6

The payment is known only from Baker's side. He says Kraftwerk got $1 for every record sold and that Tommy Boy raised the 12-inch list price from $4.98 to $5.98 to cover it. "We borrowed the melody, which we ended up paying more for than if we had sampled it now," he said in 2013.40

What was the first song to use a sample?#

It depends on what counts. "The Flying Saucer" had other people's records spliced into it in 1956.32 The first hit record with a Fairlight on it is usually given as Kate Bush's "Babooshka" (1980), where the breaking glass is a sample.41

When did samplers reach pop music?#

The Fairlight CMI was unveiled in 1979 at around $25,000, an 8-bit machine that could hold about a second of sound.4142 Peter Gabriel set up a company to import it to Britain; John Paul Jones was the first British customer, then Kate Bush and Trevor Horn.434142 Its stock orchestra stab, lifted from a recording of Stravinsky's "Firebird", is the one on "Planet Rock".416

Cheaper machines followed, and /machines/ takes them one by one. E-mu's Emulator arrived in 1981, and New Order used one to sample Kraftwerk's "Uranium" for "Blue Monday" (1983).43 The E-mu SP-1200 (1987) gave hip-hop producers about 10 seconds of gritty 12-bit memory, and the Akai MPC60 (1988) gave them 16 pads and swing.2118

The discovery that mattered most to hip-hop was an accident. Marley Marl, an intern at Unique Recording Studios in the early 1980s, was trying to lift a riff off a record during a Captain Rock session when he caught a snare hit by mistake, and it sounded better than his drum machine's.1617 "Thank God the beat was playing, because it probably wouldn't have happened if the beat wasn't playing," he told NPR in 2013.1617 The year and the machine have never been pinned down; one account puts it a couple of years before 1986.18

What were the collage years?#

Roughly 1987 to 1991, when the machines were affordable and the bills had not yet arrived. Hank Shocklee of the Bomb Squad built Public Enemy's records on an SP-1200 with about 12 seconds of sample time shared between eight pads, by his count, and got around the limit by sampling records at the wrong speed: "You take a 33 1/3 record and play it on 45, and you cheat the system."21

Nobody kept a tally for "It Takes a Nation of Millions to Hold Us Back" (1988) or "Fear of a Black Planet" (1990). Kembrew McLeod and Peter DiCola's book "Creative License" estimates 100 to 200 samples on each of the early Public Enemy albums, and Chuck D told them the opening track of the second holds "about forty-five to fifty voices".44 Shocklee describes the first as built like a band, each one- or two-bar loop standing in for an instrument, with "Bring the Noise" pushed to about 109 or 110 beats per minute when most rap records sat near 107.45

In 1989 came the two albums the era is remembered by. De La Soul's "3 Feet High and Rising", produced by Prince Paul, included a skit called "Transmitting Live from Mars" that ran over 12 seconds of the Turtles' "You Showed Me".26 The Beastie Boys' "Paul's Boutique", made with the Dust Brothers and released on July 25, is usually said to sample 105 records - DJ Mag prints the figure flat, and Wikipedia's fan-sourced tally puts 24 of them on the last track.4647 Mike Simpson of the Dust Brothers has put it at anything from 100 to 300 samples, and two IP lawyers discussing the album in 2025 said they had seen estimates from 150 to 300.4849 Engineer Mario Caldato Jr. remembers more than $250,000 going on clearances.50

That November the Canadian composer John Oswald took the idea to its limit with "Plunderphonic", a 25-track CD of reworked recordings, Michael Jackson's "Bad" among them, with every source credited and no copies for sale. His 1993 follow-up, "Plexure", draws on more than 1,000 tracks by one count and 5,000 tape samples by another.3220 A letter from lawyers for the Canadian Recording Industry Association reached him on Christmas Eve. He settled and handed over his remaining copies to be destroyed; about 700 were already out.20

How did the collage years end?#

In court, or on the way there. Two former members of the Turtles sued De La Soul and Tommy Boy Records in 1989; SPIN reported that November that they were asking for $1.7 million in punitive damages.51 The case settled out of court. A UK law firm's later account dates the suit to 1991, the claim at $2.5 million and the settlement at up to $1.7 million, and neither the year nor the amount of the settlement has been confirmed.26 In 1991 a New York federal court granted an injunction over Biz Markie's "Alone Again" in Grand Upright Music v. Warner Bros. Records, after which labels began requiring every sample to be cleared.22

The usual account says records as dense as "Paul's Boutique" stopped being affordable at that point.4746 Prices did climb: RZA recalls paying $1,500 to $2,500 a sample in his early years, $2,000 for a Gladys Knight sample that he reckoned in 2016 would cost $10,000.52 Sample-built albums kept arriving all the same, DJ Shadow's in 1996 and the Avalanches' in 2000, the second held up for months by clearances.5354 The cases themselves are at /law/.

Was "Endtroducing....." the first album made entirely from samples?#

Guinness World Records says so, crediting DJ Shadow's 1996 album as "the first album to be recorded using only sampled sounds" in an entry that misspells the title.53 He made much of it on an Akai MPC60.18 Oswald's "Plunderphonic", built from other people's recordings, is seven years older, though it was never on sale.20 Guinness gives no method for the award.

What happened when Britain got hold of the sampler?#

It had a hit and an injunction inside the same season. M/A/R/R/S, a collaboration between the bands Colourbox and A.R. Kane with two DJs, released "Pump Up the Volume" in August 1987. With the single at number two in the UK, producers Stock Aitken Waterman obtained an injunction over seven seconds of their hit "Roadblock", and distribution stopped for five days while the two sides made a deal.5523 Editions released abroad dropped the sample.55

The Justified Ancients of Mu Mu, later the KLF, were less lucky. Their album "1987 (What the Fuck Is Going On?)", out that June, used long unlicensed stretches of ABBA's "Dancing Queen" among others. After ABBA complained, the Mechanical-Copyright Protection Society ordered the unsold copies destroyed. Bill Drummond and Jimmy Cauty drove to Sweden hoping to meet ABBA, failed, threw some of the LPs off the ferry and burned the rest in a field near Gothenburg. They then reissued the record with the samples cut out and the silences left in.56 One reference account counts 500 copies burned, at 3 a.m.57

What is the Amen break?#

Four bars of drums played by Gregory Coleman on the Winstons' "Amen, Brother", a 1969 B-side. It lasts about six seconds, 5.2 of them clean drums by UKF's measure.5859 It reached DJs through the "Ultimate Breaks and Beats" compilations of 1986 to 1991, turned up in hip-hop on Salt-N-Pepa's "I Desire" (1986) and N.W.A's "Straight Outta Compton" (1988), and then crossed the Atlantic, where British producers sped it up for raves.592760 Carl Cox's "I Want You (Forever)" and Lennie De Ice's "We Are I.E.", both 1991, are early examples, and jungle and drum and bass were built on it afterward. Mixmag put the tally at "over 2500 tracks" in 2018.27 The rest of the canon is at /breaks/.

Where did Brazilian funk get its beat?#

From the B-side of a Los Angeles electro 12-inch. Funk carioca grew up in Rio's dance parties in the 1980s on imported Miami bass and freestyle records, and DJ Marlboro has named "Planet Rock" as the main influence.61 The beat under most 1990s funk is the "Volt Mix": the "808 Beatapella Mix" on the flip of "8 Volt Mix" by DJ Battery Brain, released on Techno Hop Records in 1988 and carried into the Rio circuit by the importer Carlos Machado, known as DJ Nazz.62 A home-grown rhythm, the tamborzão, replaced it in the 2000s.62 Scenes like this one are followed at /world/.

What does Daft Punk's "One More Time" sample?#

Horns from "More Spell on You" by Eddie Johns, a record dated 1978 in one account and 1979 in another.6330 The liner notes of "Discovery" (2001) credit four samples. His is not among them.30

In May 2021 the Los Angeles Times found Johns, then 70 and recently homeless in Los Angeles, and reported that he had never been paid. A Daft Punk representative said royalties go to the French company that holds the rights, GM Musipro, whose founder said it had bought the catalog in 1995 and would look into Johns's file.63

How many samples are on "Since I Left You"?#

Robbie Chater of the Avalanches gave Sound On Sound a conservative estimate of more than 3,500 in 2002, reasoning from two Akai S2000 programs of 100 keygroups each on most of the 18 songs.64 The magazine printed the same figure again in 2016.65 Other counts stop near 900.54 The Australian group built the album largely on a Yamaha Promix 01 mixer and Akai S2000 samplers.64 It came out in Australia in November 2000 after a delay for clearances, and the editions released overseas the next year lost or swapped several samples.54 The Madonna "Holiday" bassline on it began as a joke on a tape of finds. "We just got so used to it on there we thought we may as well try to clear it," Chater said.52

What was chipmunk soul?#

Rap built on soul vocals sampled and sped up until the singer squeaks, at its peak in the early to mid-2000s. The name is said to be Questlove's.28 Rsonist of the Heatmakerz, who used it on the Diplomats' "Dipset Anthem" (2003), credits RZA with starting it around 1996 and dates the craze from about 1999 to late 2005. Just Blaze and Kanye West made it the sound of Jay-Z's "The Blueprint".28

Who was Nujabes?#

Jun Seba, a Tokyo producer who opened a record shop in Shibuya in 1995 and founded the label Hydeout Productions three years later. As Nujabes he released "Metaphorical Music" in 2003 and "Modal Soul" in 2005, and scored much of the anime "Samurai Champloo" (2004). He died after a traffic collision in Tokyo on February 26, 2010, aged 36, and is now often called a godfather of lo-fi hip-hop.6667

Was "Donuts" really made in a hospital bed?#

Partly, at most. J Dilla's "Donuts" came out on Stones Throw on February 7, 2006, his 32nd birthday: 31 tracks in a little over 43 minutes. He died three days later.68 The story that grew around it has him making the record in the hospital with a Boss SP-303 and a box of 45s. Dan Charnas's 2022 biography "Dilla Time" reports that most of it was made earlier, largely in Pro Tools, and that Stones Throw's art director Jeff Jank stretched a 27-track beat tape of under 30 minutes into the album, with Dilla's approval.6968

What was Grey Tuesday?#

A one-day online protest on February 24, 2004. Danger Mouse had released "The Grey Album" that month in a small run, and EMI and Capitol told him and the shops carrying it to stop.1170 The activist group Downhill Battle answered by organizing about 170 websites to host the album for a day, with several hundred more turning their pages gray. By its own estimate the album was downloaded more than 100,000 times. Capitol's lawyers sent letters.11

Where did vaporwave come from?#

From YouTube. Starting in 2009 Daniel Lopatin, who records as Oneohtrix Point Never, posted slowed and looped fragments of pop songs under the name sunsetcorp, then collected them in 2010 as "Chuck Person's Eccojams Vol. 1".7172 James Ferraro's "Far Side Virtual" and Macintosh Plus's "Floral Shoppe" followed in 2011.7173

The method was 1980s and 1990s ballads, ads and hold music, slowed and chopped, then given away or sold cheaply on Bandcamp and SoundCloud, built on samples one essay called "unclearable".7372 Lopatin's own description was "it's kinda folksy by nature".73 The story from there runs through stem splitters and music generators, at /ai/.

Quick answers

Who invented sampling in music?
No single person. Pierre Schaeffer composed with recorded sounds in Paris in 1948, Harry Chamberlin built tape-replay keyboards in California, and DJ Kool Herc looped drum breaks by hand in the Bronx in 1973. The word "sampling" is credited to Peter Vogel and Kim Ryrie, makers of the Fairlight CMI (1979).
What was the first song to use a sample?
It depends on the definition. Buchanan and Goodman's "The Flying Saucer" cut clips of hit records into a skit in 1956, and Kate Bush's "Babooshka" (1980) is usually named as the first hit with a digitally sampled sound, a Fairlight breaking-glass effect.
Did "Rapper's Delight" sample Chic's "Good Times"?
Mostly no. A studio band replayed the bass and guitar, which makes that part an interpolation, but Nile Rodgers of Chic has said the string line was taken from his record. The usual account is that he and Bernard Edwards threatened a copyright action, settled and received co-writing credit; Rodgers says they sued.
Did "Planet Rock" sample Kraftwerk?
No. John Robie replayed the "Trans-Europe Express" melody by hand and a programmer copied the "Numbers" beat on a Roland TR-808. Producer Arthur Baker says Kraftwerk were paid $1 per record sold.
How many samples are on "Paul's Boutique"?
Nobody has an agreed count. A commonly cited tally is 105 sampled songs, while two IP lawyers on a 2025 podcast cited estimates of 150 to 300 samples. Engineer Mario Caldato Jr. recalls more than $250,000 being spent on clearances in 1989.
Was "Endtroducing....." the first album made entirely from samples?
Guinness World Records lists DJ Shadow's 1996 album that way. John Oswald's "Plunderphonic" CD, built from other people's recordings, dates from 1989 but was never sold.
