Glossary of sampling terms

Glossary

108 terms of art

  • Advance (fee) Money paid up front for permission to use a sample, separate from any ongoing royalty. read more
  • Aliasing False lower tones created when sound above the Nyquist limit folds back down. read more
  • Amen break The four-bar drum solo by Gregory Coleman on the Winstons' "Amen, Brother" (1969), the drum loop under most jungle and drum and bass. read more
  • Anti-aliasing filter A low-pass filter that removes sound above the limit before it is converted. read more
  • Beat lease A limited license to a producer's beat, which can carry stream caps and an expiry date. read more
  • Big beat 1990s style combining heavy breakbeats, acid-house synth loops, funk and rock samples and film or TV dialogue. read more
  • Bit depth How many loudness steps each measurement can use. read more
  • Boom bap Hip-hop built on hard sampled kick and snare, usually lifted from vinyl and swung on an SP-1200 or MPC. The name imitates the drums. read more
  • Break The drum-only stretch of a record that DJs and producers reuse. read more
  • Catalog owner A company that holds rights to existing songs or recordings, such as a label, publisher or investment-backed fund, and collects when they are sampled. read more
  • Chipmunk soul Hip-hop production built on sped-up, pitched-up vocal samples, named for Alvin and the Chipmunks. read more
  • Chop A recording cut into pieces and played back in a new order. read more
  • Chopped and screwed DJ Screw's Houston technique of slowing records down and cutting them with skipped beats and scratching. read more
  • Chopping Cutting a recording into pieces and replaying them in a new order. read more
  • Circuit split Two US federal appeals courts giving opposite answers to the same question. On sampling, the two are Bridgeport (Sixth Circuit, 2005) and VMG Salsoul (Ninth Circuit, 2016). read more
  • Clearance Getting written permission from every owner of a sampled recording and song, ideally before release. read more
  • Clearance agent A specialist who identifies the owners of a sample, negotiates with them and papers the deal. read more
  • Compulsory (mechanical) license The US section 115 license to make and distribute recordings of a musical work, the route used for cover versions. Sample clearance has no equivalent and is a free negotiation. read more
  • Content ID YouTube's audio fingerprinting system. DistroKid says tracks built on shared royalty-free loops are ineligible for its Content ID add-on. read more
  • Cover A new recording of an existing song that leaves its basic melody and character unchanged. read more
  • Crate digging Hunting through records for sample material. read more
  • De minimis A taking so small the law ignores it. In the Ninth Circuit, one an average audience would not recognize. read more
  • Dembow The rhythm under reggaeton, usually traced to Steely & Clevie's 1989 'Fish Market' riddim and named after Shabba Ranks' 'Dem Bow'. read more
  • Deposit copy The sheet music lodged with the US Copyright Office. For songs under the 1909 Act it defines what the copyright covers. read more
  • Digital replica An AI-made imitation of a real person's voice or likeness, the subject of the US NO FAKES Act bill. read more
  • Dilla time Dan Charnas's name for straight and swung feels sounding at once, by design. read more
  • Discovery rule The idea that the limitation clock starts when the owner learns of the infringement. The Supreme Court assumed it without deciding in Warner Chappell v. Nealy (2024). read more
  • Dub A Jamaican remix of an existing track: vocal out, bass and drums forward, echo and reverb added. read more
  • Dubplate A one-off acetate disc cut for a sound system to play. read more
  • Fair dealing The UK's exceptions for listed purposes such as caricature, parody and pastiche. It asks whether more was taken than needed and whether the use competes with the original. read more
  • Fair use A US defense weighed case by case; the Copyright Office says there is no formula and only a court can decide it. read more
  • Flip A sample changed enough to do a job its makers never planned. read more
  • Fragmented literal similarity Exact copying of a small part of a work, as in sampling. The question becomes whether the part taken matters. read more
  • French touch (filter house) 1990s French house style that loops late-1970s and early-1980s disco samples under sweeping filter and phaser effects. read more
  • G-funk Early-1990s West Coast rap style built on 1970s funk grooves, typically replayed by live musicians instead of sampled. read more
  • Interpolation Part of an existing song re-recorded from scratch without the original audio. The US Copyright Office says it needs the song owner's permission and not the recording owner's. read more
  • Interpolation (replay) Re-performing part of an existing song instead of lifting the recording. It uses the composition only, so it needs publishing clearance and no master license. read more
  • Jersey club Newark club style descended from Baltimore club: chopped samples, a triplet kick pattern and 808 bass. read more
  • Jungle Breakbeat style built on the Amen break and other funk breaks, with basslines and vocal samples from dub, reggae and dancehall. read more
  • Layering Stacking sounds, such as one sound's attack on another's sustain. read more
  • Library music Music made for broadcasters and film to license, never sold in shops. read more
  • Log drum The electronic bass sound that is amapiano's signature, usually credited to the producer MDU aka TRP. read more
  • Loop A section set to repeat; on early gear the repeat itself was audible. read more
  • Loop kit A library of original loops composed by a 'sample producer' for other producers to build beats on. read more
  • Mashup A track that blends two or more existing songs, typically one song's vocal over another's instrumental. read more
  • Mask A map of where one instrument sits in time and frequency, used to pull it out of a mix. read more
  • Master The specific sound recording of a song, usually owned by a label or the artist. Sampling it needs its owner's permission. read more
  • Master license Permission from the owner of the sound recording. read more
  • Master royalty A percentage of the new recording's revenue paid to the owner of the sampled recording. read more
  • Merry-Go-Round Kool Herc's name for cutting from break to break on two turntables. read more
  • Moral rights Personal rights to be credited and to object to distortion of a work or performance. Japan gives them to performers. read more
  • Most favored nations (MFN) A deal term under which every owner gets terms as good as the best any other owner gets. read more
  • Musical composition (publishing) The song itself, music plus any lyrics, separate from any recording of it. Usually held by the songwriter or a publisher. read more
  • Musique concrète Music composed from recorded real-world sounds, begun by Pierre Schaeffer in Paris in 1948. read more
  • Neighboring rights Rights of performers and record producers that sit next to the author's copyright. read more
  • Non-exclusive A license that leaves the seller free to license the same sound or beat to other people. read more
  • Nyquist frequency Half the sample rate; the highest pitch a digital system can capture. read more
  • Orchestra hit (ORCH5) The Fairlight's stock stab of a full orchestra, taken from a recording of Stravinsky's "Firebird"; heard on "Planet Rock". read more
  • Page R The Fairlight Series II pattern sequencer. read more
  • Pastiche In EU law since the 2026 Pelham II ruling: a creation that evokes an existing work, is noticeably different from it, and engages with it in an objectively recognizable creative dialogue. read more
  • Phonogram producer The EU and Japanese legal term for whoever first fixes a recording, usually the label. Holds a right of its own in the recording. read more
  • Plunderphonics John Oswald's term, from a 1985 essay, for music made openly from existing recordings. read more
  • Pre-1972 recordings US recordings made before February 15, 1972, which had no federal protection until the Music Modernization Act of 2018. read more
  • Preset A factory rhythm or sound built into an instrument. read more
  • Pro rata Dividing a fee or percentage among owners in proportion to each one's stake. read more
  • Publishing The song itself, words, music and melody, owned by its writers and publishers, separate from any recording of it. read more
  • Publishing license Permission from the owner of the composition. read more
  • Publishing share The percentage of ownership in the new song that goes to the writers and publishers of the sampled song. read more
  • Quantization noise The error left by rounding each measurement to the nearest loudness step. read more
  • Quantize In a sequencer, snapping played notes to a timing grid; Roger Linn dates it to his 1979 LM-1. read more
  • Remix An existing recording with sounds added, changed or removed. read more
  • Replay A new recording made to match a sampled passage closely, so the original master is not used. The publishing still has to be cleared. read more
  • Resampling Recording a sampler's own output back in as a new sample. read more
  • Riddim A Jamaican instrumental backing track made to be reused by many vocalists. read more
  • Royalty-free A license with no further payments after the first. The sound is licensed for use in new tracks; the buyer does not own it. read more
  • Sample A piece of an existing recording placed in a new work. The word for the digital process is credited to the Fairlight's founders. read more
  • Sample drill New York drill that puts a recognizable R&B, pop or rap sample over drill drums; Complex gave it the name in print in May 2022. read more
  • Sample rate How many times a second the sound is measured. read more
  • Scratch sample A sound chosen for scratching, such as the "fresh" line from "Change the Beat". read more
  • Sleng Teng The 1985 riddim built on the "rock" preset of the Casio MT-40. read more
  • Slowed + reverb Internet-era slowed remixes with added reverb, spread on YouTube; critics tie the style to chopped and screwed. read more
  • Song camp A writing session organized by a publisher; some now start from old hits in the publisher's own catalog. read more
  • SongDNA Spotify feature announced in November 2025 that maps a track's samples, covers and collaborators, drawing on WhoSampled; in beta for Premium subscribers from March 2026. read more
  • Sound recording (master) One fixed performance of a song. A separate copyright from the composition, usually held by a label or the artist. read more
  • Sound-alike A recording made to resemble another one. It involves no master license, but the underlying song still needs permission. read more
  • Sped-up version A faster, higher-pitched edit of a song; labels began releasing official ones in 2022 and 2023 after fan edits spread on TikTok. read more
  • Statutory mechanical license The US mechanism that lets a musician record a cover of a released song at a set rate. It grants nothing in anyone's recording. read more
  • Stem One separated part of a mix: vocals, drums, bass or everything else. read more
  • Stem separation Software that splits a finished mix into vocals, drums, bass and other parts; the most common AI use producers reported in 2024 and 2026 surveys. read more
  • Substantial part The UK infringement test: whether an important part of the work was taken, judged by quality as much as quantity. read more
  • Swing Delaying every second 16th note; 50% is straight and 66% is a triplet. read more
  • Tamborzão The drum pattern that replaced the Volt Mix in 2000s funk carioca. read more
  • Tape-replay keyboard An instrument such as the Chamberlin or Mellotron in which each key plays a strip of pre-recorded tape for about eight seconds. read more
  • Text and data mining (TDM) exception The EU copyright exception AI developers rely on for training; the Munich court held it did not cover the memorized works in GEMA v. Suno. read more
  • Think break The drum loop from Lyn Collins's "Think (About It)" (1972), played by Jabo Starks. read more
  • Time-stretch Changing how long a sound lasts without changing its pitch. read more
  • Toasting A Jamaican deejay talking or chanting over a version. read more
  • Transformative use A use that gives the borrowed material a new purpose or message. Central to fair use since Campbell (1994); the Supreme Court revisited it in Warhol (2023). read more
  • Trip hop Label coined in Mixmag in June 1994 for downtempo, sample-based records such as DJ Shadow's; Portishead's Geoff Barrow has said he hated it. read more
  • Type beat An instrumental made in the style of a named artist and sold or leased online, often with non-exclusive rights. read more
  • Ultimate Breaks and Beats A 25-LP series on Street Beat Records that put the best-known breaks on cheap compilations. read more
  • Vaporwave Early-2010s internet genre made almost entirely of slowed-down samples of 1980s and 1990s smooth jazz, lounge and R&B. read more
  • Version The instrumental or dub cut of a song, usually on the B-side of a Jamaican single. read more
  • Vinyl Sim The SP-303 effect that imitates a worn record. read more
  • Vocal stab A short shouted or sung fragment used as a hit, like the two-word shout from "Think (About It)". read more
  • Volt Mix The beat from DJ Battery Brain's 1988 Los Angeles 12-inch "8 Volt Mix" that became the base rhythm of 1990s funk carioca. read more
  • Walled garden A service whose AI outputs cannot be taken off the platform; the condition applied to Udio's existing product after its 2025 settlement with Universal. read more
  • Warping Ableton's term for stretching audio so it follows the project tempo. read more
