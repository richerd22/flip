Sampling lawsuits: the cases that set the rules

The docket

21 cases that set the rules

General information, not legal advice.

CaseThe sampleWhat the court saidWhat changed
Grand Upright Music v. Warner Bros. Records (Gilbert O'Sullivan v. Biz Markie)
S.D.N.Y., 1991		Several bars and the title words of Gilbert O'Sullivan's "Alone Again (Naturally)" in Biz Markie's "Alone Again".The court granted a preliminary injunction and treated the defendants as knowing infringers, since consent had been asked for and not obtained. It referred the matter for possible criminal prosecution; no charges were filed. sourceThe first US ruling on sampling; labels began requiring every sample to be cleared with both owners.
Jarvis v. A&M Records (Boyd Jarvis v. Clivilles and Cole)
D.N.J., 1993		A vocal bridge and a keyboard riff from Boyd Jarvis's "The Music's Got Me" in "Get Dumb! (Free Your Body)".The court refused to dismiss the composition claim and rejected the argument that the two songs had to sound alike overall. The recording claim failed because Jarvis did not own the recording. sourceA sample can infringe the composition even when the new track is a different genre and sounds nothing like the source as a whole.
Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music (2 Live Crew, "Oh, Pretty Woman")
U.S. Supreme Court, 1994		Not a sample: 2 Live Crew's parody of the Roy Orbison song "Oh, Pretty Woman".A commercial parody can be fair use, and fair use is decided case by case. sourceSet the "transformative use" framework that has dominated fair use analysis since.
Produce Records v. BMG
High Court (England), 1999		A seven-and-a-half-second sample.Whether the sample was a substantial part was a question for trial with musicologist evidence. BMG settled. source
Ludlow Music v. Robbie Williams
High Court (England), 2002		Not a sample of a recording: a short borrowing of lyrics.A short lyric borrowing still needed clearance. source
Newton v. Diamond (James Newton v. Beastie Boys)
9th Cir., 2004		A three-note flute passage from James Newton's "Choir" in the Beastie Boys' "Pass the Mic"; the recording was licensed, the composition was not.The use of the composition was de minimis, too small for an average listener to recognize the appropriation, so there was no infringement. sourceThe source of the "six seconds" folklore, though it dealt only with the composition and binds only the Ninth Circuit.
Bridgeport Music v. Dimension Films (Funkadelic / N.W.A.)
6th Cir., 2005		A two-second guitar chord from Funkadelic's "Get Off Your Ass and Jam", pitched down and looped in N.W.A.'s "100 Miles and Runnin'".There is no de minimis defense for digitally sampling a sound recording: "Get a license or do not sample." Fair use was left open. sourceA bright-line rule in Ohio, Michigan, Kentucky and Tennessee; commentators say clearance costs rose.
VMG Salsoul v. Ciccone (Salsoul Orchestra / Madonna, "Vogue")
9th Cir., 2016		A 0.23-second horn hit allegedly taken from the Salsoul Orchestra's "Ooh I Love It (Love Break)".By two votes to one, the de minimis exception applies to sound recordings, and the court ruled for Madonna's side. It acknowledged it was splitting from Bridgeport. sourceThe Sixth and Ninth Circuits now disagree and the Supreme Court has not resolved it, so the answer depends on where a suit is filed.
Williams v. Gaye ("Blurred Lines" / "Got to Give It Up")
9th Cir., 2018		Not a sample: whether "Blurred Lines" copied the composition of Marvin Gaye's "Got to Give It Up".The appeals court let stand a 2015 jury verdict of infringement. A dissenting judge called the result a "dangerous precedent". source
Jaco Pastorius case
Osaka District Court, 2018		Unlicensed reuse of a recording as background music.The reuse infringed the record producer's reproduction right; the court's test was whether the sound of the original record is identifiable. source
Pelham v. Hutter, "Pelham I", C-476/17 (Kraftwerk, "Metall auf Metall")
CJEU, 2019		A roughly two-second rhythm sequence from Kraftwerk's "Metall auf Metall", looped in Sabrina Setlur's "Nur mir".A record producer can object to even a very short sample of its recording, unless it is used "in a modified form unrecognisable to the ear." source
Estate of Smith v. Graham ("Jimmy Smith Rap" / Drake, "Pound Cake")
2d Cir., 2020		About 35 seconds of the spoken-word "Jimmy Smith Rap"; the recording was licensed, the words were not.Fair use: the new track gave the passage a different message, the amount was reasonable and there was no market harm. sourceA rare fair use win for a sample-type use, but non-precedential, on unusual facts and decided before Warhol.
Skidmore v. Led Zeppelin ("Taurus" / "Stairway to Heaven")
9th Cir. (en banc), 2020		Not a sample: whether "Stairway to Heaven" copied the composition "Taurus".Led Zeppelin won. The court scrapped the "inverse ratio rule", held that the deposit copy defines a 1909 Act copyright, and treated a descending chromatic scale as unprotectable. source
Kraftwerk v. Pelham, Hamburg appeal ruling ("Metall auf Metall")
Hamburg Higher Regional Court, 2022		The same two-second Kraftwerk loop, after Germany added a pastiche exception in 2021.The court split the case into three time periods and held the sample a permitted pastiche only from June 7, 2021, when section 51a of the German Copyright Act took effect. sourceGermany's Federal Court of Justice then asked the CJEU what "pastiche" means.
Shazam Productions v. Only Fools The Dining Experience
IPEC (England), 2022		Not music: the leading UK ruling on the pastiche exception in section 30A.Pastiche means imitating a style or assembling a medley, and the result must be noticeably different from its sources. The defense failed. sourceNo UK court has yet applied section 30A to a music sample.
Griffin v. Sheeran ("Let's Get It On" / "Thinking Out Loud")
S.D.N.Y., 2023		Not a sample: whether Ed Sheeran's "Thinking Out Loud" copied the composition "Let's Get It On".A unanimous jury found Sheeran not liable. source
Andy Warhol Foundation v. Goldsmith
U.S. Supreme Court, 2023		Not music: licensing a Warhol silkscreen of Prince, based on Lynn Goldsmith's photograph, to a magazine.Seven to two, the licensing use shared substantially the same commercial purpose as the photograph, so the first fair use factor weighed against it. The ruling was limited to that one use. sourceThe opinion lists a musician who samples among would-be fair users for whom a new meaning or message is not enough on its own; one commentator expects that to chill sampling fair use claims.
Warner Chappell Music v. Nealy ("Jam the Box" / Flo Rida, "In the Ayer")
U.S. Supreme Court, 2024		An interpolation of "Jam the Box", licensed by a former partner of the label owner; suit filed in 2018 over uses back to 2008.The Copyright Act has no separate three-year cap on damages for a timely claim. The Court assumed without deciding that the discovery rule applies. sourceIn circuits that apply the discovery rule, an old uncleared sample found years later can carry damages for the whole period.
Structured Asset Sales v. Sheeran ("Let's Get It On" / "Thinking Out Loud")
2d Cir., 2024		Not a sample: a parallel claim over the chord progression in "Thinking Out Loud".Affirmed for Sheeran: the four-chord progression was "too well-explored to meet the originality threshold that copyright law demands." sourceThe Supreme Court declined review in June 2025.
Browne v. Clark (Steely & Clevie, "Fish Market" / the dembow rhythm)
C.D. Cal., 2026		Whether the 1989 riddim "Fish Market" is the owned source of reggaeton's dembow rhythm, across roughly 1,800 to 2,000 songs.On July 1, 2026 the judge denied summary judgment to both sides. On September 1, 2026 he reconsidered and granted the defendants summary judgment on the selection-and-arrangement theory, because the plaintiffs had not identified a single copyrighted work containing the claimed arrangement. sourceThe order does not decide whether the rhythm is original; the remaining claims are still to be scheduled.
Pelham II, C-590/23 (Kraftwerk, "Metall auf Metall")
CJEU (Grand Chamber), 2026		What "pastiche" means in EU copyright law, on the facts of the Kraftwerk sample.Pastiche covers creations that evoke existing works while being noticeably different, in an objectively recognizable creative dialogue with them. Humor and intent are not required; concealed imitation is outside. sourceSampling can be pastiche in principle with no presumption that it is. On September 3, 2026 Germany's Federal Court of Justice applied the test and held the loop a permitted pastiche from June 7, 2021.
