## The breaks everyone uses

**The short version:** The same few dozen records turn up under other people's songs again and again, and the most used of them is about six seconds of drumming from a 1969 B-side.[^brk-wiki-amen][^brk-mixmag-2015-11] WhoSampled, a volunteer-built database, listed "Amen, Brother" 6,873 times as of July 2025.[^brk-routenote-2025] The drummer who played it died in 2006 without a royalty, and most of the players behind the other famous breaks tell a similar story.[^brk-mixmag-2015-11]

### What is a break?

A **break** is the stretch of a record where the band drops out and the drums play alone.[^brk-musicradar-history-2022] DJs looped that stretch on two turntables, producers later sampled it, and a few seconds of one drummer's afternoon ended up under thousands of other records.

### What is the Amen break?

A four-bar drum solo in the middle of "Amen, Brother", a 1969 instrumental by The Winstons that went out as the B-side of "Color Him Father".[^brk-musicradar-breaks-2022][^brk-wiki-amen] The drummer was Gregory Coleman, known as G.C.[^brk-musicradar-breaks-2022] The A-side was the hit, a top-10 R&B single that won a Grammy. The flip drew little notice.[^gfa-insider-amen-2023][^brk-wiki-amen]

The solo starts about 1:26 in and runs at roughly 136 to 138 beats per minute.[^brk-wiki-amen][^brk-musicradar-history-2022] It lasts about six seconds;[^brk-mixmag-2015-11][^brk-musicradar-breaks-2022] some accounts round it up to seven.[^brk-wiki-amen][^brk-musicradar-history-2022]

### How did the Amen break spread?

On a compilation. "Amen, Brother" sits on volume 1 of **Ultimate Breaks and Beats**, the LP series that gathered the records DJs were hunting for.[^brk-jahsonic-ubb] That volume is dated to 1986.[^gfa-insider-amen-2023] Salt-N-Pepa's "I Desire" used the break the same year, and by the end of 1988 it was under Mantronix's "King of the Beats", N.W.A's "Straight Outta Compton" and Rob Base and DJ E-Z Rock's "Keep It Going Now".[^gfa-insider-amen-2023][^brk-wiki-amen]

Then British producers got hold of it. In early-1990s jungle and drum and bass the break was sped up and **chopped**: cut into single hits and put back in a new order.[^brk-wiki-amen][^brk-musicradar-breaks-2022] It has since turned up on records by Oasis, Slipknot, The Prodigy, DJ Shadow, Jay-Z and Tyler, the Creator, and in the Futurama theme.[^brk-wiki-amen][^brk-tracklib-most-sampled]

### What was Ultimate Breaks and Beats?

The reason the canon is a canon. Lenny Roberts and Louis "BreakBeat Lou" Flores compiled 25 volumes for Street Beat Records, starting in 1986 and ending in 1990 or 1991, depending on whose discography you read.[^brk-rockthebells-ubb][^brk-spin-ubb][^brk-wiki-ubb] Breaks that had cost DJs about $20 a copy were now on one cheap LP, with the titles printed on it.[^brk-spin-ubb]

Whether the series was legal is still argued. It followed Roberts's bootleg Octopus Breaks series, and one widely used discography calls the Street Beat volumes bootlegs too.[^brk-rockthebells-ubb][^brk-jahsonic-ubb] Flores says the series was legitimate, a matter of getting mechanical and master licenses.[^brk-rockthebells-ubb]

### Did the Winstons get paid for the Amen break?

Not in royalties. Neither Coleman nor Richard L. Spencer, the bandleader, singer and saxophonist who owned the copyright, ever received any for the break.[^brk-mixmag-2015-11][^brk-gofundme-amen] Coleman died in 2006, homeless and destitute.[^brk-mixmag-2015-11][^gfa-insider-amen-2023][^brk-wiki-amen]

By Spencer's account he learned about the sampling in 1996, when he was working for the Washington transit system and a record label called asking for the master tape. By then the statute of limitations had run.[^gfa-insider-amen-2023][^gfa-theweek-amen-2015][^brk-wiki-amen] His view of it moved over the years. To The Economist in 2011: "Now these guys copy and paste it and make millions."[^brk-wiki-amen] To the BBC in 2015: "It's not the worst thing that can happen to you."[^gfa-insider-amen-2023][^brk-wiki-amen]

What changed in between was a fundraiser. On February 18, 2015, a British DJ, Martyn Webster, opened a GoFundMe called "The Winstons Amen Breakbeat Gesture" with a goal of £1,000. The page calls it a voluntary goodwill gesture, separate from royalties or clearance.[^brk-gofundme-amen] After about a month Mixmag reported more than £20,000.[^brk-mixmag-2015-03] The campaign page shows £24,000 from about 1,811 donors, and Mixmag used the same figure when Spencer received the money in November 2015.[^brk-gofundme-amen][^brk-mixmag-2015-11] The BBC put the sum handed over lower, at more than £18,000, about $26,000.[^brk-wiki-amen] Spencer thanked the donors on camera: "Thank you so much for this great contribution to my life."[^gfa-theweek-amen-2015] He died in 2020.[^brk-wiki-amen]

On May 14, 2026, the Library of Congress named "Amen, Brother" to the National Recording Registry, one of 25 recordings in that year's class. Its announcement credits Coleman by name for the six-second break.[^gfa-loc-nrr-2026]

### Who played the Funky Drummer break?

Clyde Stubblefield, on a James Brown single recorded in 1969 and released in 1970.[^brk-rollingstone-stubblefield][^brk-isthmus-stubblefield] The break is an eight-bar drum solo, about 20 seconds long, built on 16th-note hi-hats with ghosted snare notes in between.[^brk-isthmus-stubblefield][^brk-rollingstone-stubblefield][^brk-musicradar-breaks-2022]

It runs under Public Enemy's "Fight the Power", LL Cool J's "Mama Said Knock You Out", Run-D.M.C.'s "Run's House", Dr. Dre's "Let Me Ride" and the Beastie Boys' "Shadrach", and outside hip-hop on George Michael's "Freedom '90" and Ed Sheeran's "Shirtsleeves".[^brk-rollingstone-stubblefield] WhoSampled's count rose from 1,302 in September 2016 to 1,987 in July 2025.[^brk-benzinga-2016][^brk-routenote-2025]

Stubblefield was not listed as a songwriter and saw little in royalties.[^brk-rollingstone-stubblefield] "I got none of it," he said, and to the New York Times: "They never gave me credit, never paid me."[^brk-rollingstone-stubblefield] Money from cleared uses went to Brown and now goes to his estate.[^brk-isthmus-stubblefield] Prince paid about $90,000 of Stubblefield's medical bills, a reported figure dated to 2000.[^brk-pas-stubblefield][^brk-rollingstone-stubblefield] Stubblefield died of kidney failure on February 18, 2017, at 73.[^brk-rollingstone-stubblefield]

### Where does the famous shout in "Think" come from?

Lyn Collins's "Think (About It)", released in June 1972 on People Records, written and produced by James Brown, with The J.B.'s playing and John "Jabo" Starks on drums.[^brk-wiki-think] Producers take three separate things from it: the drum loop that starts about 1:21 in, the tambourine, and the two-word vocal shout from the break, a **vocal stab** in producer terms.[^brk-splice-think][^brk-wiki-think-break] Splice credits the first shout to Bobby Byrd and the second to Brown; other accounts name nobody.[^brk-splice-think][^brk-wiki-think-break]

The first known use of the stab is The Beatmasters' production of Cookie Crew's "Females (Get On Up)" in 1987.[^brk-wiki-think-break] A year later Rob Base and DJ E-Z Rock's "It Takes Two" took it platinum, with the sung hook re-recorded by Rhonda Parris.[^brk-wiki-think][^brk-splice-think] A section of the drum loop runs through early-1990s jungle and drum and bass, and it is a breakcore staple too.[^brk-wiki-think-break] WhoSampled's count rose from 1,588 in September 2016 to 3,984 in July 2025.[^brk-benzinga-2016][^brk-routenote-2025]

### What is the Apache break?

A long drum-and-bongo passage from a studio project whose kit drummer is still disputed. "Apache" was Jerry Lordan's tune, recorded by Bert Weedon in 1960, and a UK No. 1 for five weeks for The Shadows that year.[^brk-complex-apache] The version that matters to DJs is the Incredible Bongo Band's, from the 1973 album Bongo Rock, a project of MGM executive Michael Viner and arranger Perry Botkin Jr.[^brk-stereogum-apache][^brk-wiki-bongo-band]

King Errisson played the percussion.[^brk-stereogum-apache] The kit was either Jim Gordon in Los Angeles or Kat Hendrikse in Vancouver, and it is unclear whose take is on the master.[^brk-stereogum-apache][^brk-musicradar-breaks-2022] The 2013 documentary Sample This tells the story at feature length.[^brk-ebert-sample-this]

DJ Kool Herc looped the break in his "Merry-Go-Round" routine; Complex puts that at about 1973, Wikipedia in the late 1970s.[^brk-complex-apache][^brk-wiki-bongo-band] It reached records in 1981 with the Sugarhill Gang's replayed "Apache (Jump On It)" and Grandmaster Flash's "The Adventures of Grandmaster Flash on the Wheels of Steel", then Goldie's "Inner City Life" (1994) and Nas's "Made You Look" (2002).[^brk-stereogum-apache] WhoSampled listed 808 uses as of July 2025.[^brk-routenote-2025]

### Who played the drums on "Impeach the President"?

Roy C, who wrote and produced the record, said he did not remember the drummer's name.[^brk-stereogum-impeach] The Honey Drippers were high school students from Jamaica, Queens, and the 1973 single came out on Alaga Records.[^brk-stereogum-impeach] What gets lifted is the opening bar of drums.[^brk-stereogum-impeach]

It owes its second life to a bulk buy. In 1980 Aaron Fuchs of Tuff City bought about 50 copies at 25 cents each and handed them to DJs.[^brk-stereogum-impeach] Marley Marl put it under MC Shan's "The Bridge" (1986) and Eric B. and Rakim's "Eric B. Is President"; it is also on Audio Two's "Top Billin'" (1987), The Notorious B.I.G.'s "Unbelievable", Digable Planets' "Rebirth of Slick" and Nas's "I Can".[^brk-stereogum-impeach] WhoSampled listed 824 uses as of July 2025.[^brk-routenote-2025]

As of 2018 Roy C had earned no royalties from sample use, and his attempt to sue over Shaggy and Janet Jackson's "Luv Me, Luv Me" (1998) went nowhere.[^brk-stereogum-impeach] Tuff City sued Sony and Def Jam in 1992 over LL Cool J and EPMD tracks, and Fuchs said that year that he licensed the break for a "low four-figure sum".[^brk-wiki-impeach]

### Where does the "fresh" scratch sound come from?

From the last seconds of the B-side of "Change the Beat", a 1982 12-inch on Celluloid Records produced by Bill Laswell's group Material at Martin Bisi's studio in Brooklyn.[^brk-hustle-fresh][^brk-wiki-change-the-beat] The A-side is Fab 5 Freddy. The B-side is Beside, the name used by Ann Boyle, then married to the writer Bernard Zekri.[^brk-rbma-change-the-beat][^brk-hustle-fresh] The sample is a tone followed by a spoken line through a vocoder, ending on the word "fresh".[^brk-fact-change-the-beat]

Whose voice it is depends on who you ask. Laswell, Zekri and Beside say it was Laswell's manager, Roger Trilling; Fab 5 Freddy has claimed it.[^brk-hustle-fresh][^brk-wiki-change-the-beat] GrandMixer D.ST scratched it first, on Herbie Hancock's "Rockit" in 1983, and said later, "It became like my bow."[^brk-hustle-fresh] A **scratch sample** is a sound picked for exactly that job, and this one is on Eric B. and Rakim's "Paid in Full", Slick Rick's "The Show", J.J. Fad's "Supersonic", the Beastie Boys' "Sure Shot" and Macklemore's "Thrift Shop".[^brk-hustle-fresh][^brk-routenote-2025]

Almost nobody was paid. Zekri: "And yet I didn't get a cent from the track."[^brk-rbma-change-the-beat] Trilling was not paid, and Beside does not remember whether she signed a contract.[^brk-hustle-fresh] Celluloid's Adam Karakos said the label had received one clearance request since regaining the publishing in 2008.[^brk-hustle-fresh]

### Did "Planet Rock" sample Kraftwerk?

No. The 1982 Tommy Boy single is a **replay**, a new performance standing in for a sample: the melody of "Trans-Europe Express" played by hand, the beat of "Numbers" programmed on a Roland TR-808, and no permission asked.[^brk-wiki-planet-rock] The session is told at [/history/](/history/).

Kraftwerk approached the label. As the people involved later told it, Tommy Boy's Tom Silverman agreed to pay $1 per record sold and raised the 12-inch list price from $4.98 to $5.98 to cover it.[^his-rbma-baker][^brk-wiki-planet-rock] The single was certified Gold by October 1982.[^brk-wiki-planet-rock] A year later in Detroit, Juan Atkins's Cybotron built "Clear" on Kraftwerk's "Hall of Mirrors" and "Numbers".[^brk-wdet-kraftwerk]

### What is the Volt Mix?

The beat under 1990s Rio funk, and it came from Los Angeles. It is the "808 Beatapella Mix", the B-side of "8 Volt Mix", a 1988 single by DJ Battery Brain (Mark Rogers) on Techno Hop Records, built on a Roland TR-808.[^brk-billboard-br-volt] The beat also goes by "808 volt".[^brk-billboard-br-volt][^brk-wiki-funk-carioca] Rio DJ Carlos Machado, known as DJ Nazz, is credited with bringing it in through the import-vinyl circuit, at a time when Rio DJs were buying Miami bass and freestyle records in Miami.[^brk-billboard-br-volt][^brk-wiki-funk-carioca] It anchored funk carioca through the 1990s, until a pattern called the tamborzão took over in the 2000s.[^brk-billboard-br-volt] Rio's scene and others are at [/world/](/world/).

### What keyboard preset is the Sleng Teng riddim?

The "rock" rhythm on a Casio Casiotone MT-40, a keyboard that launched in 1981 at 35,000 yen, or about $150.[^brk-nippon-okuda][^brk-engadget-sleng-teng] Noel Davey used it because he could not afford a Yamaha DX7.[^brk-wiki-sleng-teng] He and Wayne Smith wrote "Under Mi Sleng Teng" on it, producer King Jammy slowed it down, and Jammy's Records released it in 1985.[^brk-nippon-okuda][^brk-wiki-sleng-teng] In Jamaican music a **riddim** is a backing track that many vocalists record over, and estimates of the versions of this one run from 250-plus (2015) to as many as 450 (2022) to more than 500.[^brk-engadget-sleng-teng][^brk-nippon-okuda][^brk-wiki-sleng-teng]

The preset was written by Okuda Hiroko, who joined Casio in 1980 out of music college.[^brk-nippon-okuda] In 2015 Engadget reported her saying it drew on a 1970s British rock record she did not name; in 2022 she said "the bassline was something I came up with myself".[^brk-engadget-sleng-teng][^brk-nippon-okuda] Some inside Casio raised the idea of suing, and the company chose not to. Its standard answer to clearance requests is yes, no fee, with a credit line saying the track uses a sound from a Casio MT-40.[^brk-nippon-okuda]

### What Bollywood song does "Addictive" sample?

Lata Mangeshkar's recording of "Thoda Resham Lagta Hai", composed by Bappi Lahiri for the 1981 film Jyoti.[^brk-billboard-addictive] Truth Hurts's "Addictive", featuring Rakim and produced by DJ Quik, came out on Aftermath/Interscope in 2002 and peaked at No. 9 on the Hot 100.[^brk-billboard-addictive]

Two suits followed that year. Saregama India Ltd. sued in federal court in Houston, claiming $500 million, a figure from the complaint and not an award.[^brk-billboard-addictive][^brk-mansworld-lahiri] Lahiri sued Dr. Dre, Aftermath, Interscope and Universal in US District Court in Los Angeles, asking the court to halt sales of an album that had sold about 600,000 copies. His lawyer alleged that about four minutes of the recording were used.[^brk-billboard-addictive] On February 3, 2003, the judge in Lahiri's case barred further sales of "Addictive" until he was listed in the song's credits, and moved the trial date up to June.[^gfa-allhiphop-addictive-2003] The case law is at [/law/](/law/).

### Who got paid, and who did not?

Mostly the owners, rarely the players. The wider accounting is at [/money/](/money/).

Eddie Johns is the hardest case to read. Daft Punk's representative said their label, Daft Life, pays royalties twice a year to the producer and owner of "More Spell on You".[^brk-musicradar-johns] The rights sit with the French company GM Musipro, whose founder, Georges Mary, said it had been unable to find Johns since acquiring the catalog in 1995.[^brk-musicradar-johns][^brk-djmag-johns] In May 2021 Johns was living in supportive housing in Los Angeles after years of homelessness, and an attorney, Erin Jacobson, estimated that a song with those streams would generally earn in the "high six- to seven-figure range".[^brk-musicradar-johns]

"Bitter Sweet Symphony" ran the other way for 22 years. After a late-1990s dispute with Allen Klein's ABKCO, the writing credit and royalties for The Verve's song went to Mick Jagger and Keith Richards.[^brk-stereogum-bittersweet] On May 23, 2019, at the Ivor Novello Awards, Richard Ashcroft announced that the two had agreed that April to hand over their share and drop their credit, which he called "a kind and magnanimous gesture".[^brk-stereogum-bittersweet]
