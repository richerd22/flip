## Is sampling legal? The law, case by case

**The short version:** Every recording carries two copyrights, the song and the performance of it, and a sample touches both, which is why it needs two permissions where a replay needs one. In the US, two federal appeals courts disagree about whether a sliver of a recording can be too small to count, and fair use has saved a sample-type use about once. In the EU, the Kraftwerk case has been in court since 1999 and produced a new definition of pastiche in April 2026. No country has a six-second rule.

### Is sampling legal?

With licenses from the owners, sampling is ordinary business, and [/clearing/](/clearing/) covers how those get made. Without them, the starting point in the countries where courts have ruled is that it usually infringes both copyrights in the record, and the argument is over which narrow exceptions apply.[^law-wipo-2009] Those exceptions differ by country and, inside the US, by which appeals court hears the case.[^law-acc-2017]

| Place | Where a short, unlicensed sample of a recording stands |
|---|---|
| US, Sixth Circuit (Ohio, Michigan, Kentucky, Tennessee) | No "too small to matter" defense for a digitally sampled recording (Bridgeport, 2005)[^law-payforplay-ch40] |
| US, Ninth Circuit | The de minimis exception applies to recordings (VMG Salsoul, 2016)[^law-loeb-vmg-2016] |
| European Union | The record producer can object to even a very short sample unless it is unrecognizable to the ear (Pelham I, 2019); pastiche may cover some uses (Pelham II, 2026)[^law-cms-2026] |
| United Kingdom | Infringement on its face if a "substantial" part is taken, judged by quality as much as quantity[^law-wipo-2009] |

### Why does a sample need two permissions when a replay needs one?

The US Copyright Office says it flatly: "Sound recordings and musical compositions are considered two separate works for copyright purposes."[^law-circ56a] The **musical composition** is the song itself, music plus any lyrics, and its authors are the songwriters. The **sound recording** is one fixed performance of that song, authored by the performers and the producers who captured or shaped the sound.[^law-circ56a] In practice the recording (the "master") usually sits with a label or the artist, and the composition (the "publishing") with the songwriter or a publisher.[^law-wipo-2009]

Lift two bars off a record and you have copied both things at once: the notes somebody wrote and the particular performance somebody recorded. So a sample takes two licenses, one for the master and one for the publishing.[^law-spotify-2019]

A **replay** or **interpolation** re-records the part with new players. The old recording is never touched, so only the composition is used and only the publishing side has to agree. It is still a clearance: an interpolation is not a cover version.[^law-tracklib-myths] The reason the master drops out is written into US law. Section 114(b) says the recording owner's rights "do not extend to the making or duplication of another sound recording" that is independently fixed, even one that imitates the original.[^law-payforplay-ch40]

Cover versions have a set-rate route in the US, the section 115 compulsory license for "making and distributing phonorecords of nondramatic musical works".[^law-mma-faq] Nothing like it exists for samples. Sample clearance is a free negotiation, and either owner is free to name any price or say no.[^law-tracklib-myths]

### Is there a six-second rule for samples?

No. Nor a two-bar rule, a three-note rule or a ten-second rule. "There is no time limit or maximum length to avoid clearance issues," says Tracklib, a licensing service with cleared samples to sell. In-house lawyers writing for the Association of Corporate Counsel agree that no fixed rule exists.[^law-tracklib-myths][^law-acc-2017]

The folklore has a traceable origin. In Newton v. Diamond the Ninth Circuit found a three-note, roughly six-second flute passage too trivial to infringe, and the figure escaped into studio lore as a general allowance.[^law-okayplayer-2014][^law-acc-2017] The case covered only the composition, since the Beastie Boys had licensed the recording, and it binds only the Ninth Circuit.[^law-okayplayer-2014] A year later the Sixth Circuit ruled on recordings and pointed the opposite way.[^law-musicologize-bridgeport]

The "change it 30 percent" rule fares no better; Tracklib calls it "nothing but a myth."[^law-tracklib-myths]

A third belief is that free releases do not count. Clearance specialist Deborah Mannis-Gardner said in 2016 that free mixtapes still need clearing because they carry promotional value, and Tracklib's version is that the need to clear does not track revenue.[^law-djbooth-2016][^law-tracklib-myths]

### What was the first sampling lawsuit?

The first famous one never reached a judge. ([/history/](/history/) has what led up to it.) De La Soul's 1989 album 3 Feet High and Rising included a skit, "Transmitting Live from Mars", built on an uncleared 12-second piece of the Turtles' 1969 single "You Showed Me".[^law-briffa-2020] Two former Turtles, who perform as Flo & Eddie, sued the group and Tommy Boy Records in 1989. SPIN reported that November that they were asking for $1.7 million in punitive damages and that the label had offered a flat $1,000 after release.[^law-spin-1989] The case settled out of court. A UK law firm's later account dates the suit to 1991, the claim at $2.5 million and the settlement at up to $1.7 million.[^law-briffa-2020] Neither the year nor the amount of the settlement has been confirmed, and in 1989 $1.7 million was the amount claimed.[^law-briffa-2020][^law-spin-1989]

The first US ruling came in 1991. Biz Markie's "Alone Again", on the album I Need a Haircut, used several bars and the title words of Gilbert O'Sullivan's 1972 hit "Alone Again (Naturally)". His team had asked for consent and had not received it.[^law-legal500-bizmarkie] In Grand Upright Music v. Warner Bros. Records, Judge Kevin Duffy of the Southern District of New York granted a preliminary injunction in an opinion that opens with "Thou shalt not steal" and referred the matter to the US Attorney for possible criminal prosecution. No charges followed.[^law-legal500-bizmarkie] The opinion cited no precedent.[^law-acc-2017] Labels began requiring every sample to be cleared with both owners, and Biz Markie called his next album All Samples Cleared![^law-legal500-bizmarkie]

Two years on, in Jarvis v. A&M Records (1993), a dance track had digitally sampled a vocal bridge and a keyboard riff from Boyd Jarvis's 1982 "The Music's Got Me". The New Jersey federal court refused to throw out the composition claim and rejected the argument that the two songs had to sound alike overall; exact copying of a fragment was enough to send the question of its significance to trial.[^law-mcir-jarvis] Jarvis's claim over the recording failed for a plainer reason: a label, Prelude Records, owned it.[^law-mcir-jarvis]

### What is de minimis?

**De minimis** is the idea that some takings are too small for the law to bother with. In the Ninth Circuit's version, the test is whether an average audience would recognize the appropriation.[^law-loeb-vmg-2016]

The leading music example is Newton v. Diamond (9th Cir., 2004). The Beastie Boys sampled three notes of flute, C - D flat - C, from James Newton's "Choir" for "Pass the Mic" on Check Your Head. They had a license for the recording and none for the composition. The court held the use of the composition de minimis and found no infringement.[^law-okayplayer-2014][^law-musicologize-library]

### What did Bridgeport v. Dimension Films decide?

That a sliver of a recording gets no such pass. N.W.A.'s "100 Miles and Runnin'" took a two-second guitar chord from Funkadelic's 1975 "Get Off Your Ass and Jam", pitched it down and looped it, and the track turned up in the 1998 film I Got the Hook Up.[^law-payforplay-ch40][^law-musicologize-bridgeport] In 2005 the Sixth Circuit held that there is no de minimis defense for digitally sampling a sound recording, and compressed its reasoning into seven words: "Get a license or do not sample."[^law-payforplay-ch40]

The ruling binds federal courts in Ohio, Michigan, Kentucky and Tennessee, which takes in Nashville.[^law-musicologize-bridgeport] The court expected a bright line to make licensing easier; lawyers writing a decade later said clearance costs rose instead.[^law-acc-2017] Bridgeport left fair use open as a defense, and the case settled after it was sent back down.[^law-claflin-2020]

### Did the Madonna "Vogue" case overrule Bridgeport?

It could not, since a different circuit decided it, but it disagreed out loud. VMG Salsoul v. Ciccone (9th Cir., 2016) concerned a horn hit lasting 0.23 seconds, allegedly taken from the Salsoul Orchestra's "Ooh I Love It (Love Break)" and transposed, truncated and layered into Madonna's 1990 single "Vogue".[^law-loeb-vmg-2016] By two votes to one the court ruled for Madonna's side, holding that the de minimis exception "applies to infringement actions concerning copyrighted sound recordings."[^law-loeb-vmg-2016] The majority acknowledged it was creating a **circuit split**, two appeals courts giving opposite answers to the same question, and Judge Silverman dissented in favor of the Bridgeport rule.[^law-loeb-vmg-2016][^law-acc-2017]

The Supreme Court has not stepped in, so the answer to "is a quarter-second of a record too small to matter?" still depends on where the suit is filed.[^law-musicologize-library]

### Is sampling fair use?

Rarely, on the record so far. **Fair use** is a US defense that weighs four factors case by case, with no fixed number of seconds anywhere in it.[^law-acc-2017] Its modern shape comes from Campbell v. Acuff-Rose Music (US Supreme Court, 1994), which held that 2 Live Crew's commercial parody of Roy Orbison's "Oh, Pretty Woman" could be fair use.[^law-wipo-2009] Campbell made **transformative use**, a new purpose or message, the center of the analysis.[^law-claflin-2020] It was a case about parodying a composition, though, and did not address lifting a recording.[^law-okayplayer-2014]

The one sample-type win is Estate of Smith v. Graham (2d Cir., 2020). Drake's "Pound Cake" opens with about 35 seconds of a spoken-word piece, "Jimmy Smith Rap". The recording was licensed; the estate sued over the words.[^law-tushnet-2020] The appeals court upheld a fair use finding: the new track gave the passage a different message, the amount was reasonable, and there was no evidence of "an active market" for the original.[^law-tushnet-2020][^law-kluwer-2020] The ruling was a non-precedential summary affirmance on unusual facts.[^law-tushnet-2020]

It also predates Andy Warhol Foundation v. Goldsmith (US Supreme Court, 2023), decided seven to two, which held that licensing a Warhol silkscreen of Prince to a magazine shared substantially the same commercial purpose as the photograph it was based on, so the first fair use factor leaned against it.[^law-dorsey-2023] The ruling was limited to that one licensing use.[^law-dorsey-2023] But the opinion says that adding a new meaning or message is not enough on its own, and lists among the would-be fair users this leaves out "a musician who finds it helpful to sample another artist's song to make his own."[^fcl-warhol-opinion] Penn Libraries' copyright adviser reads that as a sign sampling may be treated as a derivative work and argues it is closer to quotation; he expects a chill.[^law-penn-2024]

### Were "Blurred Lines" and the Ed Sheeran case about sampling?

No. Neither involved a sample. They are composition cases about how much of a song's feel can be owned.

In Williams v. Gaye (9th Cir., 2018) the appeals court let stand a 2015 jury verdict that "Blurred Lines" infringed Marvin Gaye's "Got to Give It Up"; a dissenting judge called the result a "dangerous precedent".[^law-musicologize-library][^law-authorsalliance-2025] In Skidmore v. Led Zeppelin (9th Cir., 2020) the full court sided with Led Zeppelin over "Stairway to Heaven" and treated a descending chromatic scale as unprotectable.[^law-musicologize-library][^law-authorsalliance-2025] Ed Sheeran then won twice over "Thinking Out Loud": a unanimous New York jury in 2023, and a Second Circuit ruling in 2024 that the four-chord progression at issue was "too well-explored to meet the originality threshold that copyright law demands."[^law-mcir-griffin][^law-natlawreview-2024] The Supreme Court declined the case in June 2025.[^law-authorsalliance-2025]

The later rulings show US courts pulling back from protecting grooves and common chord patterns.

### What happened in the Kraftwerk sampling case?

Two seconds of metallic percussion have been in court since 1999. Kraftwerk released "Metall auf Metall" in 1977. In 1997 producer Moses Pelham looped a roughly two-second rhythm sequence from it under Sabrina Setlur's "Nur mir".[^law-jonesday-2026][^law-simkins-2026] Two years later Kraftwerk's founders sued in Hamburg.[^fcl-lto-2026]

The case has reached the Court of Justice of the European Union twice. In Pelham I (2019) the court held that a record producer can object to even a very short sample of its recording, unless the sample is used "in a modified form unrecognisable to the ear".[^law-cms-2026][^law-lausen-2026]

Then German law changed. Section 51a of the German Copyright Act, in force since June 7, 2021, allows use for caricature, parody and **pastiche**.[^law-jonesday-2026] In 2022 the Hamburg Higher Regional Court cut the dispute into three time periods and held the sample a permitted pastiche only from the day section 51a took effect.[^law-lausen-2026][^law-simkins-2026] Germany's Federal Court of Justice asked the CJEU what "pastiche" means.[^law-jonesday-2026]

The Grand Chamber answered on April 14, 2026, in Pelham II. Pastiche is a concept of EU law with its own meaning, and it is no catch-all for every creative use of protected material.[^law-eu-helpdesk-2026][^law-simkins-2026] It covers "creations which evoke one or more existing works, while being noticeably different from them" and which use characteristic protected elements in a recognizable artistic or creative dialogue with the source.[^law-cms-2026][^law-simkins-2026] Humor is not required. The user's intent is irrelevant; what counts is whether the dialogue is objectively recognizable. Concealed imitation falls outside.[^law-cms-2026][^law-simkins-2026]

So sampling is capable of being pastiche, with no presumption that it is.[^law-jonesday-2026] The case went back to Germany's Federal Court of Justice to decide whether this particular loop qualifies.[^law-cms-2026] On September 3, 2026 it upheld the Hamburg ruling: the loop has been a permitted pastiche since June 7, 2021.[^fcl-bgh-2026] Kraftwerk's side still holds its win for 2002 to 2021, which Pelham has challenged in the Federal Constitutional Court.[^fcl-lto-2026]

The German ruling answers for one loop, and the two CJEU rulings still leave a gap between them. Pelham I favors the sample nobody can recognize. Pelham II favors the reference everybody can. A recognizable sample that is in no dialogue with its source sits between the two.

### Is sampling legal in the UK?

The UK has no fair use. Under the Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988, sampling without consent is infringement on its face if a "substantial" part is taken, judged case by case and by quality as much as quantity.[^law-wipo-2009] In Produce Records v. BMG (1999), whether a seven-and-a-half-second sample was substantial was held to be a question for trial with musicologists; BMG settled. Ludlow Music v. Robbie Williams (2002) found that a short lyric borrowing still needed clearance.[^law-wipo-2009]

Britain's best-known sampling fights never produced a judgment at all. In 1987, with M/A/R/R/S's "Pump Up the Volume" at number two, Stock Aitken Waterman obtained an injunction over a seven-second vocal sample from their "Roadblock"; distribution stopped for five days until a deal was reached.[^law-sos-2012] The Verve's "Bitter Sweet Symphony" (1997) ended the same way, in an out-of-court settlement that gave ABKCO all of the publishing,[^law-cmu-2019][^fcl-billboard-2019] and when Mick Jagger and Keith Richards agreed in 2019 to hand their share back, the trade press cautioned that the exact effect on ownership was unclear;[^law-cmu-2019][^fcl-twobirds-2019] the money is followed in [/money/](/money/).

Since 2014 the UK has had its own exception for caricature, parody and pastiche, section 30A, which is a form of **fair dealing**: it still asks whether more was taken than needed and whether the use competes with the original.[^law-mishcon-2026] The leading case, Shazam Productions v. Only Fools The Dining Experience (2022), described pastiche as imitating a style or assembling a medley, with a result noticeably different from its sources. The defense failed there, and no UK court has applied section 30A to a music sample.[^law-mishcon-2026] Pelham II does not bind UK courts after Brexit, but lawyers expect it to be persuasive because section 30A came from the same EU provision.[^law-mishcon-2026][^law-simkins-2026]

### Is sampling legal in Japan, Jamaica and Brazil?

Japan gives record producers their own rights over reproduction and distribution, and gives performers moral rights of attribution and integrity.[^law-monolith] In the Jaco Pastorius case (Osaka District Court, 2018), reusing a recording as background music without a license infringed the record producer's reproduction right, and the court asked whether the sound of the original record was identifiable.[^law-monolith]

Jamaica's "version" culture, many vocals over one riddim, grew up under very loose copyright enforcement.[^law-mcleod-dicola] The biggest fight over who owns a riddim has played out in California. Cleveland "Clevie" Browne and the estate of Wycliffe "Steely" Johnson claimed their 1989 instrumental "Fish Market" is the source of reggaeton's dembow rhythm, naming more than 150 artists and between 1,800 and 2,000 songs.[^law-blackamericaweb-2026][^law-dancehallmag] On July 1, 2026 the judge denied summary judgment to both sides and sent the question of whether the rhythm is protectable toward a jury.[^law-blackamericaweb-2026][^fcl-browne-order-2026] On September 1 he reconsidered and granted the defendants summary judgment on that theory: the plaintiffs had not identified a single copyrighted work containing the arrangement they claimed, which they had described across three recordings.[^fcl-browne-order-2026][^law-rollingstone-ca-2026] The order does not decide whether the rhythm is original, and it leaves the remaining claims to be scheduled; press accounts say those include direct samples of the original recording.[^fcl-browne-order-2026][^law-globalnews-2026] No appeal has been reported, though a Jamaican attorney who once represented the pair told the Jamaica Observer the plaintiffs can still bring one.[^fcl-jamaicaobserver-2026]

Brazil's 1998 copyright law has a closed list of exceptions, and Brazilian scholars argue for a looser approach to sampling.[^law-gedai-2021]

### Are old records in the public domain?

In the US, far fewer than people assume. Recordings made before February 15, 1972 had no federal copyright at all until 2018, only a patchwork of state law.[^law-crs-pre1972] The Music Modernization Act, signed October 11, 2018, gave them federal remedies on much the same terms as later recordings.[^law-crs-pre1972] Protection runs 95 years from first publication plus a transition period, and recordings from 1957 to early 1972 are covered until 2067.[^law-crs-pre1972][^law-mma-faq] A 1960s soul or funk break, which covers most of [/breaks/](/breaks/), is inside that window.

The state-law lawsuits that pushed Congress to act were brought against Sirius XM and Pandora by members of the Turtles, the same two musicians who sued De La Soul in 1989.[^law-crs-pre1972]

### How long after release can someone sue over a sample?

US civil copyright claims must be filed within three years of when the claim accrues.[^law-loeb-nealy-2024] Warner Chappell Music v. Nealy (US Supreme Court, 2024) dealt with what happens after that. Sherman Nealy's 1980s label released "Jam the Box"; while he was in prison a former partner licensed it, and it was interpolated in Flo Rida's "In the Ayer". Nealy sued in 2018 over uses going back to 2008.[^law-loeb-nealy-2024]

The Court held that the Copyright Act has no separate three-year cap on damages: a plaintiff with a timely claim is "entitled to damages, no matter when the infringement occurred."[^law-loeb-nealy-2024] It assumed, without deciding, that the **discovery rule** applies, the idea that the clock starts when the owner learns of the infringement. Three dissenting justices said it "almost certainly does not" outside fraud or concealment.[^law-loeb-nealy-2024] In circuits that use the discovery rule, an old uncleared sample found years later can carry damages for the whole period.[^law-loeb-nealy-2024]
