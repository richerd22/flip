## How a flip gets made

**The short version:** A flip starts with a find - a record nobody else has, or a new idea about one everybody has - and ends with that sound doing a job its makers never planned. In between, the producer loops, chops, filters, re-pitches, stretches and layers it, and most of those moves began as workarounds for machines with a few seconds of memory.[^mac-voice-sp1200][^mac-musicradar-tricks] Since about 2018, software has been able to pull the voice, drums or bass back out of a finished mix, which changes what there is to find.[^mac-izotope-rebalance][^mac-musicradar-spleeter]

### What does it mean to flip a sample?

To flip a sample is to change it enough that it does new work. A straight loop of four bars is a sample. The same four bars cut into pieces, re-ordered, slowed, filtered down to the bassline and laid under different drums is a flip. The word covers every decision between hearing a record and finishing a beat.

Ethan Hein's hip-hop glossary gets it into one line: "To creatively repurpose or alter a sample, especially in an unexpected way."[^gfa-hein-glossary]

| Technique | What happens | Associated with |
|---|---|---|
| Looping | A short section repeats | Early samplers, short on memory[^mac-musicradar-tricks] |
| Chopping | A recording is cut up and replayed in a new order | Marley Marl, "The Bridge", 1986[^mac-rtb-ubb] |
| Filtering | The highs are rolled off to leave the bass | Hank Shocklee on the SP-1200[^mac-voice-sp1200] |
| Pitching up | The sample is sped up to fit, voices and all | Kanye West on the ASR-10; early jungle and hardcore[^mac-reverb-ensoniq][^mac-musicradar-tricks] |
| Time-stretch | Length changes, pitch stays | Akai S950; 1990s jungle[^mac-reverb-jungle] |
| Resampling | The sampler's output is recorded back in as a new sample | Public Enemy, Mirage into S900[^mac-reverb-ensoniq] |
| Swing and shifted timing | Hits sit off the even grid by design | Roger Linn's MPC; J Dilla[^mac-attack-linn][^mac-ethanhein-dilla] |
| Stem separation | A finished mix is split back into parts | Software since 2018[^mac-izotope-rebalance] |

### What is crate digging?

**Crate digging** is hunting through records for something to sample.[^mac-okayplayer-sampling] The prize, for a long time, was the **break**: the stretch of a record where the band drops out and the drummer carries on alone.[^mac-rtb-ubb] The most-used ones are in [/breaks/](/breaks/). (How records get made and pressed in the first place is its own story: https://groove.cold.fyi/.)

Digging got a shortcut early. Paul Winley put out "Super Disco Brakes" in 1979, and in the early 1980s Lenny Roberts was pressing bootleg 12-inches under the name "Octopus Breaks".[^mac-rtb-ubb] In 1985 Roberts founded Street Beat Records to do it legitimately, with licensing, and from 1986 to 1991 he and Louis "BreakBeat Lou" Flores compiled 25 volumes of "Ultimate Breaks & Beats".[^mac-rtb-ubb][^mac-wiki-ubb] "Amen, Brother", "Funky Drummer", "Apache" and "Impeach the President" are all on it,[^mac-wiki-ubb] and all in the breaks table at [/crate/](/crate/).

Lou did more than compile. "I extended many drum breaks so that it would be easier for D.J.'s and producers to use them," he has said.[^mac-rtb-ubb] He also slowed "Amen, Brother" from 45 to 33 for the break, and that slowed version is the one on N.W.A.'s "Straight Outta Compton".[^mac-rtb-ubb] The compilation had flipped the record before the producers got to it. Roberts died in 1996.[^mac-rtb-ubb]

### What is library music?

**Library music** is, in Jonny Trunk's words, "non-commercial music made for economic use in film, TV and broadcasting".[^mac-rbma-library] It was never sold in shops. Labels such as KPM, De Wolfe, Bruton, Chappell and Themes in the UK, and Telemusic and others in France, Italy and Germany, pressed it in runs of about 200 to 500 copies.[^mac-rbma-library] When the libraries cleared out their vinyl in the mid-1990s it landed with collectors, and by 2014 some LPs were reported to fetch £1,000 or more.[^mac-rbma-library]

A lot of the British catalog was recorded abroad, and taste had nothing to do with it. From 1965 to 1978 the UK Musicians' Union kept an embargo on library sessions, so publishers booked studios in Germany, Belgium, the Netherlands and Switzerland instead.[^mac-roy-library] It ended with an agreement with Bruton, KPM and Themes on July 1, 1978, which set a fee of £35 for a three-hour session.[^mac-roy-library]

Producers like library records for the playing and for the paperwork: the library owns the whole copyright, so there is one party to deal with when it comes to [/clearing/](/clearing/).[^mac-djbooth-library] Madlib and Freddie Gibbs's "Thuggin'" is built on "Way Star" by Rubba, a library cut, and KPM recordings by Alan Hawkshaw have turned up under JAY-Z and Drake.[^mac-djbooth-library] The modern descendant is Frank Dukes's Kingsway Music Library, new music made specifically to be sampled.[^mac-djbooth-library]

### How do you find out what a song sampled?

The usual first stop is WhoSampled, a database built from user submissions. In November 2025 it listed 1,246,970 songs and 622,026 samples, by its own count.[^mac-ppcland-whosampled] On November 19, 2025 Spotify announced it had bought the site for an undisclosed sum, to feed a feature called SongDNA; the site stays up on its own, with ads and app fees removed.[^mac-ppcland-whosampled]

Digging has also moved online with a license attached. Tracklib, founded by Pär Almqvist and pitched as "digital crate-digging", launched in 2018 selling original recordings at $1.99 a track, with license tiers of $50, $500 and $2,500 depending on how much was sampled, plus a share of revenue.[^mac-okayplayer-sampling] What it charges now is in [/money/](/money/).

### What is chopping?

**Chopping** is cutting a recording into pieces and playing the pieces back in a new order.[^mac-rtb-ubb] The usual origin story is drums: for MC Shan's "The Bridge" in 1986, Marley Marl lifted the kick, the snare and the hi-hat out of "Impeach the President" as separate sounds and programmed his own pattern with them, and he is credited with making the method popular.[^mac-rtb-ubb]

Small memory made choppers of everyone. A machine that holds a few seconds will not take a whole phrase, so the phrase goes in as fragments ([/machines/](/machines/) has the numbers). Ski, who worked on the SP-1200, put it this way: "The limited sampling time made you become more creative."[^mac-voice-sp1200] In early jungle the same idea was applied to whole breakbeats, sliced into hits and re-ordered from a sequencer over MIDI.[^mac-musicradar-tricks]

### Why do old sample loops sound like loops?

Because there was no room to hide them. A **loop** is a section set to repeat, and on early gear the sustained part of a sound had to be a very short one, so the repeat itself was audible and became part of the character.[^mac-musicradar-tricks] **Layering** came from the same shortage: one sound's sharp attack stacked on another's sustain gets a full note out of two tiny samples.[^mac-musicradar-tricks] Stack a clean kick under a dusty one today and you are doing the same thing for different reasons.

### How do producers pull the bassline out of a record?

They filter everything else away. The famous version of the trick was an accident. Working on Public Enemy's "Black Steel in the Hour of Chaos", Hank Shocklee found that pushing a jack only halfway into the SP-1200 "filters the high frequency" and leaves the bass.[^mac-voice-sp1200][^mac-musicradar-tricks] A half-seated cable did the job a low-pass filter does on purpose, and a muffled, bass-only version of somebody else's record became a standard part of the sound.

### Why were sped-up vocals everywhere?

Before time-stretch was any good, the only way to make a sample fit a faster tempo was to play it faster, and faster means higher. That is where the "chipmunk" vocals of early jungle and hardcore come from.[^mac-musicradar-tricks] Kanye West did it on purpose. On turning Chaka Khan's "Through the Fire" into "Through the Wire", he said: "I sample them at regular speed, then speed them up inside the ASR-10."[^mac-reverb-ensoniq]

Time-stretch broke the link between speed and pitch, and its early flaws became a sound of their own. Jungle producers ran breaks such as Amen, Think, Apache and Funky Drummer through the Akai S950's stretch, and used it on vocals for what one history calls an "odd, metallic-sounding timbre"; Amiga computers running OctaMED and Atari ST sequencers did much of the rest.[^mac-reverb-jungle] A 1989 review explains the noise: the S950's stretch is a simplified form of digital harmonising, and its delay setting gives echoes at high values and something like a ring modulator at low ones.[^mac-muzines-s950] The Akai does not get all the credit. In a 2012 Channel 4 documentary, Goldie's stretched breaks are put down to an "H3 Harmonizer", with no Akai in the story.[^mac-trench-goldie]

**Resampling** is the last move in this family: record the sampler's own output back in as a new sample, effects and all. Public Enemy's "Rebel Without a Pause" was first built on an Ensoniq Mirage and then resampled on an Akai S900, and Shocklee felt it lost some edge on the way.[^mac-reverb-ensoniq] One old-school tip is to re-pitch a drum loop and resample it before doing any editing.[^mac-musicradar-tricks]

### Did J Dilla really turn quantize off?

The popular answer is yes: Dilla left his beats "raw and unquantized", played by hand and never corrected.[^mac-brett-linn] Dan Charnas, whose book "Dilla Time" came out in February 2022, says that explanation is wrong.[^mac-pow-charnas]

Charnas sorts rhythm into three kinds. Straight time divides the beat evenly. Swing time divides it into uneven pairs. Dilla time puts straight and swung feels in the same bar at once, with some hits placed ahead of the grid and some behind it.[^mac-ethanhein-dilla] In that account the lurch is designed. The deviations were "intended and meticulously executed", and Charnas points to the MPC's independent swing and shift-timing settings, which Dilla was using from about 1997 or 1998.[^mac-ethanhein-dilla][^mac-pow-charnas] The Smithsonian, announcing the donation of his MPC in 2014, called him "often considered the first" producer to use deliberately imperfect beats.[^mac-smithsonian-dilla]

### What is stem separation?

A **stem** is one part of a mix - vocals, drums, bass, everything else - and **stem separation** is software that splits a finished stereo recording back into those parts.[^mac-ableton-stems] For most of sampling's history that was impossible: a sample came with whatever was playing at that moment, which is why breaks, intros and bare basslines were worth so much.

iZotope's RX 7, in 2018, shipped a tool called Music Rebalance that used separate neural networks for vocals, bass and percussion, with a fourth bucket for the rest. Each network estimates a **mask**, a map of where its instrument sits at every moment and frequency, and it learned to do that from mixes paired with their isolated parts.[^mac-izotope-rebalance] In November 2019 Deezer's research team released Spleeter, free and open source under an MIT license, with a claimed speed of about 100 times faster than real time on a graphics card.[^mac-musicradar-spleeter][^mac-cdm-spleeter] It ran from the command line only, and Deezer did not claim to have solved the problem.[^mac-musicradar-spleeter] CDM's verdict at the time was that it worked "not perfectly, but well enough to be legitimately promising".[^mac-cdm-spleeter]

Then it became a menu item. By one software vendor's account, FL Studio added stem separation in 2023 and Logic Pro in early 2024, with Studio One Pro 7 after them; Serato Sample splits a track into vocal, melodic, bass and drum stems.[^mac-zplane-stems] Ableton Live 12.3 went into public beta on September 2, 2025 with four stems, processed on the user's own computer, in the Suite edition only, on algorithms from Music AI, the company behind Moises.[^mac-musicradar-live123][^mac-ableton-stems][^mac-attack-live123] MusicRadar framed it as Ableton catching up with Serato Sample, FL Studio, Logic Pro and RipX.[^mac-musicradar-live123]

A vocal buried under a full band is now a candidate, and so is a drum pattern that never gets a bar to itself. The most public demonstration came from the Beatles. For the 2021 documentary "The Beatles: Get Back", Peter Jackson's team built a system called MAL, and it was later used on a 1970s home cassette of John Lennon singing at a piano, a demo shelved in the mid-1990s because voice and piano shared one track.[^mac-smithsonianmag-nowandthen] Paul McCartney said: "There it was, John's voice, crystal clear."[^mac-smithsonianmag-nowandthen] The software cleaned an existing recording and generated nothing; "Now and Then" came out in 2023 and went on to win the Grammy for Best Rock Performance, reported as the first winner made with AI assistance.[^mac-smithsonianmag-nowandthen][^mac-heise-grammy]
