## AI, stems and what comes next

**The short version:** The AI tool most producers use is the one that takes records apart. Song generators are a minority habit, but one generated track, "BBL Drizzy," was sampled on a Drake record in 2024 and cleared by paying only the human who wrote the words. The major labels sued the two biggest generators in 2024; three of those disputes settled into licensing deals in late 2025, and in 2026 the holdouts filed second, far larger suits. As of November 2026 no US court had ruled on whether training a music model on recordings is fair use.

### How is AI changing sampling?

**Stem separation** software splits a finished mix back into vocals, drums, bass and the rest; how it works is at [/making-a-flip/](/making-a-flip/). In Tracklib's 2024 survey of 1,107 producers, 25% said they used AI at all, and of those 73.9% used it for stem separation; 82.2% of the non-users gave artistic reasons for staying away.[^now-mbw-tracklib-2024] A 2026 Water & Music report for Moises, with about 80% of respondents recruited from Moises' own users, found 67% of 1,525 musicians had used AI, with stem separation and vocal isolation the top task at 71%.[^now-moises-2026] Google searches for "acapella extractor" rose 800% in the five years to 2022, and by Tracklib's 2022 report its tracks offered with stems were nine times more likely to be sampled.[^now-tracklib-2022]

### Do producers use AI to generate samples?

Some do. In the Tracklib survey 21.2% of AI users generated song elements with it and 3% made entire songs.[^now-mbw-tracklib-2024] The Moises report put full-song generation from a prompt at 24% of AI users, and listed the two leading worries as authenticity (58%) and copyright or licensing (55%).[^now-moises-2026] Neither survey is a random sample of working musicians, and both come from companies that sell to them.

The house producer Todd Edwards has described his method in Suno Studio: "make my own disco or soft rock songs, then take that out, cut them up and use them as samples."[^gfb-musicradar-edwards-2026] Young Guru, Jay-Z's longtime engineer, guessed in March 2026 that "more than half" of sample-based hip-hop was being built on AI-made funk and soul. That is one man's estimate, from someone who says he still pays for samples himself.[^gfb-rollingstone-ai-2026]

### Can you sample an AI-generated song?

One test case exists, and it started as a joke. On April 14, 2024 the comedian Willonius Hatcher, who performs as King Willonius, wrote a parody lyric and used the generator Udio to produce the vocal, melody and backing of a fake vintage soul song, "BBL Drizzy." On May 5 Metro Boomin posted a beat that sampled it.[^now-billboard-bbl-2024] Hatcher was firm about authorship: "I think it's a misconception that people think AI wrote 'BBL Drizzy.'"[^now-billboard-bbl-2024]

At the end of May, Drake and Sexyy Red's "U My Everything" sampled it, in what Billboard described as the first official release to use an AI-generated sample.[^now-complex-bbl-2024] Hatcher's lawyer, Donald Woodard, explained how it was cleared: "You only need to clear the human side of it, which is the publishing." He treated the machine-made recording as public domain.[^now-complex-bbl-2024] Hatcher got an advance and a share of the publishing, amount undisclosed.[^now-fortune-bbl-2024] Metro Boomin sought no ownership or royalties; his lawyer, Uwonda Carter, called the clearance question "the Wild, Wild West".[^now-complex-bbl-2024]

That is one deal between willing parties. Vanderbilt law professor Joseph Fishman told Fortune that a song created entirely by AI would be public domain and free to sample, a scholar's reading and no court's.[^now-fortune-bbl-2024] Music attorney Todd Rubenstein pointed somewhere less glamorous: "You really need to know what the terms of service are whenever you use an AI model."[^now-billboard-bbl-2024] The US Copyright Office published a report on the copyrightability of AI output on January 29, 2025.[^now-copyright-office-ai]

### What happened to the AI Drake song?

In April 2023 a track called "Heart on My Sleeve," posted by someone using the name Ghostwriter, used AI imitations of Drake's and The Weeknd's voices. By April 17 it had passed 250,000 Spotify streams and 10 million TikTok views.[^now-techcrunch-heart-2023] By April 18 it was gone from Apple Music, Spotify, Deezer and Tidal, after 629,439 Spotify streams, worth about $1,888 at $0.003 a stream by the BBC's estimate. Universal called training generative AI on its artists' music "a violation of copyright law".[^now-bbc-heart-2023]

A cloned voice is not a sample. No audio is lifted, so the dispute moves from copyright in a recording to a person's right in their own voice. In the US Congress, a revised NO FAKES Act was introduced on May 20, 2026. It would create liability for distributing unauthorized **digital replicas** of a voice or likeness and for platforms that knowingly host them, with a counter-notice procedure and exemptions for libraries and archives. Its sponsors listed the RIAA, the Recording Academy, the three majors, YouTube, TikTok and OpenAI as supporters. On that date it was a bill awaiting action in the Senate Judiciary Committee.[^now-blackburn-nofakes-2026]

### Is AI music trained on copyrighted songs?

The labels say so, and that is what the lawsuits are about. In 2024 the RIAA, acting for the three major label groups, sued Suno in federal court in Massachusetts and Udio in New York, alleging mass infringement of sound recordings in training.[^now-dmn-sony-dec-2025]

| Dispute | Status | As of |
|---|---|---|
| Universal v. Udio | Settled, with licenses for recordings and publishing and a joint subscription platform planned for 2026; amounts undisclosed | Announced October 29, 2025[^now-mbw-udio-2025][^now-cmu-udio-2025] |
| Warner v. Udio | Settled, with a license for a 2026 platform using participating artists' voices and compositions; amounts undisclosed | November 19, 2025[^gfb-mbw-wmg-udio-2025][^now-decrypt-wmg-udio-2025] |
| Warner v. Suno | Settled, terms private; Suno to launch licensed models and retire its current ones, limit downloads to paying users, and take over Songkick from Warner | November 25, 2025[^now-mbw-suno-2025][^now-eweek-suno-2025] |
| Universal and Sony v. Suno | In litigation, two suits: the 2024 case over 560 recordings and a second over 60,202; Suno's request to pause the second undecided | November 16, 2026[^gfb-mbw-suno-second-2026][^gfb-dmn-suno-nov-2026][^now-aimusicpreneur-2026] |
| Sony v. Udio | In litigation, two suits: the 2024 case over 333 recordings and a second over 30,117, which Udio has answered | September 1, 2026[^gfb-mbw-udio-answer-2026][^now-dmn-suno-apr-2026] |

Under the first deal, Udio's outputs were to stay inside a "walled garden" until the licensed service arrived.[^now-mbw-udio-2025] The same month as its Warner settlement, Suno was reported to have raised $250 million at a $2.45 billion valuation.[^now-eweek-suno-2025]

The holdouts kept going. On April 6, 2026 a magistrate judge refused Universal and Sony access to the terms of the Warner-Suno deal, which Suno had argued would hand its opponents "a strategic blueprint".[^now-mbw-suno-may-2026] In the New York case, Judge Alvin Hellerstein declined to dismiss Sony's claim that Udio got around YouTube's "rolling cipher" copy protection, as Digital Music News reported on April 24, 2026.[^now-dmn-suno-apr-2026] On June 29 the court refused to let Sony add more than 30,000 recordings to that case, saying there was "no requirement that it be done in [that] lawsuit," so on July 20 Sony filed a second one.[^gfb-mbw-sony-udio-second-2026] Udio's answer, filed August 27, calls its training "quintessential fair use".[^gfb-mbw-udio-answer-2026]

The Suno fight ran the same way. On August 18, 2026 Judge F. Dennis Saylor IV declined to add 61,026 recordings to the first case and said the labels could file them separately. Universal and Sony did, on September 18, in a second suit over 60,202 recordings that takes aim at Suno's v6 models.[^gfb-mbw-suno-second-2026] Suno asked in late October to pause the new case until the first is resolved. The labels opposed a full pause, telling the court the older case "could realistically extend into 2028."[^gfb-dmn-suno-nov-2026][^gfb-dmn-subpoena-nov-2026] One site that follows the docket puts summary judgment motions in the first case at June 22, 2027, with no trial date.[^now-aimusicpreneur-2026]

Suno released its v6 models on September 9, 2026 and says they were built with Warner, BMG and Believe; Universal and Sony's second complaint calls v6 "the fruit of the same poisoned tree".[^gfb-mbw-suno-second-2026] Udio's replacement, a fan app reported in May 2026 to be called Starstruck and designed so that nothing made in it can be exported, had not launched by November 17, 2026, when the trade press still listed it among products expected soon.[^gfb-mbw-starstruck-2026][^gfb-dmn-subpoena-nov-2026]

A settlement is a private deal. It sets no precedent and admits nothing.

### What did the German court decide about Suno?

A court has ruled against a music generator, in Munich. GEMA, the German authors' society, sued Suno in January 2025 over six compositions, among them "Daddy Cool," "Rasputin," "Big in Japan," "Forever Young" and the refrain of "Mambo No. 5." On July 31, 2026 the Munich Regional Court I ruled largely for GEMA.[^now-simmons-gema-2026][^now-reedsmith-gema-2026] It found the works were memorized inside the model, a reproduction the EU's text-and-data-mining exception did not cover, and reproduced in outputs with "no doubt as to the recognisability of the original musical works." It held Suno responsible, not its users, and ordered an injunction, disclosure and damages still to be calculated.[^now-simmons-gema-2026] The judgment is not final: Suno appealed, and the case was before the Munich Higher Regional Court as of September 30, 2026.[^gfb-lausen-gema-2026][^now-cms-gema]

The case concerns compositions, because GEMA represents songwriters and publishers, and says nothing about recordings. It is also a first-instance German ruling. The same court found against OpenAI on November 11, 2025 over memorized song lyrics, and that decision was under appeal too.[^now-cms-gema]

### What has the US Copyright Office said about AI training?

It has published a three-part report and decided nothing, because it cannot. Part 3, on generative AI training, appeared in a pre-publication version on May 9, 2025.[^now-copyright-office-ai] In one law firm's summary, it says training implicates the reproduction right, that fair use turns on the facts of each case, and that commercial use of vast troves of works to make competing content, especially from illegal access, "goes beyond established fair use boundaries." It prefers voluntary licensing to a compulsory scheme and has no binding force.[^now-crowell-2025]

One question sits right on top of sampling law. US law has long let anyone make an independent soundalike of a recording.[^now-payforplay-ch40] Whether a generated track that resembles a record, while copying none of its audio, touches the recording owner's rights at all is open. A 2026 Dubspot explainer for producers put it flatly: "no clear legal precedent exists yet."[^now-dubspot-2026]

### Can streaming services detect AI music?

Deezer says it can, for the two best-known generators. Its detector identifies output from Suno and Udio models, and by the company's own figures fully AI-generated uploads climbed from about 10,000 a day in January 2025, 10% of everything delivered, to about 90,000 a day in June 2026, more than half of daily uploads.[^now-techcrunch-deezer-2026] Deezer began labeling AI tracks in 2025, offered the technology to other platforms in January 2026, and in June 2026 released a tool that scans Apple Music and Spotify playlists. Its policy is to remove AI tracks that go unstreamed for six months or are tied to fraudulent streams.[^now-techcrunch-deezer-2026]

Other services drew different lines, as of July 2026: Bandcamp bans AI-generated tracks, Tidal cut monetization for AI music, Apple Music runs voluntary tagging and Spotify has a disclosure policy.[^now-techcrunch-deezer-2026]

### Can an AI song win a Grammy?

Not by itself. The Recording Academy's June 2023 protocols say: "A work that contains no human authorship is not eligible in any category." They add that the human contribution "must be meaningful."[^now-ap-grammys-2023] A February 2026 analysis found the guidelines rest largely on an honor system and leave cases such as AI-written chord progressions or cloned voices unclear.[^now-techrepublic-grammys-2026]

### Is sampling theft or homage?

Both words have been in use since the 1980s. In 1987 Pete Waterman, whose record was sampled on M|A|R|R|S' "Pump Up the Volume," called it "wholesale theft," his description and no court's.[^gfb-sos-marrs-2012][^now-wp-pump] Chopstix, who built Burna Boy's "Last Last" around a Toni Braxton record in 2022, sees a guest spot: "When a song is sampled and done right, it is indirectly a feature or collaboration."[^now-okayafrica-2022] A Mixmag writer tracing Bollywood samples in Western dance music ([/world/](/world/) has the records) asked, "as long as the source material is given due credit, what's not to love?"[^now-mixmag-2023]

AI has restarted the argument one level down. Lawyers on the "BBL Drizzy" deal saw a recording with no owner to ask.[^now-complex-bbl-2024] The labels' lawsuits say the tool that made such a recording was itself built from theirs.[^now-dmn-sony-dec-2025] In Europe, one law firm's reading of the EU's 2026 sampling judgment, covered at [/law/](/law/), is that prompting "in the style of" an artist and publishing the first result is unlikely to count as pastiche.[^now-mishcon-2026]

### Do the original players get credit and pay?

Often not. The Winstons' "Amen, Brother" is the most-sampled recording WhoSampled lists,[^gfb-routenote-most-sampled-2025][^now-wp-whosampled] and in NPR's words the band "never saw a cent from it".[^now-npr-amen-2017] The fundraiser that came later is at [/breaks/](/breaks/). Even when a sample is paid for, the check may not reach a musician. Hank Shocklee of Public Enemy's production team said of fees for recordings: "the money just goes to the record company."[^now-littlevillage-2011]

There is a case that sampling pays the past. Burna Boy said in 2022 that Toni Braxton takes about 60% of "Last Last,"[^now-okayafrica-2022] and journalist Joey Akan argues that such records put new money in older artists' pockets.[^now-africanarguments-2024]

### Did clearance kill sample-heavy albums?

The people who made them say yes. Chuck D: "So we had to change our whole style ... by 1991." Shocklee heard the result everywhere: "If you notice that by the early 1990s, the sound has gotten a lot softer."[^now-littlevillage-2011] The records in question were dense. Estimates for the Beastie Boys' "Paul's Boutique" (1989) run from 100 to 300 samples, with Wikipedia's own tally at 105 songs, and engineer Mario Caldato Jr. recalled the band spending "over $250,000 for sample clearances" in 1989 dollars.[^now-popmatters-creative][^now-wp-pauls-boutique]

The best-known price tag comes from the 2011 book "Creative License" by Kembrew McLeod and Peter DiCola. DiCola estimated that if every sample on "Paul's Boutique" were licensed at modern rates, Capitol would lose about $20 million on roughly 2.5 million copies, a hypothetical figure that comes by way of Techdirt's summary.[^now-techdirt-mcleod] The book also quotes journalist Harry Allen's back-of-envelope figure that early Public Enemy albums would have needed to retail at "$159 each" to cover royalties, and EPMD's Erick Sermon recalling asks of $10,000 and $5,000 for single samples in the early 1990s.[^now-popmatters-creative]

The counter-evidence is that sampling never left the charts. Tracklib, which sells licensed samples, counted them in 17% of 2022's Hot 100 hits and found that about a fifth of those leaned on the source so heavily they approached a remake.[^now-tracklib-2022] One big, fully credited sample had replaced a hundred small ones.
