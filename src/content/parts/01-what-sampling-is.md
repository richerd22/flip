## What sampling is, and what it is not

**The short version:** A sample is a piece of an existing recording placed inside a new one. Play the same part again with your own hands and it becomes an interpolation; re-record the whole song and it is a cover; rework the original recording and it is a remix. The distinctions sound fussy until money is involved, because each one calls on a different set of owners.

### What is sampling in music?

A **sample** is part of an existing recording placed in a new work.[^his-usco-sampling] The word that matters is "recording". If the sound coming out of the speaker was captured at somebody else's session, with somebody else's drummer in somebody else's room, it is a sample, whether it lasts one snare hit or four bars.

The name arrived with the machine. Peter Vogel and Kim Ryrie, the two Australians behind the Fairlight CMI of 1979, are credited with calling the process "sampling".[^his-roli-2026] The practice is older than the name. Pierre Schaeffer was building pieces out of recordings of trains in Paris in 1948,[^his-wp-etudes] and Kool Herc was stretching drum breaks across two copies of the same record in the Bronx in 1973.[^his-npr-2023] The long version is at [/history/](/history/).

It is also common. Tracklib, a company that sells sample licenses and so has an interest in the answer, counted samples or interpolations in 17% of the songs on the Billboard Hot 100 in 2022.[^his-tracklib-2022]

### What is the difference between a sample and an interpolation?

The difference is who is playing. An **interpolation** takes part of an existing song and records it again from scratch, with none of the original audio.[^his-usco-sampling] Studios call the result a **replay**.

Two of the records most often filed under "early sampling" are mostly replays. No Kraftwerk recording is on "Planet Rock" (1982): the melody and the beat were copied by hand.[^his-sos-planetrock] The groove on the Sugarhill Gang's "Rapper's Delight" (1979) is a studio band playing Chic's "Good Times", though Nile Rodgers of Chic has said the strings came straight off his record.[^his-wp-rappers][^gfa-freshair-rodgers]

The distinction has a price tag. A sample uses the recording and the song inside it. A replay uses only the song, and the US Copyright Office says an interpolation needs a license from the song's owner and no permission from the recording's owner, "regardless of how similar the new recording may be to an old recording".[^his-usco-sampling] That is one permission fewer, which is not the same as none. Tracklib's version: "an interpolation is not a cover version so interpolations do need clearance."[^his-tracklib-myths]

### Is a remix the same as a sample?

No, though they are cousins. A **cover** is a new recording of an existing song that keeps its basic melody and character; in the US it travels on a statutory license at a set rate, and that license grants nothing in anybody's recording.[^his-usco-sampling] A **remix** is an existing recording with sounds added, changed or taken away, so the owner of that recording is part of the conversation.[^his-usco-sampling] A **mashup** combines two or more sampled recordings into one work.[^his-usco-sampling]

Jamaica had the remix first. By 1970 nearly every single pressed there carried a "version" on the B-side: the same recording with the vocal pulled out, the mix that grew into dub.[^his-udiscover-dub] The best-known mashup is Danger Mouse's "The Grey Album" (2004), Jay-Z's a cappellas laid over pieces of the Beatles' "White Album".[^his-voice-grey]

| Term | What gets reused | Whose playing you hear |
|---|---|---|
| Sample | A piece of a recording | The original musicians |
| Interpolation (replay) | A piece of a song, performed again | New musicians |
| Cover | A whole song, performed again | New musicians |
| Remix | One recording, reworked | The original musicians, rearranged |
| Mashup | Two or more recordings, combined | Everybody's originals at once |

### What do loop, chop and flip mean?

- A **loop** is a section of a record set to repeat. On early samplers, with a few seconds of memory, the audible repeat became part of the sound.[^his-musicradar-tricks]
- A **chop** is a recording cut into pieces and played back in a new order. The standard example is Marley Marl lifting the kick, snare and hi-hat out of "Impeach the President" for MC Shan's "The Bridge" (1986).[^his-rtb-ubb]
- A **flip** is a sample changed enough to do a job its makers never planned. Four bars looped is a sample; the same four bars cut up, slowed, filtered and laid under different drums is a flip.

The moves themselves are at [/making-a-flip/](/making-a-flip/).

### Do you need permission to sample a song?

Every recorded song is two separate works, the composition and the sound recording, and a sample copies both, so it usually involves two owners: whoever holds the recording (the master) and whoever holds the song (the publishing).[^his-circ56a][^his-spotify-2019] The popular belief in a safe number of seconds has no law behind it - "There is no time limit or maximum length to avoid clearance issues," as Tracklib puts it[^his-tracklib-myths] - and the courts that have ruled are at [/law/](/law/), the paperwork and the prices at [/clearing/](/clearing/).

### Why do producers sample instead of playing it themselves?

Because a recording holds things a new performance cannot: one room, one drummer's hands on one afternoon, the wear on the copy it came from. Marley Marl has said he found this out by accident in the early 1980s, when a snare he caught by mistake sounded better than the one in his drum machine.[^gfa-npr-marley-2013][^his-wp-marley][^his-fact-samplers] DJ Muggs of Cypress Hill describes what followed: "Once Marley Marl started sampling, it really flipped the whole rap game on its head and created this new artform."[^his-guardian-2023]

The artistic case has been made in writing since 1985, when the Canadian composer John Oswald argued that recordings should be quotable the way books are: "Without a quotation system, well-intended correspondences cannot be distinguished from plagiarism and fraud."[^his-vice-oswald] Producers add a practical point, that the limits of the machines did some of the composing. Ski, on the 10 seconds of memory in an SP-1200: "The limited sampling time made you become more creative."[^his-voice-sp1200]

The case against is as old. The first US court to rule on a sample, in Grand Upright Music v. Warner Bros. Records (1991), opened its opinion with "Thou shalt not steal."[^his-legal500-biz] Pete Waterman, whose production team obtained an injunction in 1987 over seven seconds of their record inside "Pump Up the Volume", called the practice "wholesale theft".[^his-sos-marrs] Record companies tend to put it more coolly. When EMI moved against "The Grey Album" in 2004, its spokeswoman said only that "there is a well-established market for licensing samples".[^his-voice-grey]

A third complaint comes from the people on the old records. Gregory Coleman, who drummed the Amen break on the Winstons' "Amen, Brother" (1969), died in 2006 without receiving royalties for it.[^gfa-theweek-amen-2015][^his-wp-amen] Who gets paid, and who never did, is the subject of [/money/](/money/).

### How can you tell what you are hearing?

| If you hear... | It is probably a... | For example |
|---|---|---|
| The old record itself, surface noise included, repeating under new drums | Sample, looped | The Turtles' "You Showed Me", 12 seconds of it, on De La Soul's "Transmitting Live from Mars"[^his-briffa-delasoul] |
| A melody or bassline you know, played by somebody else | Interpolation | The "Good Times" bassline under "Rapper's Delight"[^his-wp-rappers][^gfa-freshair-rodgers] |
| A drum fill you have heard on a hundred records, sped up and rearranged | Chopped break | The Amen break in jungle[^his-mixmag-amen] |
| A soul singer pitched up until the voice squeaks | Sped-up sample ("chipmunk soul") | The Diplomats' "Dipset Anthem" (2003)[^his-rbma-chipmunk] |
| One song's vocal over another song's music | Mashup | "The Grey Album"[^his-voice-grey] |
| The vocal you know, on a beat you do not | Remix | A Jamaican "version" with a deejay toasting over it[^his-wp-dub] |
| The whole song, start to finish, by a different act | Cover | Any wedding band |
| A sampled-sounding riff that turns up in no database | Possibly original | Guy-Manuel de Homem-Christo has estimated that Daft Punk played half of what sounds sampled on "Discovery" (2001)[^his-wikiwand-discovery] |
