## Who gets paid

**The short version:** Sample money follows paperwork. It goes to whoever owns the recording and whoever owns the song, which is often a label, a publisher or a fund that bought the catalog, and often not the musician you can hear. The drummer on the Amen break never received a royalty for it. A newer economy sells sounds that are cleared in advance - Splice, Tracklib, composer libraries - and each comes with fine print about what "royalty-free" covers.

### Where does the money from a sample go?

When Beyoncé's "Energy" came out in 2022 with an interpolation of Kelis's 2003 hit "Milkshake," the credits went to Pharrell Williams and Chad Hugo, who wrote it. Kelis is not a credited writer of her own best-known song. She objected publicly that nobody had told her, and within days the interpolation was gone and Williams and Hugo were off the credits.[^mon-grio-energy-2022] Permission and payment run to the names on the copyright, and the voice on the record may not be one of them.

A [clearance](/clearing/) deal pays a fee, a share of the new song to the sampled song's writers and publishers, and a percentage of the new recording's revenue to whoever owns the sampled master.[^mon-performer-2018] The shares vary enormously. Paul Anka, offered a small piece of Doja Cat's "Freak" in 2021, shrugged: "I'm not going to fight over 3 percent. Take it."[^mon-rs-noshelf-2021]

Increasingly the owner is an investor. Primary Wave has operated since 2006 and grew fast after a reported $300 million investment in 2016 from firms including BlackRock.[^mon-fader-vc-2023] Hipgnosis bought the Rick James catalog and offered stems to producers, which led to Nicki Minaj's "Super Freaky Girl" in 2022. Primary Wave bought a share of Rick Astley's publishing and then pitched the idea that became Yung Gravy's "Betty (Get Money)" the same year.[^mon-fader-vc-2023] A song that lands inside a new hit earns again. Ava Max's "Kings and Queens" (2020) credits Desmond Child for an interpolated chorus melody, which gave that melody its first platinum certification 35 years after he first used it.[^mon-rs-noshelf-2021]

Not everyone reads that as good news for musicians. The FADER's column on the catalog funds argues the negotiations "tend to benefit executives and middlemen more than the artists."[^mon-fader-vc-2023] Credit lines are getting longer either way. In 2025, 44% of Billboard Hot 100 top 10 songs had five or more credited writers, up from 37% in 2024, by one analyst's count, though the report does not say how many of those names are interpolation credits.[^mon-hsd-2025]

### Did the Amen break drummer get paid?

No. Gregory "G.C." Coleman played [the break](/breaks/) on the Winstons' "Amen, Brother" in 1969, and the bandleader, Richard L. Spencer, held the copyright. Neither received royalties from its use on other people's records.[^mon-mixmag-amen-2015][^mon-wiki-amen] Spencer learned about the sampling in 1996, by which point the three-year limitation period had run out. Coleman died in 2006.[^gfa-theweek-amen-2015][^mon-wiki-amen]

In 2015 two British DJs, Martyn Webster and Steve Theobald, organized a GoFundMe that paid Spencer.[^mon-mixmag-amen-2015][^gfa-theweek-amen-2015] Mixmag put the total at £24,000.[^mon-mixmag-amen-2015] The BBC figure, as relayed by Wikipedia, is "more than £18,000," about $26,000, possibly an earlier count.[^mon-wiki-amen] It was a collection from fans, and no royalty came with it.

### Does George Clinton own the Parliament-Funkadelic samples?

Mostly not, by the court record so far. Bridgeport Music, of [Bridgeport v. Dimension Films](/law/), owns the rights to about 170 songs written by Clinton and his bandmates, as NPR reported in 2012. Bridgeport says Clinton signed them over in 1982 and 1983; Clinton disputes the signature.[^mon-npr-clinton-2012] In 2001 a federal court in Tallahassee ruled that Clinton did not own the publishing in songs written between 1976 and 1983. In 2005 a federal judge in Los Angeles returned the masters of four Funkadelic albums to him, and Bridgeport kept the publishing.[^mon-npr-clinton-2012] Bridgeport's attorney, Richard Busch, told NPR: "to the best of my knowledge, Bridgeport has prevailed each time."[^mon-npr-clinton-2012]

### Why did The Verve lose the royalties to "Bitter Sweet Symphony"?

Half the paperwork was done. "Bitter Sweet Symphony" (1997) uses a few seconds of the Andrew Oldham Orchestra's 1965 recording of the Rolling Stones' "The Last Time." According to the band's manager, John Kennedy, Decca cleared the recording; the publishing - the Jagger-Richards song, administered by ABKCO - was sought late, after copies had been pressed.[^mon-billboard-bss-2019]

ABKCO ended up with 100% of the publishing, including Richard Ashcroft's own writer share, and the credit became Jagger/Richards/Ashcroft. Billboard describes a dispute settled out of court; Rolling Stone describes a lawsuit from ABKCO's Allen Klein that resulted in Ashcroft handing over the royalties.[^mon-billboard-bss-2019][^mon-rs-bss-2019] Ashcroft's only publishing payment was a reported $1,000, as part of the settlement. By 2019 Billboard estimated the song had earned almost $5 million in publishing.[^mon-billboard-bss-2019]

In April 2019 Mick Jagger and Keith Richards signed their share over to Ashcroft and dropped their credit. He announced it at the Ivor Novello Awards on May 23, 2019, calling it "a truly kind and magnanimous thing for them to do."[^mon-rs-bss-2019] A month later ABKCO was still the song's sole publisher.[^mon-billboard-bss-2019]

### How much does Sting make from "I'll Be Missing You"?

The figure everyone quotes is Sting's own. Puff Daddy's 1997 single used "Every Breath You Take" without clearing it first, and in a 2018 Breakfast Club interview Sting said Combs had agreed to pay him $2,000 a day. In April 2023 Combs replied "5K a day" on social media, then said he was joking.[^mon-mbw-sting-2023]

Sting has said the proceeds put "a couple of my kids through college."[^mon-afrotech-sting-2023] One outlet says he was awarded 100% of the royalties through a lawsuit; that is unconfirmed, and Music Business Worldwide's account of the same story mentions no suit.[^mon-afrotech-sting-2023][^mon-mbw-sting-2023] Universal Music Publishing Group bought his catalog in 2022.[^mon-mbw-sting-2023] Sting's "Shape of My Heart" turned up again under Juice WRLD's "Lucid Dreams" in 2018, where his share of the publishing has been reported, secondhand, as 85%.[^mon-symphonic-2026][^mon-mbw-sting-2023]

### Why did Olivia Rodrigo add songwriters to "Sour"?

Three songs on "Sour" gained writers in 2021. Taylor Swift and Jack Antonoff were credited on "1 Step Forward, 3 Steps Back" in May, for an interpolation of "New Year's Day." Swift, Antonoff and St. Vincent were added to "Deja Vu" in July. Hayley Williams and Josh Farro of Paramore were added to "Good 4 U" in August, three months after it came out.[^mon-rs-rodrigo-2021][^mon-dmn-rodrigo-2021]

Billboard's 2021 estimates, relayed by Rolling Stone, were at least $325,000 to Swift's side from "Deja Vu," and up to $1.2 million for the Paramore writers from "Good 4 U."[^mon-rs-noshelf-2021] A 50% share for the Paramore writers is widely repeated online; it is unconfirmed.

### Why was De La Soul not on Spotify?

Because the first six albums had never been legally available in digital form at all, and getting them there meant going back to the owners of everything on them.[^mon-okp-dela-2023] Tommy Boy first tried to clear the catalog in-house and then hired DMG Clearances. Owners were reluctant to step into the group's dispute with the label, some wanted back payment, and the effort stalled.[^mon-okp-dela-2023]

Reservoir Media then bought the catalog and worked with the group. Deborah Mannis-Gardner started over in January 2022 and said, "It actually took one year." Streaming was announced in January 2023 and began on March 3, 2023.[^mon-okp-dela-2023] On a handful of songs the release used replays and interpolations in place of the original samples - for cost, for fear of a refusal, or to keep some of the revenue with the group.[^mon-okp-dela-2023]

Mannis-Gardner contrasts that with "Paul's Boutique": Capitol/Universal and the Beastie Boys took the risk of putting their album on streaming services, and De La Soul's rights holders did not.[^mon-okp-dela-2023]

### Which other albums were held up by samples?

- **Chance the Rapper.** "10 Day" (2012) and "Acid Rap" (2013) reached streaming on June 28, 2019. "Juice" was left off "Acid Rap" because a sample of Donny Hathaway's recording of John Lennon's "Jealous Guy" was not approved in time. A 30-second spoken placeholder took its slot, with proceeds going to his nonprofit. Chance: "I couldn't get it cleared in time."[^mon-complex-chance-2019]
- **Mac Miller.** The 2014 mixtape "Faces" arrived on streaming on October 15, 2021, seven years late, held back by sample clearance.[^mon-targum-faces-2021]
- **Kendrick Lamar.** Mannis-Gardner says some tracks with uncleared samples were pulled from "To Pimp a Butterfly" before it came out.[^mon-okp-dmg-2019]

### Are Splice samples really royalty-free?

Yes, in the sense Splice defines, and that sense is narrower than "yours." Its licensing FAQ says every downloaded sound comes with a **royalty-free**, non-exclusive, perpetual license that survives canceling the subscription, that no credit to Splice or the sample's creator is required, and that it places no restriction on sync use.[^mon-splice-faq] By the same terms the finished track belongs to the person who made it and the sound does not: "you do not own exclusive rights to the actual Sounds."[^mon-splice-faq]

The license stops short of several things. It does not let a buyer pass the sounds on as samples or loops, sublicense them in isolation even after modifying them, put them in a competing pack, use the creator's name or likeness without written permission, or train AI on them.[^mon-splice-faq] And because thousands of people hold the same loop, Splice says it "can't guarantee" a track will not be flagged by Content ID; it issues a certified license, covering up to 25 samples, for disputing a claim.[^mon-splice-faq]

The company behind that FAQ was founded in 2013 and is large. In 2025, sources told the Financial Times it had about 600,000 paying subscribers and more than $100 million in annual revenue. By its own count Splice had nearly 350 million sample downloads in 2024, and it was reported to be valued at nearly $500 million in 2021.[^mon-mbw-splice-2025] In April 2025 it bought Spitfire Audio for an undisclosed sum the FT put at about $50 million.[^mon-mbw-splice-2025] On April 15, 2026, it announced AI tools built on a catalog of more than 3 million licensed samples, with a payment to the original creator each time a sound is used as a source and each time a variation is downloaded.[^mon-mbw-splice-ai-2026]

### How is Tracklib different from Splice?

Splice sells sounds made to be used. Tracklib sells pieces of real records with the paperwork attached. The Swedish service launched in April 2018 with original recordings at $1.99 a track, a release license of $50 to $2,500, and a revenue share of 2% to 50% depending on how long the sample runs, by press accounts of the launch.[^mon-ddt-tracklib-2018] It raised a reported $1.7 million in 2019 in a round led by Sony Innovation Fund and WndrCo, and moved to subscriptions in May 2020 at $5.99, $13.99 and $29.99 a month.[^mon-hypebot-tracklib-2019][^mon-musicradar-tracklib-2020]

Today its licensing page bundles "Sample clearance with no upfront fee" into its Create plan in exchange for "a small percentage of any potential income," without stating the percentage, and describes its own loops as "100% royalty-free."[^mon-tracklib-licensing] In February 2025 it added a model for labels: a lower record royalty in exchange for a higher upfront fee, so the sample deal fits an existing artist contract.[^mon-tracklib-bespoke-2025]

### Who makes the loops?

Frank Dukes (Adam Feeney) started Kingsway Music Library in 2011 because clearing samples frustrated him. His answer was to write original compositions meant to be sampled. Boi-1da used one on Drake's "0 to 100" in 2014, which led to Feeney's publishing deal and the first real money he made from music.[^mon-sos-dukes-2022] Kingsway now lists more than 100 volumes, with contributors including Louis Bell and Allen Ritter, and claims songs built on its material have passed 10 billion streams.[^mon-kingsway-about] Dukes was the most-sampled artist on Tracklib in 2021.[^mon-tracklib-sos-2021]

Kingsway's basic license, as the software lawyer Kyle Mitchell read it in 2021, worked like this: about $40 a pack covered self-released recordings up to one million streams, and a major-label release meant going back to Kingsway to negotiate with the loop's producer, with clearance not guaranteed and a co-producer credit required.[^gfa-kemitchell-kingsway-2021]

Beat stores work on similar fine print. The Copyright Office warns that "lease," "premium" and "exclusive" are often all licenses of differing scope, that a "free" beat can carry terms, and that licenses can come with stream caps and expiry dates.[^mon-usco-2021]

### Will a distributor release a song with an uncleared sample?

DistroKid distributes covers, but its help page says "we cannot help approve the use of samples from other songs" and that stores do not allow another artist's stem or sample without permission.[^mon-distrokid-samples] The same page accepts royalty-free samples from Splice, Loopcloud and the libraries that ship with a DAW, and says a track built on them is ineligible for DistroKid's YouTube Content ID add-on.[^mon-distrokid-samples]

TuneCore files samples, interpolations, remixes, mixtapes and mashups under one rule. A release that uses a recording you did not make needs a master use license from that recording's owner, and its help page is blunt about the rest: you "cannot distribute any content through TuneCore if you do not have 100% of the rights to that content."[^gfa-tunecore-samples]

### Are DJ mixes cleared?

A DJ mix is an hour of other people's records, so the licensing sits with the platform. SoundCloud has scanned uploads with automated content identification since 2010. In December 2016 its co-founder Eric Wahlforss said new licensing deals, with GEMA "among others," meant DJ mixes would no longer be removed for copyright - a remark that reached the English-language press secondhand, with no comment from the company at the time.[^mon-fader-soundcloud-2016] One distributor's guide describes Mixcloud as licensed with major labels and publishers, identifying the tracks in each show by audio fingerprint and paying rights holders on plays.[^mon-labelgrid-mixcloud] Mixcloud's own help page says only that it is licensed and partners with "many of the largest rights holders, record labels and publishers around the world," without naming them.[^gfa-mixcloud-licensed] The booth end of that story is at https://showfile.cold.fyi/.
